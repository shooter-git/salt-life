# Sources: rods, rod holders and line colours (M21, step 0)

Gathered 2026-11-16 on `m21` (6eba66d). Research only; nothing in the project was changed.

What was checked: real sizes and looks for the game's six rod types (hand-me-down glass spinning rod, 7 ft graphite inshore rod, 9 ft surf rod in spinning and conventional form, 50 lb stand-up rod, 130 lb bent-butt chair rod, 9 ft fly rods in 8, 10 and 12 weight); the rod holders on a 23 to 26 ft centre console, a 38 ft Downeast boat and a 48 ft sportfisher; and the colours and diameters of the game's six lines. The game's current stand-up model (`src/game/rigs/BigRod.js`) was read for comparison: 2.13 m long (`ROD_L`), butt and seat to 0.424 m, fore-grip to 0.535 m, blank 27 mm across at the grip and 6.4 mm at the tip, six roller guides.

How to read the labels:
- **Opened**: I fetched the page and read its text. **Viewed**: I also looked at the photograph itself.
- **Search result only**: I saw the figure in a search summary but couldn't open the page (blocked, built by script, or a PDF I couldn't read). Treat these as weaker.
- **Derived**: worked out from a sourced figure. **Judgement**: no source gave it.
- Blank tip sizes in rod-building tables are the tip-top tube in 1/64 in. Guide sizes (Fuji and similar) are the ring's outside diameter in mm (MHX, below).

## 1. The hand-me-down rod (old glass spinning rod)

**What it is.** The Ugly Stik is the archetype. Shakespeare introduced it in 1976, made by the Howald process: a spiral glass core with lengthwise glass fibres in pigmented resin, wrapped in tape and oven-cured (Wikipedia). Wikipedia on the look: "The first Ugly Stik rods were very large, featured metal handles, the graphite color showed through the blank, and the wraps were black with white pinstripes." Later rods have a red and yellow basket-weave pattern near the handle, a shiny black finish and a clear tip. The page carries maintenance tags, so treat the details as rough.

**Today's equivalent, Ugly Stik GX2** (FishUSA and uglystik.com, opened):
- 6'6" and 7'0" spinning models, one or two pieces. The 6'6" medium (USGXSP662M) is two pieces.
- 6 guides + tip on every 5'10" to 7'0" model; 8 on the 9'0". 5 on rods of 5'0" and under.
- "Ugly Tuff" one-piece stainless guides, Clear Tip, graphite-and-glass blank, EVA grips (FishUSA) or shrink tube (uglystik.com), an "ergonomic reel seat with exposed blank".

**Blank sizes for a glass spinning rod** (Rainshadow E-Glass SPG series, Charkbait, opened):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPG783 | 1.98 m (6'6") | M | 13.8 mm (0.545 in) | 2.6 mm (6.5/64) | 82 g (2.9 oz) |
| SPG843 | 2.13 m (7'0") | M | 14.6 mm (0.575 in) | 3.2 mm (8/64) | 96 g (3.4 oz) |

## 2. The 7 ft graphite rod (modern inshore spinning rod)

**St. Croix Mojo Inshore 7'0"** (Tackle Warehouse and Black Hall Outfitters, opened):

|---|---|---|
| Line, lure | 8-17 lb, 3/8-3/4 oz | 10-20 lb, 1/2-1 1/4 oz |
| Handle (whole) | 375 mm (14¾ in) | 425 mm (16¾ in) |
| Fore-grip / rear grip | not given | 76 mm (3 in) / 229 mm (9 in) |
| Weight | not given | 181 g (6.4 oz) |
| Guides | 7 + tip | 7 + tip |

- Seat and gap between the grips on the MH: about 120 mm (Derived: 425 − 229 − 76).
- SCIII carbon blank; Sea Guide Hero guides with aluminium-oxide rings and "gun smoke" frames; Fuji DPS seat "with gun metal hoods"; EVA and cork grips; a rubber gimbal cap; two coats of Flex-Coat. Blank colour not stated.

**Blank sizes.** I found no 7'0" medium graphite spinning blank on a page I could open. Search results only: Mud Hole CRB IS661M (6'6" M) 0.455 in (11.6 mm) butt, 5.5 tip; Rainshadow RX7 inshore 7' M 0.480 in (12.2 mm) butt, 5.5/64 (2.2 mm) tip. So the graphite rod is about 2 to 3 mm thinner at the butt than the glass one.

**Guides.** Fuji KL-H "KR concept" kits for spinning rods up to 7'6" (Mud Hole, opened): #25H, #12H, #6L, then one KB #5.5 and five KT #5.5 (or the #16H and #20H versions). Fuji numbers are the ring's outside diameter in mm (MHX). So a modern rod's first ring is 16 to 25 mm and the rest drop fast to 5.5 mm. Factory rods use fewer, larger guides (7 + tip on the Mojo).

**How a conventional surf rod differs.**
- Reel on top, guides on top with it (general sources in search results; also seen in the Sport Fishing butts image, where a casting rod has a trigger).
- Casting guides are smaller than spinning guides (search results only, general articles).
- First guide position: Mud Hole, for casting rods: "Measure 19 to 21 inches from the front of the casting reel to the first stripper guide" (48 to 53 cm; guide spacing basics, opened).
- Okuma's conventional 9' keeps the spinning model's grip lengths and guide count. I found no source on surf rods' triggers. Many bass casting rods have one (search results); I saw none on Penn's surf photos. So no trigger is Judgement.
- Gap: no ring sizes for either kind of surf rod.

## 5. The bent-butt chair rod, 130 lb class

**Whole rod.**
- Penn International VI 130 lb, Aftco rollers, bent butt (INTVI130LBARBB): 7'6" (2.29 m), "Extra Heavy" (Miami Fishing Supply, opened). 229 cm, 2588 g (FishFriender, opened; Nootica gives 232 cm and 2588 g, search result only).
- Penn International V 5130B (B = bent butt): "Parabolic tapers configured for maximum leverage when used in conjunction with a quality fighting chair, coupled with AFTCO roller guides and a detachable machined aluminum butt" (Greentoe, opened). 7'8" (2.34 m) according to search results.

**Blank.** Batson RT66130, IGFA 130 lb, E-glass, one piece, black gloss, 1.68 m (5'6"), 604 g (21.3 oz), tip tube 9.5 mm (24/64) (Batson, opened). Diameters: butt 29.2 mm (1.15 in), 22.9 mm at 10 in, 20.6 mm at 20 in, 18.0 mm at 30 in. Caution: the same page's 50 and 80 lb blanks list a butt figure smaller than their 10 in figure, so these columns aren't consistent. American Tackle AXST56XXH, 60-130 lb stand-up blank: 23.4 mm (0.92 in) butt (Mud Hole, opened).

**Curved butt** (AFTCO Unibutt table, opened):

| Unibutt | Class | Length | Weight | Ferrule O.D. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UB6 straight | 130 lb | 596 mm (23.45 in) | 907 g (32 oz) | 33.9 mm (1.336 in) |
| UBC6 curved | 130 lb | 722 mm (28.44 in) | 1049 g (37 oz) | 33.9 mm |

- Overall: 1.68 m blank + 0.72 m butt ≈ 2.40 m, less the part of the blank inside the ferrule (Derived). This agrees with Penn's 2.29 to 2.34 m.

**The bend.**
- No maker I found states an angle for a fixed bent butt. ALPS sells "Low Angle (25°)" and "BUB2C (70°)" bent butts, but measures them as the rod's angle above the water in a holder, not the bend itself (Johnny Jigs, opened). Winthrop's T-10X adjusts through 10 angles from 0 to 90° (Black Hall, opened).
- Measured on AFTCO's curved Storabutt photo (Chaos Fishing, viewed): the two straight legs meet at about 30 to 35°, the bend about half way down the butt, its radius about two to three tube widths (Derived, rough; one photo, perspective unknown).
- Why it's bent: AFTCO (Peter B. Wright): "A curved butt is used solely to change the angle of the tip section of a rod". In a chair it keeps the tip down with the reel in easy reach.

**Rollers.** AFTCO's Heavy Duty set is rated for "stand-up tuna rods up to 130 lb. test" (5 + tip). The Wind-On set, (2) #22 (2) #32 (1) #47, is for extra-heavy stand-up rods (search result only). Gap: no roller count for a 130 chair rod. 5 + a large roller tip is Judgement.

**Weight on the boat.** FishTrack: "a 130 outfit weighs about 10 kilos (22 pounds)".

- Sources: Performance Fishing Electronics and Guild Marine (opened). Lee's photo (viewed): an oval polished flange, a white plastic liner, a cross pin through the bottom of the tube.
- The gimbal slot across a big-game rod's butt "locks into a rod belt with a gimbal pin or into a chair-holder socket" (Sport Fishing, opened).
- Angles (Marine Depot, opened): 0° holders stand "straight up". 15° are used for kite fishing (and "at this angle, you can also go trolling"). 30° "are best suited" for trolling. Steeper angles need more room under the gunwale. Gemlux (opened) says the opposite about 0° ("keeps rods parallel to the water"), against the usual convention, so I've followed Marine Depot.
- George Poveromo's Mako 334 centre console (opened):
  - three holders a side in the aft covering boards: 30° Lee swivel holders farthest aft for flat lines, 30° about 3 ft (0.9 m) forward for the short outrigger baits, 15° flush mounts another 3 ft forward and offset outboard for the long rigger baits;
  - zero-degree holders in the centre transom for the centre rigger, "for clearance when the outboards are trimmed up";
  - eight more a side forward, mostly 15° flush mounts "aimed seaward".
- FishTrack (opened):
  - covering-board holders: "one vertical rod holder and one 30-degree angled rod holder, which lays flatter";
  - "A rocket launcher on the aft flybridge rail or T-Top is one approach";
  - "Boats with towers usually feature rod holders welded to the tower legs";
  - chairs "will have at least two rod holders (and up to four) in the armrests";
  - "the standard heavy-tackle setup consists of two rod holders in the port and starboard gunwales, and two to four in the chair armrests, with perhaps one shotgun running off the bridge, as well as a bait rod in the transom".

**How a rod stands in a holder.**
- AFTCO's photo (viewed): straight-butt trolling rods stand in vertical covering-board holders. The butt goes all the way in; the reel seat sits just above the flange; the reels are on top, facing up.
- Commons charter-boat photo (viewed): rods in angled gunwale holders lean outboard and aft, each reel on top of its rod.
- So the butt goes in to the tube's bottom pin: about 0.23 to 0.28 m (Derived from Lee's lengths).
- A spinning rod in the same holder has its reel on the underside, toward the water (Judgement).
