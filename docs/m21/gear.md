# M21, stream GEAR: each rod and reel drawn as itself

## What's drawn now

Other players saw the spinning rod or the fly rod, whatever you held, and the rods in a boat's holders were dark tubes.

## How they're built and drawn

- **Shared parts** (`RodParts.js`): the blank, wraps, ring, snake and roller guides and their tops, the line through them, a screw seat, a spinning reel and a lever-drag reel to any size. `BigRod.js` builds the stand-up and chair rods and holds the big reels' figures; `FlyRod.js` the fly rods and reels; `GearModels.js` the light rods, the spinning reels, the conventional and the electric, and the tables.
- **One mesh a rod and its reel**, in the rod's own material, as before.
- **Built the first time it's wanted, kept by what it is** (`gearModel( rod, reel )`): your hands, every other player's copy and every holder drawing the same pair share one geometry. Its arrays are let go once it's on the GPU (`releaseGeometry`, as the load's still meshes are); its bounds are kept.
- **Nothing more before ready:** the rod in your hands is built at the load as what you start with (the hand-me-down and the old reel, 11,096 triangles against the old model's 16,352). Any other is built when it's first taken up (`setModel` waits for `equip`), a copy's when that player's pose first says it, a holder's when her spread's first out.

### The moving parts and the rod's length: per-material uniforms

Before, the rod's shader had the reel's axes and the rod's length written into its source as constants (`REEL_Z`, `BODY_Y`, `PIVOT_Y`, the braid's 20.5 mm, `BLANK_START`, `ROD_L`), and every model had to be built round them: the stand-up reel's crank was put on the spinning reel's shaft, and the fly rod was built 9 ft, squashed to 7 ft and stretched back by the hand's matrix (`FLY_K`).

**Now they're per-material uniforms**, set as a model's fitted (`fitUniforms`):
- `rodFit`: where the blank bends from, the rod's length, the least radius of the line on its spool, the bail's pivots;
- `reelAxis`: the spool's centre (y and z) and the crank's;
- `reelLine`: the line in use's colour, and whether it's braid;
- `rodShape.y`: the length the bend's a share of (the rod's own; a fly rod's M19's 7 ft, so it bends as FlyCast has it).

**Why uniforms, not the other two ways:**
- **On the shared axes** the source wouldn't change, but every spinning reel would hang at one height (a 4000's axis is 93 mm under its foot, a 5000's 100), a drum's spool couldn't turn faster than its crank, and a rod of another length would still need the fly rod's stretch, which draws a crank or a spool turning across the rod as an ellipse.
- **Per-vertex data** would need another attribute (more bytes in every model) and change the source anyway.
- Each copy and each holder rod already had a material of its own for its bend, so the figures cost nothing more to carry.

**New parts the shader moves** (`PART`): a drum's spool (6) about its own axis across the rod, and the line on it (7), its radius going down as line goes out, as a spinning reel's braid did. The line along the rod (8) is coloured, not moved.

**How they move:**
- **A spinning reel** as before: the rotor at its gear (4.7 on the old reel, 5.3 on the smooth), the bail, the crank, the spool in and out.
- **A drum** (the conventional, the big-game reels, the electric): its crank, its spool turning against it at its gear (5.3 on the conventional; 3.1, 2.5 and 2.1 on the big-game reels' high gear; 3.1 on the electric), shown at most three turns a second as the rotor is. Its spool turns back when the drag slips.
- **The electric reel's handle** stays still while its motor winds.
- **A fly reel's spool and knob** turn with your winding, one to one, and back as a fish takes line.
- **On another player's copy** (it moved nothing before): the crank and the rotor or spool turn while their line comes in, and a spinning reel's bail opens as they cast.

**The rod's material stays one program.** All its materials share one program key; `test/gear-m21.mjs` checks the hand's, a copy's and a holder's are the same. **But its source has changed:**
- three new uniforms (`rodFit`, `reelAxis`, `reelLine`);
- the constants replaced by them;
- the drum's two parts;
- the line's colour written into `vColor` and `vAux` for the line's parts.

On a GPU the rod's mesh is in the scene at the load (on a CPU it waits till after ready: `App.heldTools`), so it's one of the programs built before ready: **the ready A/B is needed**. No texture is added.

### The fishing's as it was

The tip the cast, the line out and the fight go by (`FishingRod.tip`) is where the one model's was before M21: a 7 ft rod held 0.405 m up it, bending from 0.535; M19's fly rod 9 ft held 0.3 m up. The drawn rod's own tip (`lineTip`) is where the line's drawn from.

A rod drawn longer or shorter would otherwise have moved where a cast lands. The hand-me-down's shorter rod took a full cast from 24.6 m to 24.4 m in `test/game-logic.mjs` before this was done.

`test/game-logic.mjs`, `test/tackle.mjs` and `test/fly.mjs` print the same figures as on `m21` before the stream, line for line. Nothing in Gear, the fights or the line's metres changed.

The fly rods are held 0.3 m up, as M19's was, so their drawn tip is where FlyCast draws the loop from (within 2 cm while a stroke loads the rod).

## Seen by others

**The pose** carries (`Together._mine`):
- `gr`: the rod, reel and line in your hands, while the rod's out;
- `sg`: those in your holders, with your spread out.

Each is one number, rod x 100 + reel x 10 + line (`gearCode`, `readGear`): about 9 bytes a pose. Measured with two players, the biggest pose was 278 bytes against the room's 16 KB.

**The other player's game** draws:
- their copy's rod as that model: held where it's held, its reel on top if it rides there, at its own length, the line its colour;
- their holders' rods as theirs.

**An older game** that sends neither is drawn as before:
- its fly rod by `fw`;
- otherwise the graphite rod and the smooth reel (the nearest to the one model there was), clear mono;
- its spread's rods the stand-up rod and the two-speed.

**Her kit** (`Rigs.kitOf`, `cleanKit`) carries her heavy tackle's levels (`rd`, `rl`, `ln`), so a rider who takes her rod off its holder, flies her kite or drops on her electric reel holds her rod and reel, not his own. From an older game, the stand-up rod or the surf rod with the two-speed.

## The rods in the holders

**Your spread** (`Rigs._drawLine`) and **everyone's copy of it** (`Spread.update`) stand her owner's heavy outfit in each holder (`rigs/HolderRods.js`):
- **In the tube:** the butt down it to the pin, 25 cm in (Lee's 229 to 279 mm tubes).
- **The reel:** a conventional on top, a spinning reel underneath.
- **A bent butt:** its lower leg is what's in the tube, the rod above it lying 30 degrees lower.
- **With a fish on:** bowed toward its line, its spool turning back as line goes out, the line on it going down.

**What stayed:** the line's place in the game (`L.tip`) is as it was; the line's drawn from the drawn rod's tip.

**How it's drawn:**
- **Each rod a mesh of its own** in the rod's material (its bend and spool are its uniforms): one draw a rod in view, culled by its own bounds grown by its bow, and not drawn past 150 m from the eye (a few pixels; its line still leaves where its tip is).
- **Before:** one RigMesh drew every rod with the lines, their tubes written each frame. **Now:** each frame writes a rod's matrix and four uniforms; nothing's written to its vertices.
- **Kept for the next frame,** re-fitted only when her gear changes.
