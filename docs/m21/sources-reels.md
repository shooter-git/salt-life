# Sources: the reels (M21 step 0)

Date: 2026-11-16.

Checked: real sizes, handles, weights, line capacities, colours and mounting for the eight reel types the game draws (old spinning, smooth spinning, 30-class conventional, 50-class two-speed, 80W two-speed, 130-class two-speed, electric deep-drop, and saltwater fly reels for 8, 10 and 12 weight). The game's line figures were checked against the makers' stated capacities.

## How to read this

- **Stated**: a maker's or retailer's figure, on a page I opened.
- **Search result only**: I saw the figure in a search result but could not open the page. Penn, Daiwa US, Yellow Dog, Trident and Chaos Fishing run on Shopify and refused the fetches (HTTP 429). Shimano's own sites, Mysticparts, BCF, eBay and WorthPoint refused them (HTTP 403).
- **Derived**: a number I worked out from a stated figure. There are two kinds:
  - *Spool diameter from retrieve.* Full-spool diameter = line per crank ÷ gear ratio ÷ π. I tested this on four Shimano reels whose spool diameter is stated. It gives 95.5, 121.0 and 148.5 mm for the Tiagra 50W, 80W and 130, against the stated 96, 121 and 148 mm. It gives 90.4 mm for the Beastmaster 9000, against the stated 90 mm. Shimano's "spool diameter" is therefore the diameter of a full spool, close to the flange's.
  - *Sizes from a photo.* I measured a maker's square-on product photo in pixels and scaled it by a part whose size is stated (a spool's diameter or width). Expect about ±10%.
- **Judgement**: no source gave the number. It is my estimate.
- Metric comes first, with imperial in brackets. 1 yd = 0.9144 m, 1 oz = 28.35 g.

## 1. Old spinning reel

**Models.** The Penn Spinfisher 650SS is the best match for a worn 4000–5000 saltwater spinner. It weighed 22 oz (624 g), held 250 yd of 15 lb (229 m) and geared 4.7:1. Two search results (Berinson Tackle and a Pier & Surf thread) gave these figures; neither page would open. The thread says the 650SS was dropped in 1996.

The 4400SS is a smaller reel: 12 oz (340 g), 5.1:1, 200 yd of 10 lb (183 m), made 1992–2005. A parts reference gave these figures in a search result, again unopened. It is nearer a modern 2500–3000.

The Mitchell 300 dates from 1948 and comes from Cluses, France (Wikipedia, opened). It is the archetype of the open-face look. Judgement: it is smaller than a 4000, so take the look from it, not the size.

**Spool.** The later all-metal 650SSm gears 4.7:1 and takes in 86 cm per crank (BCF, search result only). That gives a full spool of about **58 mm (2.3 in)** Derived. Stroke: not found, a gap. Judgement: about 20 mm.

**Overall size.** No source gave one. Derived: I scaled the Stradic 4000 photo measurements (section 2) by 58/52 = 1.12. That gives about 100 mm from the foot top to the spool axis, about 130 mm from the foot to the bottom of the rotor, and about 145–150 mm long. Judgement: older reels have a longer stem and a rounder, plainer body, so lean towards these upper figures.

**Handle.** Not found. Judgement: a crank of about 60–65 mm with a round or T knob.

**Drag and bail.** The SS series is front drag, with the knob on the spool face. eBay listings of the 6500SS say "Front Drag" (search result only). A parts page for the series (search result only) describes the 9500SS–6500SS housings as "anodized die-cast aluminum with a baked enamel finish" and the bails as stainless steel with an external trip.

**Colour.** Several 650SS and 6500SS listings say "black and gold" (eBay, search result only). Recommendation: a black body, a gold anodised skirted spool and a chrome bail. Wear the paint through to grey metal on the edges, the foot and the rotor.

**Line.** The game's 180 m equals the 4400SS's stated 183 m. A 650SS holds 229 m, so 180 m reads as a reel not quite full.

**Mount.** Under the rod. The spinning reel is "normally mounted below the rod" (Wikipedia, opened).

**Photographs.** I found no clear free picture of a vintage Penn SS.
- [Two spinning fishing reels on pier, Deerfield Beach FL](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Two_spinning_fishing_reels_on_pier.JPG): public domain. Seen: gold-and-black saltwater spinners on pier rods.
- The Berinson Tackle 650SS listing would not open.

Penn's own Fathom II page shows "Max Drag: 33lb | 14.9kg" (search result only). TackleDirect's Fathom II 30LD page (opened) lists a "One piece frame and palm sideplate", 5 bearings and "Removeable Top Spacer Bar and Lugs (60 an 80 sizes only)". So a 30 has **no harness lugs**.

**Spool.** Derived full-spool diameters: Fathom 30LD 54.7 mm (2.15 in); Squall 30 49 mm; Jigmaster 500 51 mm. Spool width was not found. Judgement: about 45–50 mm between the flanges.

**Overall size**, Derived. I measured TackleDirect's front photo of the Fathom II 30LD (seen). I had to fix the scale by Judgement: side plate about 75 mm, which is 1.3–1.4 × the spool, as on the Tiagra. That gives:
- frame about 90 mm wide (3.5 in) between the side plates' outer faces;
- about 185 mm (7.3 in) with the handle;
- about 105 mm (4.1 in) tall from the lever top to the bottom of the gear housing;
- about 83 mm from the top of the left plate to the foot.

Treat these as ±15%.

**Handle.** About 80 mm crank (Derived, rough). The knob is a black oval paddle, about 50 × 33 mm (Derived, rough). The drag lever is black and sits at the top rear of the handle-side plate; Judgement: about 50 mm long. A star drag (the Jigmaster) has its star between the handle and the side plate. Wikipedia (opened) describes it: "the adjustor wheel looks like a star with rounded points". Judgement: the star is about 40–45 mm across.

**Colour.** The Fathom II is silver anodised, with a black lever, knob and trim (photos, seen). The old star-drag look is black side plates and chrome pillars with a black T-bar knob ([Fishing Reel.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fishing_Reel.jpg), seen).

**Mount.** On top of the rod, foot in the reel seat. The handle is on the right; left-hand versions exist. On a 9 ft surf rod a 30-class lever drag is heavy for casting. Judgement: surf anglers more often cast a 15–25 size, but the 30 is fine for a rod that mostly soaks bait.

**Line.** The game's 350 m fits: 370 m of 25 lb or 302 m of 30 lb.

## 5. Wide 80-class two-speed reel

**Stated figures:**
- **Shimano Tiagra 80WA** (reelcatalog and Shimano 2020 catalogue, both opened):
  - spool Ø121 × 97 mm (4.8 × 3.8 in); handle 80 mm;
  - 3265 g (115.2 oz);
  - 2.5 / 1.3:1; 95 cm per crank;
  - 80 lb / 950 yd = **869 m**.
  - Drag: 18 kg (40 lb) on reelcatalog; TackleDirect's Hooker pages give 35 lb at strike and 44 lb at full (search result only).
- **Penn International 80VSW** (2014 catalogue, opened): 111 oz (3147 g); 950 yd of 80 lb (869 m); drag 40 lb at strike and 51 lb at full; 3.0 / 1.4:1; 43 / 19 in per crank. Derived spool 116 mm.
- **Penn International 80VISW** (reelcatalog and TackleDirect, opened): 113 oz (3205 g); 1120 yd of 80 lb (1024 m); 65 lb drag; 3.1 / 1.5:1. Gold, or silver as the 80VISWS.

The game's 868 m equals the 950 yd of 80 lb stated for both the Tiagra 80WA and the Penn 80VSW.

**Overall size**, Derived. I measured TackleDirect's square-on side and end photos of the TI80WA (seen). I scaled them by the 97 mm spool width, seen through the frame's windows; Avet's 80-class gives a cross-check at "Reel diameter: 5-1/2 in" (TackleDirect, opened).
- Side plates: about **145–150 mm Ø (5.7–5.9 in)**. They are nearly round discs. The left plate is plain gold; the right plate carries a chrome gear plate.
- Height from the top of the lugs to the bottom of the foot: about 170–177 mm (6.7–7.0 in).
- Frame width over both side plates: about 185 mm (7.3 in). About 315 mm (12.4 in) with the handle.
- Two **harness lugs**, one on top of each side plate. Each is a flat triangular plate with a ring hole, standing about 20 mm proud. They are about 128 mm apart and roughly 39 mm wide at the base.
- Lee's hanger listings say the hanger's "Pins snap into harness lugs on top of reel" (search result only).

**Handle and lever.** An 80 mm crank (stated). The knob is a large black rubber "power handle", about 60–75 mm long and 40–45 mm across (Derived, rough).

The drag lever is chrome with a knurled tip and sits on the handle-side plate. Its tip rides just above the frame top at the rear. Derived, rough: it is about 85 mm from its pivot, and the knurled tip is about 12 × 20 mm. The two-speed button is a black "PUSH HIGH" button on the handle hub (photo, seen).

**Foot and clamp.** The foot is a bronze-toned plate with upturned ends under the frame (photos, seen). Its length is not stated; Judgement: about 85–90 mm.

Shimano's Tiagra 80W rod clamp (part ASET0080, Marine Deals, opened) "secures your reel in place and takes the pressure off your reel foot". It "features an eye for attaching a rod leash".

**Colour.** Gold anodised frame and side plates, a chrome gear plate and screws, a black knob (photos, seen). The Penn is gold or silver.

**Handle side.** Wikipedia (opened): "Most modern fly reels are ambidextrous, allowing the angler to place the crank handle of the reel on either the right or the left side as desired." Hatch 7 Plus: "available as left- or right-handed retrieve" (opened). Tibor reels are sold by side, for example "Left Hand Retrieve" (Yellow Dog titles, search result only).

Search results only: a right-handed caster usually reels with the left hand, but "many saltwater anglers cast and retrieve with the same hand" for power on big fish. Judgement: give the game's owner left-hand wind by default, and make it switchable.

**How it hangs.** The reel sits below the rod at the very butt. Its foot is on top of the frame and slides into an up-locking seat. The spool axis is across the rod, and the handle faces left or right. The Tibor photo (The Fly Fishers, seen) shows the reel foot-up in this attitude, with the knob on the spool's face.

Judgement:
- 10–12 wt rods have a short fighting butt below the reel, about 40–50 mm.
- The foot is about 55–60 mm long and 15 mm wide; not found, a gap.
- The knob is a cylinder about 25–30 mm long and 15–18 mm across. Nautilus calls theirs a "Big game handle" (opened).
