# How Salt Life is put together

Salt Life is [Tidewater](https://github.com/dgreenheck/tidewater) rebuilt as an All the Spaces creation.

- **What carried over:** its game logic, physics and procedural geometry, ported with little change.
- **What was rewritten:** its WebGPU drawing (WGSL materials, compute, storage buffers, GPU readbacks), for three.js and WebGL 2.
- **What was left out:** the systems that only made sense on a strong graphics card. These are the ocean FFT, the wake and shore simulations, GTAO, TAA, motion blur and the lens effects. Tidewater's history in this repository keeps them.

## Start-up

`src/main.js` connects to All the Spaces (`connect()` from `@allthespaces/portal`), then hands the portal to `App`, which it loads alongside (`import()`: most of the download). On its own (`pnpm dev`, GitHub Pages) there's no veil from the app, so `src/ui/LoadingVeil.js` puts one up at once, follows the same stages and lifts at the start; it also picks the device's tier, as All the Spaces would:

1. `App.init(progress)` builds the island behind the loading veil, reporting each stage (`portal.progress`, and a `performance.mark('load: …')` for profiles).
2. `portal.ready()`, then `App.start()`.
3. After the start, `App` brings in the things a person doesn't need at once, one after another:
   - the plants, where the device draws on its CPU;
   - the shaders for later (`prepareLate`);
   - Joe and Marta;
   - the beach's debris;
   - the reef and its fish;
   - the whale;
   - the air (motes, sun shafts).

   (M16) Only the boat you start in is built before then. `FleetDesk` makes the boats you own and aren't in on its first update after the start ([Your fleet](#your-fleet)), and the others' boats are built when someone is within sight of one ([Each boat, for the others](#each-boat-for-the-others)).

## A frame

`App.frame(dt)` runs, in order:
1. The island's clock (`settings.timeOfDay` and `settings.day`, advanced by `IslandClock.advance`, or worked out from the session's clock: see [The island's day](#the-islands-day)).
2. The weather (`WeatherDriver`): the wind, the sea, the surf, the cloud and the light, from the clock (see [The weather](#the-weather)).
3. Input.
4. The boat's physics (`BoatController`).
5. The player (`Player`: walking, swimming, on deck or at the helm), then the fishing game (`Game`).
6. The sky.
7. The CPU water queries (`WaterQuery`: Gerstner waves plus the shore waves, and (M17) the boats' wakes' far arms, followed from their tracks just before: `Wake.track`). They give heights for the boat, the swimmer, the bobber and the birds.
8. Everything else (plants, reef, fish, wildlife, spray, sound).
9. The drawing, through the SDK's `createStage`. On high it also goes through `PostChain`.

## Drawn about where you are

(M15) The game keeps its positions in the world's frame, as JavaScript's doubles: fine to well under a micrometre at any distance it goes. The GPU's are 32-bit floats, whose step is 0.5 mm at 5 km and 4 mm at 40 km, so anything drawn from a world position on the GPU shakes out there (see [M15.md](M15.md), The far sea, measured). three places its objects from the camera in doubles (`modelViewMatrix`), so they're steady; what isn't three's own is drawn about a point near it:

- **The sea** (`WaterSurface`): its grid follows the camera 512 m a step (`CDLOD`'s `follow`), each level's offset and parity worked out in doubles so a vertex lands on the world's own lattice point and morphs its way; the mesh is put at that origin. What's keyed on where the water is takes the origin's share in doubles: the ripples' textures (their offsets, fractions of their repeat), the rain's rings and the foam's noises (whole cells and a fraction: `waterKey`, `foamKeys`; an integer hash, `waterHash`), the whitecaps' crests and cells (`Whitecaps.place`). The whitecaps' marbling turns about the camera as the wind turns, not about the world's middle.
- **The ground** (`Terrain`): its grid follows the camera as the sea's does (512 m a step, two root nodes of 2 km each way), drawn about its origin; the island's ground and the seabed out there alike. See [The seabed out there](#the-seabed-out-there).
- **The sun's shadows** (`materials/ViewShadows.js`): looked up from view space; the light's matrices (mid's, high's cascades) times the camera's world matrix, in doubles, for each render's camera; three's `shadowmap_vertex` patched on import.
- **The fishing line and the anchor's rode** (`createLineMaterial`): its ends about a point by its first end, the view's matrix to that point given for each render (`onBeforeRender`); the uniforms the game sets stay the world's.
- **Particles and cloth** (the cast net, the spray, the wake, the drips): their positions in doubles (`Float64Array`), drawn about a point by the camera or by them, to a metre, the mesh put there; the shaders use `modelViewMatrix`, and the camera less that point. The boat's lamps are drawn by their own matrices; the water's queries (`WaterQuery`, the boat's) are doubles.
- The birds too, since step 7 (their instance records about an origin near the camera: What's found out there); a hurricane's spume since step 6 (The weather where you are). Not the whale's marks, keyed on world positions: it stays in the bay. The rain and the air motes wrap a box about the camera from its world position; measured, their few mm don't show.

New code that draws from world positions on the GPU takes the same care: a point near it, in doubles.

## The seabed out there

(M15) Past the island's 2 km square (its 1 m map) there's a seabed of its own: the shelf, the drop-off, the ledge, the canyon, the hump and the bank, laid out in [M15.md](M15.md), The seabed out there, built.

- **Made** by `terrain/SeabedModel.js` (`SEABED` places the features; `SeabedModel.depthAt`). Next to the square it's the island's own seabed formula (`TerrainData._base`), so the two meet. It's baked by `pnpm bake` (`tools/bake-terrain.mjs`, with the island; `--check` in `pnpm test`) into `public/world/offshore.bin`, 32 m over 32 km. Low-powered devices get `offshore-512.bin`, each texel the mean of four. Its heights are packed as the island's are: u16 steps of about 3 cm, row deltas, zlib. Within 1.4 km of the square each texel is the mean of 16 samples, as the island's seabed has detail finer than a texel.
- **Read** by `terrain/Offshore.js` (`decodeSeabed`, `Offshore`: `heightAt`, bilinear, the deep's floor at 1,800 m past the map; `boundsFor`, a min/max pyramid), fetched with the island at start-up.
- **Joined to the island** by `new TerrainData( seed, island, { offshore } )`. Its `heightAt` reads it past the square and its `boundsFor` includes it. The bake's last 120 m of the square, faded to a flat 90 m, are blended into the seabed instead (`_blendEdge`: the same smoothstep, worked back out of the bake, averaged over the 1 m texels in a 2 m one). Without `offshore`, as in the older tests, it's the flat 90 m as before.
- **On the GPU**, `Terrain`'s `heightGlsl` reads it past the square (`offshoreHeightAt`, `offshoreHeightMap` and `offshoreHeightInfo` in `heightUniforms`: every material that reads the ground's heights needs them). The ground's grid follows the camera. Nodes wholly deeper than 40 m (`HIDE`: never seen) aren't drawn (`CDLOD`'s `below`). Past the island's baked maps the surface is the seabed's: its normal from its heights (worked out per vertex), bare rock where it's steep, sand, with seagrass in patches on the shallow flats and rubble in others. It's blended with the island's over the square's last 48 m. Its detail is still keyed on world positions: a float's step is 1 mm at the bank, 10 km out, a hundredth of the finest ripple's length, and wherever it's 4 mm (40 km) the seabed's too deep to be drawn.
- **The shore's breakers** are the island's. Their fields are solved over the blended edge: off the north it's about 20 m deep now, not falling away to 90. So the north sea's waves arrive about 3.5 s later all along the north beach, a shift of phase with nothing else changed; the bay's are as they were. Its fields' exposure fades out over their last 100 m (`EDGE` in `ShoreWaves`, on the GPU and the CPU), so no shallows out there are given the island's surf.
- **The sounder** loses the bottom past `SOUNDER_REACH` (1,200 ft): "-- ft", no bottom drawn, the fish still.
- `tools/seabed-chart.mjs` draws a chart of it all (shaded relief, depth colours and contours) into `qa-output/seabed/`. `test/seabed.mjs` tests it.

## The west's mangroves

(M15) `terrain/Mangrove.js` lays them out (`MANGROVE`: the shore they're along, the stand's width, its floor, the creek's line, width and bed, the pool) and `mangroveAt( x, z )` says, anywhere, how much of the stand, the creek and the fringe it is, and the floor's and the bed's heights there. Four things read it:
- **The ground**: `TerrainData._mangrove` raises the bight's flat to the stand's floor (never the land above the waterline) and cuts the creek; `_mangroveMasks` makes it mud; loose rock sites out on it are dropped. It's baked (`tools/bake-terrain.mjs` lists the file among the island's sources).
- **The trees**: `Scatter` places them last, on their own random numbers (nothing else on the island moves), as canopy trees (`out.trees`: `vegTreeSpecies` 2, scaled from `PlantGeometry.MANGROVE_TREE`, their trunks' feet `lift` over the water) with their prop roots (`out.mangroves`), drawn by `Vegetation`'s `mangroveRoots` kind in their own material (`VegMaterials.rootMaterialDef`: the canopy's bark, without its cross-fade into the impostors, out to `rootFade`).
- **The shore's waves**: `computeShoreField` scales each cell's exposure by the stand, so the shore waves die in it, every field alike (the south's, the west's, the north's).
- **The bites**: `Game.habitatAtPoint` passes the creek and the fringe as `creek` to `Bites.habitatAt`.

## The places a fish is caught

(M15) `game/Places.js`: `PLACES` (ids and names) and `placeOf( habitat, x, z )`, from `Bites.habitatAt`'s water with the game's `side` and `big` (`Game.placeAt( x, z )` works it out for a spot). Each catch carries it:
- `GameState.addFish( …, { place } )`, `addHaul( fish, hour, place, day )`: the entry's `place`; `logPlace` keeps `places` (saved: `{ n, day, first }` a place) and `lastCatch.firstAt` when it's the first there.
- `Orders.ordersFor` adds one ask a day for a place's fish (`PLACE_ASKS`, drawn last so the rest of the list is as it was; never the same kind as the others): `{ place, mul: PLACE_MUL }`, or Marta's tag-and-release `{ place, each, tag }`. `ordersOf( list, who, species, place )` is what a fish fills, its place's first (`GameState.quote`, `wantedIds`); `tagFor` and `GameState.tag` pay a tagged one as it's let go (`Game`, at the rod's catch).
- The north ledge is only the north's: `placeOf` keeps `ledge` to its bearings from `SEABED.centre`; flat bottom as deep elsewhere (the canyon's floor) is the shelf. Its water's still `ledge` to the bites.
- `Together.caught( …, place )` keeps the place's heaviest under `'@' + place` in the shared `records` (with `s`, the kind); `_setRecords` keeps them; the cooler's Records tab lists them.
- **(step 16) Found:** `FAR_PLACES` (those with `far`, each with its `what`) are noted the first time you're there (`Game.updateFound`, a second at a time, from `FOUND_FROM` of the world's middle; `GameState.findPlace`: `found`, place -> day, saved; a fish there finds it too, and an older save's fished ones). The log's "Far places you've found" lists those not fished yet, and how many more there are; the Records tab names a far place only once it's found (`farName`: "Somewhere far out" till then).

## The life list and the slams

(M17, [m17/life-list.md](m17/life-list.md)) The cooler's Log tab is a life list: every kind in `FISH`, caught or still to catch (`game/LifeList.js`, pure).
- **Counted:** `KINDS` (39), `caught( state, k )` (in the log, or a billfish trophy: a protected kind let go is logged as it's let go), `lifeCount`, `lifeList` (each caught kind's count, best, and first and best `{ day, place }`; each kind left with its `hint`), `whenWhere` ('day 12, the drop-off').
- **The log entry** (`GameState.log[ k ]`): `{ count, bestKg, bestCm, bestDay, bestAt, first: [ day, place ] }`, written by `logFish( species, kg, cm, day, place )` (from `addFish` and `addHaul`; the day is the one the places' list keeps, `Game.tradeDay`), read back by `LifeList.cleanLog`. A save from before has no days or places: blank until a kind's next best, its first unknown.
- **The tab** (`GameHUD.renderInventory`, 'log'): the count over a bar and a button down to the kinds still to catch (`[data-left]`); the slams had and still to have, with today's tally (`slamRows`, `slamToday`); the billfish let go; the kinds caught, in the table's order, with their first and best; the kinds still to catch, dimmed (`leftItem`, `.gm-item.is-left`), each with Joe's line; the places, as before.
- **Joe's line for every kind:** `FishTable.HINTS` (below the table, so its rows stay as they are; the trout's and the swordfish's in their rows), each `hint`. `Orders.biteHint` reads it first, so it's Joe's slate's line too: the slate's kinds keep to 40 characters.
- **The slams** (`Trophies.SLAMS`): `grand` (stream 1's), `superGrand` (the grand slam and a snook; `over: 'grand'`, so `slamTold` tells only it when both come at once) and `billfish` (a sailfish, a white marlin and a blue marlin), each `{ name, short, kinds, what }`. `FlatsFishing.caught( info )` returns the ids `slamCatch` completed; `Game.updateFight` puts them on the catch (`info.slams`), the catch card makes the slam its badge and its line (`slamNote( ids, bold )`, `.gm-catch-note.is-slam`), and `Together.lifeChanged( ids )` tells the group. `FlatsFishing.update` watches the clock for a sleep (`slamSlept`) before it asks for the flats' fish. A guest's fish is landed on the guest's own game, so its slam is theirs.
- **The group's board:** each player's `{ who, k, sl }` (`LifeList.lifeOf`) under `'#'` and their id in the shared `records`: below.

## The lighthouse

(M15) `world/Lighthouse.js` (`LIGHTHOUSE`: where, its parts' sizes, its light's character). Built by App after the village and before the ground's drawn: it levels its pad (`TerrainData.flatten`, as the village's), its footprint joins the village's (the rocks and the plants keep off it), its post a collider. One merged mesh of vertex colours (its tower's bands, plinth, gallery, railing, lantern frames, dome), its panes, its lens (lit by `G.night`), its beams (two additive cones turning with the lens, soft at their sides, faint), and its glare (`world/Glare.js`): `update( camera, visibility, heightPx )` gives it how bright `flashAt` says (the panels' beams turning past you), and `Glare.place` puts it along its line of sight no further than 11 km (the far plane's 12), no smaller than a few pixels, as much of it as `seen` lets through (`horizonOf`, the earth's curve; the air, `exp( -3 d / range )` with the Sky's visibility). `lightsOn()` (from `G.night`) is when the island's lights are lit.

The bay's harbour lights (`world/HarbourLights.js`, `HARBOUR_LIGHTS`: where, their colours, daymarks and characters, `lightAt( t, char )`) are built and updated the same way, their footprints and colliders too; a glare each, its colour boosted (`gain`) for a coloured light's single channel.

## The wreck

(M15) `world/wreck/WreckPlan.js` (pure): `WRECK` its measures, `SECTIONS` each piece's span and lie, `toWorld( piece, x, y, z )` from the ship's frame, `inWreck( x, z, pad )`, `wreckSpots()` for the fish. `world/Wreck.js`: `wreckGeometries()` builds each piece in the ship's frame (a `Builder` of shared-vertex grids, plates, boxes and tubes; each vertex's `aMeta`: what it is, hull, deck, plate or solid, and a value for its holes) and takes it to the world; `Wreck` draws them in one material whose `onBeforeCompile` cuts the holes (discard), the relief (a bump from `wkRel`), the rust and the growth (in the plates' metres above the sea, the world's below it), and holds the environment map at `FILL` x the scene's. ## The sea caves

(M15) `world/terrain/SeaCaves.js` (pure): `CAVES` (each one's waterline point, its way out to sea, the inlet's length, the cave's depth, its mouth and back), `tunnelAt( cave, fz )` the inlet or cave at a distance in, `carveAt( x, z )` the floor; `TerrainData._seaCaves` cuts it into the ground as a bake's loaded (at the bake's own texels), keeping what it cut (`_uncut`): `TerrainData.uncut` is the ground as it was (`heightAt`, `normalAt`; the rest as the terrain), which `Rocks._place` and the plants' `VegSite` place from (each from one random stream: the cut, baked in, had moved every rock placed after it); Rocks takes its rocks off a cave's strip and cut floor after (`_crowds`). `Overhangs.js`'s kind `seacave` (`SEA_CAVE_SITES`, a coarser grid: `cellK`) is the rock: the slope's own surface over the cut (from the ground either side of it), less the inlet and the cave; `clearHalf` keeps the plants off the strip over it; `overhangColliders` gives it walls to 3.5 m up only; `caveRoof`/`caveRoofAt` its top, which `Rocks.roofAt` gives `Player.groundAt`. `world/terrain/CaveLight.js`: `caveLightGrid` (a 1 m grid of the air in it, 24 rays each: the sky, the sunlit sea, the sunlit rock (a hit open to the sky over it), kept as L1 harmonics, 12 terms a point; from inside the cave the view out through the mouth from 15 points over it, its rays out of it not counted; built once with its coarse rock, its fine one takes it), the rock's `sea` attribute (vec4: what each vertex's face sees of each, and 1 for a cave's; the other rocks' padded open), `caveOpenness` (the light there for the eye), `caveWaterLight` and `caveLightTexture` (a map over each cave's plan: the sky straight up and the sea's glow at the sea's height, and where its floor's cut), `SEA_CAVE_GLSL`'s `seaCaveLightAt( xz )`, read by the terrain's (its light, and cobbles on the cut floor) and the water's shaders (`seaCaveUniforms`, set by Rocks), and `SEA_CAVE_CALM_GLSL`/`seaCaveCalm` (the water's vertex shader and `WaterQuery.heightAt` damp the waves there). The rock material (Rocks) shades a cave's vertices (`vSea.w`): the land's rock up top (without the flows' bands), coralline at the waterline, damp inside; the sky's light by `vSea.x` (its sheltered faces x `CAVE_FILL`), the sun only where it gets in, the sun thrown in off the sea and the rock (`CAVE_BOUNCE`), and the sea's caustics on its walls by the mouth. App raises the exposure in a cave (`caveAdapt`, from `Rocks.caveLevel` where you are and 2 to 9 m on where you're looking, up to 12 x).

## The island from far off

(M15) `world/FarIsland.js`: the island's ground every 16 m in one mesh in world coordinates (vertex colours from the terrain's masks and slope), 1.5 m under the real ground with a polygon offset, drawn past 1.5 km from its middle (`update( camera )`: its matrix pulls it in toward you past 10.5 km and shrinks it by as much). Its vertex shader takes the attribute as its true place, set down by the earth's curve (`curveDrop( d, eye )`, refraction 0.13), and gives the fog chunks that place (`vFogWorld`, `vFogDepth`); its fragment shader discards what's under the sea's horizon and hazes toward a greyer fog colour. `materials/HeightHaze.js`' distance fog fades out from 4 to 30 m over the sea while you're out of the water. App builds it after the turtles and updates it each frame.

(M15) `game/Beachcomb.js` (pure): `FINDS` (each kind's name, what it is, price, buyer, how common), `BEACHES` (each beach's stretch along x, its line's height, how many a day), `findsOf( day, { north, storm } )` (a day's finds from a hash of the day: kind, along the beach, across its line, a seed), `lying( day, days )` (the last `LIES` days' finds; App's `beachDays( d )` gives each day's north sea and hurricane wrack from the weather's own reckoning), `messageOf( seed )`. `GameState`: `finds` (what you've picked up), `picked` (their ids, pruned to those still lying), `pickFind`, `quoteFinds( who )`, `sellFinds( who, ids )`, `dropFind`, saved. `Game.updateBeachcomb` sets what's on the sand when the day or what you've picked changes and gives the pick-up prompt; GameHUD's Finds tab, the bottle's note and the traders' blocks (`findsBlock`). `world/Beachcombing.js`: each beach's high-water line (`lineAt`: walked up from the water, `_walkUp`; carried across where it isn't reached), the finds' meshes (the debris shapes', and its own sea beans, glass float in its own clear material and ambergris), and the north beach's wrack line in twelve stretches, each built when you're within reach of it (one a frame in `update( camera )`), its weed one mesh a stretch (`Weed`: the clumps, their leaves and stems and bladders, and the scraps, vertex-coloured in the village's hard material). ## The moon

  - The front between them is over the shelf's edge, at about 110 m of depth, wandering ±30 m of it as a noise 1.7 km across, sharp over ±12 m.
  - Along it, over most of its length, a slick: within 14 m of the line, fading out 25 to 60 m off it (its edge ragged), the line's distance worked out as its depth off the front's over the slope there (the slope read from the ground's heights). The sea's detail there is glassy (`seaD`: slick up, ripples down, the windrows' foam swept off), its normal flattened toward a mirror's (`flat`), its roughness cut, and no whitecaps on it. A ragged lace of foam runs down its middle (`foam`: in clumps and gaps, wider and narrower, wandering off the line). It's worked out before the whitecaps, which it sweeps off.
  - The bottom's taken for sand past where the seabed's drawn through the water (4.5 m). Its seagrass and rubble seen deeper would need the seabed's map in the sea's shader, which is at 16 textures on high, many GPUs' limit (the caves' light was moved to its vertex stage to fit: `tools/gpu-limits.mjs`).
- **The wind's sea, as grown as its fetch lets it** (`Waves`: `LEE`, `setWind`, `leeAt`; on the GPU `waveLeeOf`). Each wind wave needs a fetch to be grown, JONSWAP's (`fetchFor`): in the trades (7 m/s) 2.8 km for the 4 m chop, 36 km for the 23 m wave, less in a stronger wind. The island, as a disc, blocks part of each wave's spread of directions (±40° for the wind sea), and the blocked share is grown only over the open water between (`grownOver`).
  - So in its lee (the bay, in the trades) there's hardly any wind sea. Downwind it fills in from either side as it goes: half at 1.6 km, 89% at 6 km. Upwind and abeam it's the open ocean's.
  - The swell (±18°) is shadowed behind the island, to 15% of it 1.2 km off the north shore for the south's, filling in further off.
  - CPU and GPU alike (`heightAt` takes each wave's share at the point), so what floats sits on what's drawn. The whitecaps go with the wind sea's grown share (`windGrownAt`, `waveWindGrown`): none in the bay, as many as ever in the open.
- **The bank's breakers** (`ShoreWaves`' fourth train: `setBank`, `setBankSea`; on the GPU `shoreTex4`, `shoreInfo4`, `shoreFieldSampleIn`). The island's own shore-wave model on the bank, its field solved over a square of its own round it (7.2 km, 14 m cells, 28 on low; a field's square can now sit off the world's diagonal: `originZ`). It's solved once you're within 9 km of it, in a worker (`App.bankSea`, 0.2 s on a laptop), for whichever sea's the bigger out there by the power it carries (H² T): the south's swell or the trades' sea from the north, solved again when the other takes over by a third.
  - A fair day's swell breaks on the sandbar's crest. A bigger one builds as it comes onto the bar and breaks across it from 2 m of water. Its rim breaks in a bigger sea still. The bank's middle never does, and its waves add nothing at the island.
  - The boat feels them (the CPU's trains), and its surf zone is milky as the island's is.

## The sound out there

(M15) `audio/OpenSea.js` (pure; `test/sound-out-there.mjs`), `SoundScape`. App works out what's heard from the sea four times a second (`updateAudio`: `state.sea`):

- **`SurfLine( terrain, { bank } )`**: the island's waterline as stretches (`points`: where, seaward, 32 m each; read from `heightAt` on a 16 m lattice) and the bank's shallows after them (under 9 m, on a 40 m lattice). `heard( x, z, trains, out, gamma )` adds them as power, each by the surf reaching it (a train's `amplitude` x its field's exposure 40 m offshore of it, kept per field; the bank's only where its depth's under where that sea breaks), over distance squared, and if it faces you: `g` (the far law's amplitude), `surf` (the height of what's heard), `dx`, `dz` (where it's from), `land` (the island's waterline's nearest: use this, not `coastDistance`, far out), `near` (the nearest of what's breaking). `KG` matches it to the near law at 800 m off the bay.
- **`surf_far`**: `1.25 / ( 1 + d / 200 )` by the shore as it always was, `g` by `FAR_SURF` (300 to 800 m off the land; on the water only), through `surfLP` (`roarCut( near )`), `roarCarry` for the wind, placed by `surfX`, `surfZ`; out of its voice under `ROAR_GONE`.
- **`sea_open`**: `MIX.seaOpen x seaGain( wind, grown ) x openWater( land )`, `grown` being `Waves.windGrownAt`. `wind` and `wind_sea` crossfade as power by the same `open`, which also steadies the gusts.
- **`_whitecaps`**: `Whitecaps.rollers` five times a second within `CAP_RANGE`, each new id once (`rollers`' seventh argument: crest, cell and epoch; a cell breaks again a few periods on).
- **`_barStations`** (`addShore( ..., 3 )`, `src.bar`; App's `surfHeard( 3 )` each time the bank's field is given): crashes only. `_field` reads a field's own square (`field.origin`, `originZ`, `size`).
- **`big`** (`Waves.reachOf` with the wind sea as grown there, against `reachOf( 1, 1 )`) sizes the hull's slaps and lapping; **`bottom`** gates the reef's bed (`REEF_BOTTOM`); **`Birds.onSplash`** calls `birdSplash`, and `_working` counts the terns working near you for how often they call.

The recordings were chosen by ear (`tools/audio/build-sea.mjs`). They load on first use, nothing at the start.

## Playing together

`src/together/Together.js` handles everyone else on the island. It works through `portal.session`, the SDK's live session for the people in the space.

- **Who's here:**
  - Each player sends a pose message (`'p'`) about ten times a second, only while anyone else is here. It carries:
    - where they are and their heading;
    - walking, swimming, on deck or at the helm;
    - their speed;
    - the rod's state, their bobber and the line's tension;
    - their boat's pose, wherever she is (moored too: without it, someone whose own boat was out saw an empty berth);

  - Others are drawn 0.15 s behind the newest pose and eased between poses.
  - Each player is one of four Rocketbox islanders (`Avatar.js`): two men and two women. Everyone picks theirs on the cooler's Islander tab; it's saved with their progress and sent in the pose (`l`). Until someone picks, the same person always gets the same one. Each has their name over them in their colour and a dot on the minimap.

(M16) That was the lobster boat's alone: each boat is a copy of her own type now, and at the dock theirs lies at a spare berth ([Each boat, for the others](#each-boat-for-the-others)).

## The island's day

`src/game/IslandClock.js` is the clock's arithmetic, with no three.js, so `test/island-day.mjs` runs it under Node.

- **Pace:** by day an hour takes 100 s (`DAY_RATE`, 0.01 game hours a second). Deep night, from about 20:30 to 3:30, runs four times faster (`NIGHT_X`), eased in and out, so dusk and dawn linger and the dark passes in a few minutes. A whole day is about half an hour.
- **One record:** the time is worked out from `{ at, hour, day, speed }`, the hour and day at `at` (ms since 1970) running at `speed` (0: held). A table of the day's pace turns real seconds into hours, so every device gets the same hour from the same record without anything ticking over the network.
- **(M17) The tide and the moon** (`tideAt( T )`, `tideRate`, `tideTurns( T0, T1 )`, `tideState( T )`, `moonAt( T )`, from the island's time T as the weather's are): Settlement Point's tide (NOAA 9710441, Grand Bahama, the one station in the Bahamas with harmonics of its own), twelve of its constituents (`TIDE`: all but the seasons' SA and SSA, at their constants' own size), each turning at its own speed from the moon's, the sun's and the perigee's mean longitudes, as a tide table's worked out. The island's day 1 is 15 January 2027 by the sky (`TIDE_EPOCH`; its hours local mean time at 79 W, `TIDE_LON`): a first-quarter moon the first evening, high water at 12:33, the tide near its mean and rising at a new game's 9:30. Its mean range 0.83 m, springs 0.96 and neaps 0.69 (NOAA's 2026: 0.97, 0.64), springs at perigee 1.12 and apogee 0.79, a day after new and full moon; +0.72 and -0.62 m its extremes in twenty years (`TIDE_HIGHEST`, `TIDE_LOWEST`). The moon: its phase (0 new, 0.5 full), age, how much is lit and its distance. All of it + - x / alone (`cosTurns`, a series in place of `Math.cos`), so every device has the same tide to the bit, as the weather is. `test/tide.mjs` sets it beside NOAA's own predicted highs and lows for November 2026 (within 7 min, 7 cm but for the seasons' mean level).
- **Saved:** the hour, the day and whether you'd held it are in your save (`'clock'`, written every 30 s, when the app covers the game and when you leave). (M15) It goes on while you're away, at the real world's pace (`at`, `Away.awayClock`: an island hour a real hour), unless you'd held it: see [Picking up where you left off](#picking-up-where-you-left-off). A new player starts at 9:30 on day 1. On its own (`pnpm dev`), `?hour=21` starts at that hour.
- **On your own** (a solo session, or nobody else here once the first lists are in: `Together.surelyAlone`), you can hold the clock and rest until dawn, noon, dusk or night. In company it runs for everyone, and a rest needs all of you (Playing together, above). (Just after joining, before the lists named everyone, holding or resting set the room's clock.)
- **The clock in the HUD** (`TimeHUD.js`): the time at the left of the purse, with a sun, a moon, or two bars while it's held. T (R3 on a gamepad, or a tap) opens a small menu under the purse. It says when Joe and Marta open or shut, and offers holding and resting, or, in company, who wants to rest until when. A dot on the clock says someone else wants to rest. Nothing about the clock is a toast: a rest dims the world for a moment instead.

**The week's order** (`src/game/WeekOrder.js`): a restaurant on the mainland orders one kind of fish through Joe, so many kilos by the end of the week, and everyone in the group fills it together.

- **Real weeks:** it runs from Monday 00:00 UTC to the next, the same for everyone wherever they are, and comes from the week alone (`weekOrder(now)`). The kinds come round in turn (red snapper, black grouper, mahi-mahi, tuna, yellowtail, hogfish), never the same two weeks running. The kilos are about twelve fish of the kind; the bonus is half of what that would fetch at Joe's price, and at least $200.
- **Filling it:** every one of those fish sold to Joe, or left in his night box, goes toward it and fetches half as much again (`WEEK_MUL`), or the day's list's price if it's on that too. The biggest go first, and the fish that fills it counts whole. The cooler and the catch card tag the fish it would take.
- **The tally** is the group's (`'weekOrder'` in the shared save: Playing together, above): who brought how many kilos. On its own the game keeps it in the stand-in's storage, so a player alone fills it alone.
- **Getting paid:** once it's filled, the bonus is shared by what each player brought. Joe pays your share when you next come to his stall while he's in. The last filled week's tally is kept for whoever hasn't collected yet, and your save records the last week you were paid for (`weekPaid`).
- **Where it shows:** under the day's list on Joe's slate (the fish, the group's kilos and a chalk bar), and as a block in Joe's panel with the bonus, the days left and what you've brought. A sale toward it says so in its usual line. Nothing else is a toast.
- A tally saved for a fish that has since been protected (the Nassau grouper's week) can't be filled; the week's order replaces it (`tallyFor`).

**Tarpon under the pier lights:** after dark the pier's lamps draw the tarpon (`lights` in `FishTable.js`).

- Their bites under the pier double while the lamps are lit (`Bites.lampsLit`, `Bites.pickSpecies`). A tarpon is let go: it's catch and release (Spearfishing, below).
- The bay's tarpon swim to the pier head once the lamps come on and back to the bay by day (`night` on their group in `world/Fish.js`; the app sets the lamps' state on the fish each frame).

## The weather

`src/game/Weather.js` is what the weather is, with no three.js (`test/weather.mjs` runs it under Node). `src/world/WeatherDriver.js` puts it on the island each frame.

- **From the day alone:** the weather is a function of the island's time (`islandTime( day, hour )`, game hours since day 1 began), as the wanted lists are of the day. Everyone on one clock has the same weather, and nothing is sent.
  - Three slow fields over that time: storminess (the wind, the swell), cloudiness (it can grey a calm day too) and how far the wind has turned from the trades (`veer`: up to about 35° either way). Each is value noise in two octaves, about 60 h and 14 h, mapped through its own distribution so that it's spread evenly.
  - They're read against the conditions' rows (`CONDITIONS`): calm, fair, breezy, cloudy, rain, blustery, gale and thunderstorm (`storm`). About half the time it's fair, a tenth calm, and a gale (or a thunderstorm in a gale's wind) about one hour in twenty-five.
  - **The rain** (M9) comes from two quicker fields: showeriness (about 3.5 h and 1.1 h) and how unsettled the air is (about 9 h and 3 h). It rains where it's showery enough for the sky: now and then a light shower on a clear fair day, about a third of the time under a grey sky, most of a gale (`RAIN_CHANCE`). It's heavier the greyer and stormier it is (`rain`, 0..1: 1 a tropical downpour), and the heaviest, in unsettled air, is a thunderstorm (`thunder`, 0..1). Rain brings its cloud, greys the sky and closes in the horizon. All told it rains about one hour in five (mostly showers), and thunders about one in twenty-five (`test/weather.mjs`).
  - **A blow on its way** (`front`, 0..1): how much stormier it gets in the next 2 to 4 hours. The fish feel it (below).
  - **(M12) Each blow its own track** (`trackAt`, `windAngle`): each peak of storminess's slow octave (on its lattice, every 60 game hours, `TRACK_SPAN`) is a blow, and a hash of it gives its track: down the trades about half the time (turned by `veer` as they always were), from the south about a sixth, from the west about a seventh, and now and then (about a fifth) a front. Between two blows the track goes over in the middle of the gap. A blow turns the wind to its track as it comes on (from a breeze to a stiff one, `BLOW`), so short of a breeze the wind's the trades' exactly, and a southerly backs it round from the trades through the west.
    - A front (`frontAt`): from the south ahead of it; at its blow's height (a few hours either side) a line of showers comes over (`FRONT_RAIN`) and the wind veers to the west in two game hours, then on round to the north over five more; behind it the sky clears (cover down to 0.4, no grey, the far horizon clear) for most of a day.
    - The weather comes the way the wind blows: the showers, the front's bank and the lightning come from up the track (`towardOf( windAngle )`).
    - **A westerly's swell** (`west`, 0..1): how much of the swell comes from the west, from the storm it comes from (the last six hours' westerly, as the swell it raised outlasts it, or the one on its way). None from a blow down the trades or from the south: the island's own swell comes from the south (`swellFrom`: 'S', 'S + W', 'W', on the slate and the clock's menu).
    - In 1,000 days: a blow is off the trades about two fifths of the time, the swell's from the west 5% of the time, and 14 fronts pass in full (`test/weather.mjs`).
  - **(M14) The north shore's sea** (`north`, m: the shore waves' amplitude; `northPeriod`, s; `NORTH`): the trades' own sea, raised by their wind over the open ocean up them and breaking on the island's windward side, its north shore, in the mountain's lee from the village, as a windward shore's trade swell does. As high as the wind blows down the trades (all of it within 0.5 rad of them, none past 1.2: none from a southerly or a westerly), growing as the wind sea does (wind^1.5): 1–3 ft on a fair day at 6.4 s, 3–5 breezy, 6–12 ft in a gale at 9 s (its period 5 s and a fifth of the wind's m/s). It comes a little ahead of its blow, builds over hours as the wind comes on and outlasts it, dying down over hours once it's dropped or swung away (the wind's sea of the hours about, from four ahead to four back, `hours`). A hurricane's waves break on it as far as they come that way, as into the bay (`BAY`'s floor and confused sea). Past 5 ft about one hour in fourteen (`test/weather.mjs`).
  - **(M14) The bay in the island's lee:** a blow's sea and swell get into the bay only as far as they travel that way (`bayInto`, `BAY`: from the south, and round its western arm from the west; a little from anywhere), the swell from the storm ahead and the sea of the last six hours (a swell outlasts its wind's swing, as a westerly's does). A blow down the trades adds little to the bay (2–4 ft at most of its own: the bay's swell is the south's, as on a fair day, or what's left of an earlier blow's), while its sea breaks on the north shore; the open sea's swell (from the south) stays a fair day's with it. Before M14 every blow sent the bay its swell, from whichever way it came. Big surf in the bay (3–6 ft and up) is now about one hour in eleven, from southerlies, westerlies, fronts and hurricanes; it was one in four, three in five of those down the trades.
  - **(M14) A picked weather's wind is the trades'** (its row), so its sea's the same: a picked gale's breaks on the north shore (6–12 ft) and leaves the bay 2–4 ft (`row`: `bayInto` of the trades). The clock's menu has a southerly gale after the gale (`SOUTHERLY`, 'Southerly': `PICK_IDS`, `isPick`), a gale blowing in from the south, its sea and swell into the bay (3–6 ft at 7.5 s, as a picked gale's were), its clouds and showers coming up from the south, and none on the north shore. Its name is a gale's (`id`: what shuts the stalls, what's chalked).
  - **(M11) The showers about the island** (`showers`): cells of rain drifting with the weather (`FRONT_KMH`, 8 km a game hour), each bringing the rain the island has when it's over it (`weatherAt` then). So a shower you see coming is the rain that comes, and one going away out to sea is the rain that went. A slot every 4 km in the drifting frame, two cells a slot set off to either side (up to 14 km); those that pass over the island rain whenever it does, those off to the side half the time ((M12) a front's line all of them); each 1.2 to 3 km across. (M12) Each slot comes the way the wind blows when it's over the island (each cell's `x`, `z`): a southerly's showers are seen coming up from the south, where from the pier and the village the mountain no longer hides them. A picked weather holds its own rain in every slot (none for fair), down the trades. `test/weather.mjs`.
  - The name goes by a thunderstorm first, then the wind, then the rain and the cloud (`nameOf`).
  - The swell comes from the storm ahead (storminess 3 and 6 hours on), so the surf builds before a blow: (M14) in the bay before one whose sea gets there, on the north shore before one down the trades.
  - The surf's period: a storm's own swell is short (7.5 s in a gale), a swell from one still on its way long (up to `PERIOD_MAX`, 12 s), and by then smaller (`GROUNDSWELL_MAX`, 0.4 m, at the longest). Fair's is 9 s.
  - Only `+ − × ÷` and integer hashes: every device works out the same condition from the same time.
  - Day 1 is fair, and the forecast comes in over the first hours of day 2 (a new player's first morning; the checks start there).
  - **Fair is the island as it always was:** 7 m/s of wind from the trades' direction, today's sea, surf (9 s), cloud and light, and dry.
  - **The lightning's strikes** come from the real second and how thundery it is (`strikeIn`): in each second, a strike or none (at most `STRIKE_RATE`, one every 6 s or so in a full thunderstorm), where (from the island's middle, mostly up the blow's track until the storm's overhead, near ones rare; (M12) the track from the clock, `WeatherDriver.track`, not the eased wind, so everyone's strike is in the same place), and whether it comes down as a bolt (a third do). Everyone on one clock sees the same strike at the same moment, whatever their island clock (held, or resting).

## What the weather costs

(M10) Mild costs, never a game over: the boat's hull, the fishing in a blow, the water after one, and the island in it. None of it on a fair day.

**The boat's hull** (`src/game/Hull.js`, pure, tested under Node by `test/hull.mjs` with the real boat in the CPU's sea; `Game.updateHull` feeds it each frame and does what it says):
- **Wear** (`GameState.hull`, 0..1, saved until Marta repairs it):
  - From slams into a sea past 0.5 (`slamWear`: a fair day's hardest flat out are under it). Flat out into a gale's sea she wears about 8% a minute; at half throttle, next to nothing.
  - From knocks on the piles and drops onto the ground (`hitWear`, from 1.5 m/s closing: a pile at full speed, about a tenth), and from being driven along the bottom (`scrapeWear`, and a grind under power while she slides: a beaching at full speed, about a tenth). `BoatController.contact` records the hardest of each since the frame before.
  - It costs her speed (`thrustMul`: fully worn, 35% of her thrust gets her anywhere, a quarter off her top speed) and it leaks (half a litre a second, fully worn).
- **The bilge** (litres, for the voyage: not saved):
  - In: the leak, the rain into her open cockpit (0.1 L/s in a downpour), and green water over the bow in a slam past 0.85.
  - Out: the bucket (hold E on deck: 3 L/s, a scoop every 1.6 s; the rail's "Jump overboard" makes way for it, `Player.bailable`) and Marta's electric bilge pump (`bilgePump` in `Gear.js`, $120: 0.3 L/s, more than a downpour brings in).
  - Her mass is hers and what's in it: she sits lower (+500 kg, 3.6 cm), and others see it in her pose.
  - It reaches the engine at 500 L (`FLOOD_L`): the gauge pulses from 70% of it, and she coughs from 80% (her revs dip).
- **The engine** (`Hull.engine`: off, cranking, running, stalled; `BoatController.engineOn`):
  - Taking the helm cranks her (1.1 s, `CRANK_S`): a sound engine catches first time, one that's stalled takes a try or two (`crankChance`, less in a worn boat).
  - She stalls when the water reaches the engine, and won't start again until it's under 80% of that; or on a hard slam (0.95) in a worn boat (30%), now and then; or when the tank's dry.
  - Stalled, opening the throttle cranks her (W, the stick), and the helm's prompt says so. Not running, she has no thrust and her revs run down; she burns no fuel.
- **Marta's repair** (`Game.repairJob`, `GameState.payRepair`): her panel's row while she's open, with the boat at her dock: $150 for all of it, or as much of it as your money pays for.
- **The HUD**: the hull's gauge in the purse once it's worn, the bilge's while there's water in it; the guide's tips for the first water and the first wear; toasts when she stalls and as she wears.
- **Sound** (`SoundScape`; CC0 field recordings built by `tools/audio/build-hull.mjs`):
  - the starter (`starter.ogg`: a crank that won't catch, and one that catches as `CRANK_S` ends and runs into her idle, as the engine's bed comes up), on every start;
  - the hull knocking a pile (`hull_bump.ogg`, with the shutter's bang slow and low under a hard one) and the keel's scrape (`keel_scrape.ogg`);
  - the bucket (`bucket.ogg`), the pump (`bilge_pump.ogg`), the water sloshing in the bilge as she works (`bilge_slosh.ogg`), and the hull creaking in a sea, more often the more she's worn (`hull_creak.ogg`).
  - The boat's set loads once you're aboard (`boatReady`), so the starter's ready for the helm.
  - (M11) Whoever rides along hears them too: while you're aboard your boat and there's something to hear, her hull goes with her pose (`H`: the bilge's share of the engine's water, the pump, the keel scraping, her wear, how hard she's working: `App.hullHeard`), and their soundscape plays it as yours does (`Together.boatSound().hull`). A boat going by is heard the same way, faintly.

**Fishing in a blow** (the wind past a fair day's: `Weather.windBeyondFair`, `blowShare`):
- **The cast** (`FishingRod`): the wind bellies the line and carries the float along (`LINE_WIND`): into a gale a full cast falls a few metres short, across one it's set aside; downwind it runs on as far as the rod casts.
- **The float** drifts downwind, 2% of the wind past a fair day's, up to 6 m from where it came down and no shallower than 0.3 m. The bites follow it (`Game.habitat` reads where it is), and others see it in the pose.
- **The cast net** (`CastNet._step`): in the air each piece is dragged toward the wind, not still air; the netting's carried off far more than the leads, so in a gale it lands downwind of where it was aimed.
- **The fight** (`CatchMinigame`, `wind`): gusts on the line every few seconds, for a second or so, haul on it (`GUST`: 0.6 of the breaking point in a gale). Reel on through one and the line snaps; let it be and the drag gives it line (`GUST_EASED`). A fair fight draws nothing for it and goes as it did.
- **(M14) A snapped line** (`FishingRod.snap`): its float and the broken end of the line (1.6 m of it, lying back the way it broke) are left on the water where they were, drifting off downwind on the waves (3% of the wind, at least 8 cm/s, as a float and its line are pushed), washed up where the water ends and lying there, gone once they're 120 m off or two and a half minutes on. You tie on a new hook and float before the next cast (`rigging`: 8 s, the rod held low and close, a real one's a minute or two), however the rod's been put away or the line cancelled meanwhile. No charge: tackle isn't an item, and a fair day's fishing costs what it did. The same when a goliath or a shark takes your fish off the hook. Only you see it drift (the others see your rod as at rest).

**The water after a blow** (`Weather.murkAt`, 0..1): the strongest blow of the last day (storminess past a stiff breeze's, full in a gale), as murky for six hours after and clearing over eighteen more; day 1 counts as fair, a held pick has its own. It's quite murky about one hour in eight.
- Under water the fog closes in (28 m down to 11 m) and turns a silty green-grey (`App`), and the caustics dim. (M11) And the fish notice you later: at the murkiest, at 60% of the distance they would in clear water (`Fish.wariness`, `MURK_WARY`; `test/spearfishing.mjs`).
- Joe's slate says the water's murky (unless a blow's coming, which it says first), and so does the clock's menu.

**(M12) The surf and a swimmer** (`ShoreWaves.currentAt`, `breakerAt` on the CPU, through `WaterQuery`; `Player.surf`; `test/waves.mjs`):
- **Rip currents:** seaward through the bar's rip channels (the shore waves' `shoreBar`, about every 100 m along the beach, some 20 m across: along the south's field, as the bottom's), from where you can't stand (1 m deep, in full from 1.6) out past the breakers (1.3 to 2.6 times their depth), at 0.5 m/s for each metre of the breakers' height there (the bigger swell's), pulsing with the sets (0.7 to 1.5 of that, about a minute apart). A few tenths of a m/s on a fair day; near 1 in the biggest surf's pulses (MacMahan et al. 2006). Out of the channels, inside the breakers, feeder currents run along the beach into them at under half that.
  - A swimmer is carried by it (the water moves; their stroke is through it). Four seconds carried out by one and the guide's tip says so: swim along the beach, not against it.
  - On the GPU, a rip's water is milkier, in a plume out through its channel past the breakers (`shoreSurfTurbidity`), as you'd spot one from the beach.
- **Tumbled:** a breaker past 2.8 m (`TUMBLE_H`: trough to crest as the shore waves shape it; one in thousands on a fair day, one in seven of the biggest surf's), plunging, as its crest passes over a swimmer within 55% of its height of the surface: carried toward the beach (1.8 to 3.2 m/s, easing), held under (0.7 to 1.6 m), the view rolled over, and 2 to 6 s of breath gone, for 1.3 to 2.4 s, the more the bigger it is. Dived deep enough under it, it passes over. The first time, the guide's tip says so.
- **(M14) Knocked over wading:** a broken wave (past `TUMBLE_B`) taller than `knockHeight` at the still depth you stand in (`KNOCK_H`: 3.8 m of the shore waves' height at the knee, 3.0 at the thigh, 2.2 at the waist; less the deeper you stand, as the water takes your weight off your feet and more of you meets it: a person's footing in moving water, Jonkman & Penning-Rowsell 2008), a third more side-on to it (`KNOCK_SIDE`, as lifeguards tell waders to stand), knocks you off your feet as it passes (`Player.wadeSurf`): tumbled toward the beach as a swimmer is, more briefly (0.9 to 1.8 s, carried at 1.2 to 2.6 m/s, 0.3 to 0.9 m under, 1 to 3 s of breath), and you find your feet again once it's let you go. At the waist a fair day's set wave now and then (0.7% of them), a blustery surf's one in five, the biggest one in three; at the knee only a big surf's, one in ten; side-on, the biggest at the waist one in eight (`test/waves.mjs`). The first time, the guide's tip says so: turn side-on, or keep to the shallows. A breaker's bore height is on the CPU's `breakerAt` (`Hb`). Before M14 the waves never moved a wader.

**The surf and the fish in it** (`FishSchools.setSurf`, from the shore waves' height):
- The breakers' depth follows the surf (`breakDepth`, a fair day's where it always was), and their look-ahead grid is built again once it's moved 3 cm.
- The bait runners inside the breakers (`runnerMullet`, `runnerFry`) keep inside them, deeper in a big surf. From a surf of 0.5 m some of their schools go off, up to about half at the biggest: one that's out of range doesn't come back into it.

**The island in a blow:**
- **(M14) The shutters** (`village/Shutters.js`): the houses' side-hung shutters (louvered and board) are a mesh of their own (Buildings' `windowUnit` into the village's `shutterB`: one more draw, its vertex arrays kept to move, `userData.keepArrays`), 188 of them, and one in eight hangs loose. - **(M14) Litter blown along** (`fx/BlownLitter.js`): from a fresh breeze (9 m/s, all of it by 17), the island's dry litter skitters over the sand and the paths in stops and surges with the gusts: sea grape leaves (round, tan to rust) that stand up on their edges as they go faster and cartwheel, rolling as far as they run; dry palm leaflets, swinging round to trail and flipping; tufts of sargassum, hopping. One instanced draw (60 on low, 150 on mid, 240 on high), held out of the load's compile with the rain's.

## The hurricane

(M13) Now and then a hurricane passes: a Category 1, (M14) or a 2 or a 3, from the clock as the rest of the weather is, so the same one for everyone. Its weather is `Weather.js`'s (`HURRICANE`, `SURGE`; `test/weather.mjs`, `test/waves.mjs`); what it does to the island, the animals and the sky is in the files below, each with its own tests.

**The storm** (`hurricaneAt( T )`: its eye's place and its own figures; `weatherAt` adds `hurricane`, its share of the weather here, and `eye`, `surge`, `rainHaze`, `sprayHaze` and `boarded`):
- **When:** one blow on the lattice (`TRACK_SPAN`) in 16 is a hurricane's, about every 40 days. Never two within a week, and none before day 15 (a new player's first fortnight).
- **(M14) Its category** (`HURRICANE.cats`, `categoryOf`: Saffir-Simpson, by its strongest wind, a minute's sustained, its motion and all): as often as they've struck the US mainland (NOAA, 1851-2020: of the 271 Categories 1 to 3, 45% a 1, 32% a 2, 23% a 3; none stronger here). A 1's vortex 32 to 37 m/s at rmax (with its motion 34 to 41, as it was for all of them before), a 2's 41 to 45 (43 to 49), a 3's 48 to 54 (50 to 58). Its waves are the bigger for it, as its wind is (`size`: its surf into the bay and on the north shore, and its swell, a 2's a fifth over a 1's, a 3's two fifths: a 3's surf to 2.1 m, `SURF_BIGGEST`), its surge the higher (its wind's set-up as its square, its low's lift as its pressure falls further: allowed to 3.2 m, `SURGE.high`, from a 1's 1.6), and its wind tears more: every sheet off the roofs in a 2's or a 3's, the palms stripped to their crowns (`DAMAGE.palm.more`). The sea's highest reach (its surge and its surf's crests) a 1's never past 2.9 m, a 2's to 3.7 now and then, a 3's to 4.6: up into the village's front row (its ground at 3.3 to 3.8 m) and the stilt houses, the plaza's edge (`test/storm-damage.mjs`). The bay's own lee still keeps most of it out: one coming down the trades blows the bay's water out on its strong side, and only the wind coming round behind its eye piles it in. Your own, picked, is of its category as the day's come, from a hash of when it's picked. It's said with it: "A Category 3 hurricane far off to the NNW", "Hurricane warning (Category 3)" in the clock's menu, "Cat 3 hurricane warning" on Joe's slate, and on the shelter's chart. Its wall comes over faster, and its surge rises faster, than a 1's.
- **Its track:** steered down the trades (turned a little either way), at 14 to 24 km a game hour, its eye over the island or to one side of it (within 1.2 of its radius of strongest wind, `rmax`, 20 to 32 km; a quarter pass their calm over it).
- **Its wind:** a vortex's, counter-clockwise and turned 20° in toward the eye, calm within 0.4 rmax, rising through its wall to 32 to 37 m/s at rmax and broad beyond it (vmax (rmax + a) / (r + a), a = 5.6 rmax). Its own motion adds to it, so its right side is stronger and its left a strong gale, as on a real Category 1's weak side. It fades out past 450 to 750 km, and the island's own weather comes back. The wind comes round the compass as it passes: `WeatherDriver` turns it the shorter way round, quicker in the eye's light airs.
- **Its rain:** the eyewall's downpour and the rain shield about it (`core`, out to 1.6 to 3.2 rmax), and two outer bands spiralling in, 60 km apart, crossed one after another as it comes, thundering where they're heaviest. The eyewall seldom thunders, and none falls in the eye.
- **Its cloud** spreads 700 km ahead and greys the sky from 600 km; in the eye the sky opens (the eye's sky, below).
- **Its sea:** its swell runs out ahead of it, long and low days ahead (16 s), shorter and higher close in (11 s; the open sea's swell to 3.5 times today's). Into the bay comes as much of its waves as come in the way they travel (`BAY`): close in, its wind's own sea, going the way the wind blows; further off, its swell running out from the eye; about the eye, a confused sea from all round. The bay opens to the south, so one coming down the trades leaves it in the island's lee until its wind blows in from the sea. Its surf goes past `SURF_MAX`, to 1.5 m (8 to 15 ft breakers).
- **The water's murky** from 8 hours before its eye until a day after, clearing over three more days.
- **What's said of it** (`hurricaneNews`): an outlook four days before its tropical-storm winds, a watch 48 hours before, a warning 36 hours before, as the National Hurricane Center's. On Joe's slate, the clock's menu, the dash's plotter (`App.weatherAlert`), and as toasts when it's watched for, warned of and arrives (`Game.updateHurricane`).
- **On your own**, the clock's menu's Hurricane brings one of your own straight over, its eye passing six game hours from when you picked it (`since`, saved with the clock). `?weather=hurricane` does it from the hour you start at.
- All of it `+ − × ÷` and `sqrt` on the clock, as the rest is; only the wind's direction takes `atan2`.

**The surge and the surf** (step 2):
- **The surge** (`SURGE`): the sea drawn up under the storm's low pressure (the inverse barometer, about 1 cm a hPa), and piled into the bay by its wind blowing in over the hour before, or drawn out by one blowing out (the wind's set-up, as on the narrow shelf of the Keys). On the left of its track the sea rises up to a metre; on its right the bay drains and lies in the lee. `G.seaLevel` follows it, and the sea's culling box moves and opens with it. (M14) The box is never smaller than the open sea's own reach (`WeatherDriver.seaBox`, `Waves.reachOf`: its waves' amplitudes all in step, and 15% more): a Category 3's open sea comes to 3.85 m on the high tier (sampled, its crests to 3.2 m and its troughs to 3.5), inside the ±4 m it always was by 15 cm, so its box opens to ±4.4 m; on low, 2.85 m, and the box as it was.
- **Its shore field:** the island's own swell's field solved again at the highest the sea will rise (`App.solveSurgeField`, in the westerly's worker), once the storm's watched for or near, never at load. The shore waves blend it in as the sea rises toward it (`ShoreWaves.setUpperField`, on the GPU and the CPU), and it's dropped once the storm's gone. (M17) It's solved on the tide's high water (its peak plus `TIDE_FIELDS[ 1 ]`), and takes high water's field's place while it's there (one texture slot, `shoreTexS`); high water's is solved again after.
- **What assumed the sea at 0 follows it:** the fish's ceilings and leaps and the diver they notice; the boat's reset and its fallbacks; the sonar's and the anchor's depths; wet sand underfoot; the reef's culling; the birds' floors; the rocks the rain splashes on; the terrain's wet band; the surf's sound stations.
- `test/waves.mjs`: green water over the pier, the beach awash, the village dry, the surge field's blend, the drawdown, a hurricane's open sea.

**The wind and the rain** (step 3):

## A catch with no room for it

(M15) `GameState.addFish` logs every catch; one that doesn't fit isn't kept, and `lastCatch` keeps what's needed to keep it later (`at`, `day`). `GameState.roomFor( info )` says whether room could be made for it (not let go for its own sake, no heavier than the cooler), and `keepLast( info )` keeps it once it fits (counting a bag-limited one in that day's bag), only once.

The catch card (`GameHUD.showCatch`) for such a catch is a room card (`hud.room`): the cooler's fish to let go (`renderRoom`, redrawn by `refresh` as the cooler changes), no timer, and taps on its background ignored. `Game.cardEnd( info, t, lDown )` ends every catch card, the rod's, the speargun's and the lobster net's: a room card with its own buttons (`takeCatchAct`) and keys (← → `roomStep`, R `roomLetGo`, Enter, E, Esc), keeping the fish on 'keep'; any other as before. `Game.makingRoom` keeps R, N, I / Tab and the arrows from the gear and the panels while it's up.

(v2.1.6) **Let go off its card.** A catch just kept may go straight back instead, alive, while its card is up: `GameState.canFree( info )` (kept, not let go already, still in the cooler, not speared: a speared fish is dead) and `freeLast( info )`, which takes it out of the cooler (`lastCatch.id` names its entry) and out of the day's bag. The rules count the fish kept, not caught: one put straight back never was, while one let go from the cooler later still counts (`bagged`), so a full bag can't be swapped for a bigger fish. It stays in the log. The card's button (`hud.free`, `takeCatchAct` 'free') or R / X does it (`Game.cardEnd`, `freeCatch`: a splash and a toast with the day's count); while it's offered `Game.cardFree` keeps R from the rod, as `makingRoom` does.

## Spearfishing

The speargun is `src/game/Speargun.js` and its half of `src/game/Game.js`. The fish's side is in `src/world/Fish.js`, and the held breath in `src/player/Player.js`.

- **The law** (`game/FishTable.js`):
  - Each species is a fish spearfishers take (`spear`), protected (`protected`: the Nassau grouper, the stoplight parrotfish, the tarpon), or neither (the reef's aquarium fish and the bait).
  - Some have a minimum size (`minCm`). `spearRule()` says whether one may be taken, and why not.
  - A protected fish caught on a line is measured, logged and released. The island's records still count it.
  - Joe pays a bounty on lionfish, an invader on the reef.
- **One key, R**: the rod on land or on deck, the speargun in the water. Neither leaves its element (`Game.canFish`, `canSpear`), so changing one for the other means getting out of the water or into it. The speargun fires only once you've dived (`Game.submerged`).
- **The sights** (`Game.sights`) name the first fish along the view within the shot's range (`Fish.pierce`), unless the reef is in the way (`groundHit`). The gun won't fire at one that mustn't be taken (`gun.safety`).
- **The shot** (`Speargun.stepShaft`):
  - It flies with the water's drag and a little sinking, and stops at the end of its shooting line.
  - Each step it's tested against the fish it may take (`Fish.pierce`), the bottom and the reef (`groundHit`, in ~10 cm steps) and the piles (`Colliders.segmentHit`).
  - A long shot at a big fish glances off.
- **A fish on the shaft** is the reef's own fish:
  - `Fish.hold` poses it on the shaft, thrashing, and the simulation leaves it alone. `release` lets it swim off; `take` removes it until its place is filled, out of sight, a few minutes later.
  - A clean shot through the head, or a small fish, is hauled straight in. A big one fights: `CatchMinigame` with `landAt` and `tired` lands it only once it's tired. One shot through the tail can work itself off.
  - It's weighed by the size it swam at (`fishWeightKg`), and its catch keeps the method (`'spear'`).
  - It sells like any other fish: it counts toward Joe's list and the week's order.
- **The fish notice the diver** (`Fish.wary`, `WARY`): the faster the diver comes, the farther off they react, and a fright keeps them warier a while (`SPOOKED`). (M11) In murky water they notice later (`MURK_WARY`). Frightened groupers tuck in at their coral head. Jacks, barracuda and mahi come to look at a diver keeping still (`curious`).
- **The spearfisher's fish**: hogfish on the sand at the reef's foot, black groupers and lionfish at the coral heads, mutton snappers along the reef's edges, red snappers over the deep holes in the bay and beyond its mouth, mahi and blackfin tuna in the blue water off the bay.
- **The breath** (`Player.breathe`):
  - 50 s finning easy, less kicking hard, more keeping still. It comes back at the surface in 10 s.
  - Running out is a blackout: the swimmer floats up, and whatever's on the line is lost.
  - With the speargun out the swimmer stalks (`Player.stalk`): a slow glide along, still brisk going down or up.
- **The gun in your hands**:
  - It's held like the rod: a pose in camera space, eased between its states.
  - Its depth is pressed against the near plane (`GameMaterials` `viewModel`), so the reef never cuts through it.
  - The bands are tubes the vertex shader lays out: stretched thin on the shaft's notch, slack under the muzzle, or on the way between as they're loaded.
  - Under the sea it takes the water's colour, as the reef does (`underwater`).
- **Playing together**:
  - The pose carries the speargun (`g`), which gun it is (`gl`) and the shaft while it's out (`G`).
  - Others see it in your hand (`gunModels`, per gun), its shaft and line, and a speared fish on the shaft.
  - The catch message and the records say a fish was speared (`m`).
- **Sound**: the speargun has no recordings of its own yet, so the fishing ones stand in (`SoundScape.spearShot`, `spearHit`, `bandStretch`).

## The cast net

The cast net is `src/game/CastNet.js` and its half of `src/game/Game.js`. The fish's side is in `src/world/Fish.js`; the bait is `src/game/Bait.js`, kept in `GameState`.

- **One key, N**: the net goes wherever the rod does (`Game.canFish`: ashore, wading, on the pier or on deck) and takes its place. R swaps it for the rod. Swimming or taking the helm puts it away, and whatever's in it goes back.
- **The throw**:
  - Hold the left mouse button (F, RT, or Throw on a touch screen) to wind up; let go to throw.
  - A ring on the water shows where it will land and how wide it opens. It brightens during the wind-up's moment (`SWEET`, as wide as the net's `spreadWin`): let go then and the net spins fully open; early or late, it opens partly (`openQuality`). Held too long, the arms tire.
  - The ring reddens over dry ground. Where the water is deeper than the hand line reaches (`aimReaches`), the prompt says the net will close before it gets to the bottom.
- **The net** is a radial cloth (`CastNet._step`):
  - 9 rings of 28 particles from the horn to the lead line, joined by ropes that pull and never push (a net folds and bunches), solved by position-based dynamics in substeps.
  - The leads are heavy and the netting light. In the water everything slows to the net's sinking speed (`sinkMs`). The horn is tied to the hand line and to the brails.
  - The hands pin the particles they hold (`HANDS`, in camera space): loaded, the horn and a gather of netting are in the right hand and a third of the lead line over the left. The throw sets the particles off with the spin the wind-up gave it.
  - Drawn, the netting is a smooth surface through the particles with a diamond-mesh shader (twine, knots, a veil far off). The lead line is a tube with sinkers along it, the brails and hand line are ribbons, and the horn and swivel are a small mesh.
  - States: `stowed`, `held`, `windup`, `throw`, `flying`, `sinking` (everything from the splash until it's out), `lifted`, `dumping`, `gather`. The game drains its moments with `takeEvents` (`'throw'`, `'splash'`, `'land'`, `'bottom'`, `'pursed'`, `'lifted'`, `'dump'`).
- **Closing and hauling**: holding the button draws the hand line in. First it pulls the brails through the horn, closing the lead line under the net into a purse (`draw`, 0 to 1); then the purse comes in hand over hand, slower with a heavy catch (`load`). It hangs in front of you a moment, dripping, then the brails let go and the catch spills out.
- **The fish under it** are the island's own fish (`Fish.netLand`, `netStep`):
  - When the net lands on the water, every fish inside the lead line (within `NET_EDGE` of it) is in it, and the rest nearby scatter.
  - The lead line's ring of splashes turns the fish inside in toward the middle for a moment (`NET_TURN`). Then each makes for the edge. One that gets there below the lead line slips out under it, until the lead line is on the bottom or drawn closed. A mullet may leap clear while it settles. A fish too long for the net tears out (`holdCm`); one small enough slips through the mesh (`meshCm`).
  - Drawn closed before it's on the bottom, the purse is the netting above the lead line, and a fish below it gets away. So in deep water (off the pier, round its piles) it's let sink first, and a heavier net gets down before they're out: from the deck, the 4 ft net keeps one now and then, the 10 ft net most of what it lands on.
  - Off a pier or a deck in deep water, the hand line runs out before the lead line reaches the bottom: the net purses where it hangs, and fish can still swim out under it until it's closed.
  - The net counts the ones that got away and how (`Fish.net.out`). An empty haul says so, instead of a tip.
  - Once the brails are mostly drawn (`BAG_FROM`), the fish ride in the purse, kicking (`Fish.hold`).
- **The haul** (`GameState.addHaul`): each fish is logged.
  - A mullet of 0.45 kg or more (`KEEPER_KG`) goes in the cooler (method `'net'`), and sells like any other fish.
  - Silversides, sergeant majors, grunts and smaller mullet go into the bait bucket while there's room.
  - Anything else, a protected fish, or a fish with no room for it, is thrown back toward where the net was thrown.
  - The haul card (`GameHUD.showHaul`) says what went where and why, for a few seconds over the prompt.
- **Where the bait is**:
  - Along the main beach, pods of finger mullet and silverside fry run the trough inside the breakers at wading depth (`runnerMullet`, `runnerFry`: `surf`, so they may swim where waves break). More fry school just outside the breakers.
  - Sergeant majors and grunts mill round the pier's piles.
  - Schools of bigger mullet cruise the bay in chest-deep water and deeper: from the pier or a boat.
  - Silverside schools swim over the reef, and a bait ball turns at its drop-off: from a boat.
- **The shop** (`Gear.js`, at Marta's chandlery):
  - The cast net: a hand-me-down 4 ft net; 6 ft monofilament ($70); an 8 ft bait net with heavy lead ($210); a 10 ft pro net ($520). Each step covers more water, sinks faster, throws farther and holds bigger fish, but its moment to let go is narrower.
  - The bait well: a 5-gallon bucket (24 baits), an aerated bucket ($90, 50), a livewell with a pump ($300, 120).
  - An upgrade bought while the net is out takes effect once it's back in the hands (`CastNet.setGear`).
- **Bait on the hook** (`Bait.js`):
  - B (the d-pad's ← → with the rod out, or the bait button on a touch screen) picks what goes on the hook at the next cast: the frozen shrimp, which never runs out, or a kind from the bucket. The cast takes one from the bucket; a live one still on the hook when the rod goes away goes back in.
  - Live and cut bait bite sooner (`speed`), draw their predators (`likes`), put the little fish off (`small`) and bring bigger fish (`skew`), through `Bites.pickSpecies`, `rollWeight` and `biteDelay`. A nibble can steal it (`steal`).
  - A mullet in the cooler can be cut into 2 to 8 chunks of cut bait (`GameState.cutBait`, one per 200 g or so). Cut bait doesn't count against the bucket.
  - Marta's daily bait order can be filled from the bucket (`GameState.sellBait`).
  - The bucket and the pick are saved with your progress (`bait`, `baitPick`).
- **Playing together**:
  - The pose carries the net's state (`n`), its size (`nl`), and while it's in the water how far the brails are drawn (`nd`) and how much hand line is out (`nh`). The throw itself is a `'net'` message: where it was aimed, how well it opened, and the seed of its spin.
  - Others throw a copy of your net as you threw it (`CastNet.remoteThrow`, `follow`), from their copy of you, and its arms reach for the net's hands (`Avatar.reachArm`). A net more than 90 m away isn't drawn or simulated.
  - A mullet netted for the cooler counts toward the island's records, and the toasts say it was netted.
- **Sound**: no recordings of its own yet (`SoundScape.netThrow`, `netSplash`, `netLand`, `netPull`, `netLift`, `netDump`). The rod's swish slowed down is the net spinning out; splashes sped up are the lead line coming down; swim strokes are the wet netting drawn in.
- **On the island**: cast nets hang drying by their horns on the beach racks (`Props.castNetHung`, through `netRack`), and lie in heaps on the sand by the chandlery and on the pier by the bait bucket (`castNetHeap`).

## The boats

(M16) `world/boat/Boats.js` is the boats there are, as data (no three.js, so the game's logic and the tests read it freely). For `kayak`, `console`, `lobster` and `sportfisher` it has her `name`, `feet`, `fuel` (`'gasoline'`, `'diesel'` or null), `stats` (what she is before any of her tracks) and `load( tier )`, which makes her model. `START_BOAT` is `'kayak'`; `loadBoat( id, tier )` builds one. `?boat=console` in a dev server or a driven browser adds that boat to your fleet and starts you in it. [M16.md](M16.md) says what each is; the pages in `docs/m16/` are the detail.

- **Only the boat you're in is built.** `App.setBoat( id )` gets you into another of yours while you're ashore (not aboard, not riding): it loads her, takes the old one and her lamps out of the scene and the local lights, gives `BoatController` the new model (`setModel`, `reset`), lights her as the rest of the world is lit (`lightUp`), gives the instruments, the drips and the spray her points, and tells `Together.boatChanged` and `Game.boatChanged`. What asks `app.boat`, `boatCtl.model` or `boatCtl.handling` each time needs nothing. The others you own are drawn lightly (Your fleet, below).
- **What the game asks of a boat** is the header of `world/boat/Vessel.js`, which every boat extends: `BoatModel` (the lobster boat, `world/BoatModel.js`), `kayak/KayakModel.js`, `console/ConsoleModel.js` and `sportfisher/SportfisherModel.js`. In the boat's frame (+Z forward, +Y up, +X port, y = 0 the design waterline) she gives her `group`, `meshes` and `materials`; `lines` (a `HullLines`: floating, spray, walking and the lamps read them); `handling`; `dimensions`, `hydro` and `hullSamples` (from `floatBody()`); where the `helm` is and where you step aboard and off; her `colliders`; what the rain falls on (`rainTops`, `shelter`); her `wake`, `lamps` and `floods`; `seated`; `nearDetail` and `nearDist`; `bunks`; `transom`; `stations` (where a crew's jobs are done); `lowCopy` and `perch` (for the others' copies); and four that do nothing until a boat needs them: `fit( stats )`, `setPaint( hex | null )`, `walkedAs( stats )` and `dressCopy( copy, stats, shared )`.
- **Her lines** are a `HullLines` (`HullLines.js`, with `HullBuilder.js` and `GeoKit.js` to build from); a boat extends it and overrides its design curves. `ConsoleLines` is a polyline of sections with hard edges (a keel pad, strakes, a chine and its flat); `KayakLines` is the maker's plan and profile read off at 40 stations; `SportfisherLines` has a Carolina bow and hard chines, and `SF` says where her decks and bulkheads are.
- **How she handles** is `model.handling` (`Handling.js` lists every field), which `BoatController` reads (`this.handling = model.handling`): the wind she shows, her added mass and inertia, her resistance and trim by speed, the points that can take the ground, her `engine` (or `null`: nothing to burn) and her `propulsion( ctl, q )`, which puts on what drives and turns her. The lobster boat's is `propellerAndRudder` (`Handling.js`); the sportfisher's `twinScrews` (at the dock one engine goes ahead and one astern, and she turns on the spot); the console's `outboards` (no rudder: the thrust's line swings with the engines, `console/Outboards.js`); the kayak's `paddling` (`kayak/Paddle.js`: the blade pushes back by the speed it goes through the water). The rigid body, the buoyancy, the mooring, the anchor and the contacts are the controller's, the same for every boat.
  - **`rightFrom` and `rightK`.** The hull's buoyancy samples hold a boat only to a GZ of 0.05 to 0.1 m. Past `rightFrom` (rad) of heel or trim, her flare and chines right her harder, `rightK` N m per radian past it: 14° and 45,000 for the lobster boat, 10° and 52,000 the console, 8° and 420,000 the sportfisher, 46° and 2,200 the kayak (past where she capsizes: `kayak/Capsize.js`). Without it the sportfisher sat at 30° all but going straight, a console lay at 45° at a walking pace, and a lobster boat with a turbo diesel went to 73° with her bow in the air.
  - **`test/turns.mjs`** holds each boat to it. On flat water, the lever on for a minute and then the wheel over for 25 s, at each lever (0.45, 0.7, 1), both ways, with each engine upgrade she can have (the lobster boat ×1, ×1.15 and ×1.3; the console ×1 and ×1.25): she stays finite, leans no more than 25° out or in and keeps her bow within 15°. On the plane the sportfisher and the console lean into the turn (4 to 16°); below it the sportfisher is about upright; the lobster boat, no planing hull, leans a little out (1 to 8°).
- **(M17) The lobster boat's way forward and up** ([m17/back.md](m17/back.md); `DeckGear.WAY_UP`): two teak steps at the house's aft corner, to port, onto her washboard; her side decks past the house (about 8 in, as a Downeast boat's), walked with a hand on the roof's grab rail, to her foredeck with its forward-opening hatch and her anchor on its roller; and two fold-down steps on the house side, by the rail's post, up onto her roof, climbed as the sportfisher's ladders are (`ladderAt`, `climb`: a `LadderClimb`, its `lean` outboard). Her eye up there is about 4 m over her waterline (`roofEye`), and what's seen from it is the camera's own height (Glasses, Sight). On the side decks a walker's kept in by her sheer, not by her breadth less a walker's width (`clampToDeck`), and her house's sides and windshield are pressed close to (a collider's own `r`, Player). Her bait barrel's 0.32 m further aft, and her life ring's on her transom: the house side's her way forward now.
- **On a phone.** `nearDetail` names the meshes a phone may leave out far off, from `nearDist` m (`App.nearDetail`; 70 unless the model says: the lobster boat's is 45, where on Marta's cradle her fittings alone took a phone past its triangles). The sportfisher takes the tier: `new SportfisherModel( 'low' )`.
- **What's fitted, and her paint.** `Game.applyGear` calls `fit( stats )` as the gear changes and as she's built, with `Gear.gearStats( upgrades, boat )`; a model shows or hides what's fitted without another draw (the console rewrites a mesh's index, the kayak folds vertices to a point, the sportfisher shows or hides meshes). `setPaint( hex | null )` sets the hull's colour.
  - (M17) **Colours go to linear once.** three's `Color( hex )` and `setHex` take a hex as sRGB and make it linear themselves. The materials' own colours and the console's and sportfisher's paint went through `convertSRGBToLinear()` after that too (`BoatMaterials`' and `SportfisherMaterials`' `linear()`, `ConsoleMaterials.setPaint`), so every dark colour on a boat was too dark: barn red at 0.85% albedo where it's 6%, the console's own navy near black. Write a boat's colour as a hex and leave it to three ([m17/looks.md](m17/looks.md)).
  - (M17) **Her shade** (`BoatMaterials`' `SHADE` and `useShadeFill`, on her hull, decks, wood and fittings once she's built: `Vessel.shadeFill`, from `loadBoat`). The world's fill is kept low for the sand, so a boat's side out of the sun was a tenth of its sunlit brightness, the underside of a hardtop black, a dark paint near black. Her outside takes what a person's does (`GroundBounce`): the sky's light as much again, and the light thrown up from under her (`SHADE.bounce`, the sea's; the console's her white decks', `ConsoleMaterials`), diffuse only; and the sky in her gloss, the scene's environment `SHADE.env` times over, held by the material itself (three puts the scene's own intensity in place of a material's that reads the scene's). The sportfisher's own `OUTSIDE_FILL` went into it. The boats at their berths take the same (`MooredBoats`), so a boat matches herself at sea. Applied after a boat's own material changes: something that sets a material's `onBeforeCompile` afterwards loses it.
- **A trap, for anything built on `BoatMaterials`:** three shares a program between materials whose `onBeforeCompile` reads the same, and `BoatMaterials`' `extend()` reads the same for every one. Give each its own `customProgramCacheKey`, or a cabin silently comes out as the gelcoat ([sportfisher.md](m16/sportfisher.md)).
- **Her pictures** in Marta's list are `public/boats/<id>.webp`, drawn from the models by `tools/boat-pictures.mjs`. To look at a boat alone: `tools/console/` (`look.html`, `look.mjs`, `game.mjs`, `lines-check.mjs`), `tools/sportfisher/` (`view.html`, `shots.mjs`, `drive.mjs`, `walk.mjs`, `night.mjs`; her seats are modelled in Blender, `seats.py`, `build-seats.mjs`).
- **The pages:** [kayak.md](m16/kayak.md), [console.md](m16/console.md), [sportfisher.md](m16/sportfisher.md). Tests: `test/kayak.mjs`, `kayak-play.mjs`, `console.mjs`, `sportfisher.mjs`, `boats.mjs`, `turns.mjs`.

## Days out

(M16) `game/Seaworthy.js` is the figures (pure, so `Gear.js`, `Chart.js` and `Away.js` read it); `game/DaysOut.js` does what they say (`game.daysOut`, made by `Game`). [days-out.md](m16/days-out.md) has the sources.

- **What each boat can stand** (`ABLE`): the sea and the wind she is easy to and too much at (`seas`, `wind`), her cruise (`rpm`, `speed`) and burn (`[ idle, k ]` litres a second: `Gear.fuelBurn( rpm, boat, twin )`; (M17) the console's from Yamaha's bulletins for one F150 and, `twin`, two: `burnOf`), her drift and draught, the days of stores she holds and her drift sock's mouth. `seaHere( w, depth, fetch )` is the sea where she is: the open sea's (`Weather.seaHeight`, more past the far grounds by `withEdge`), its wind part grown with the root of the open water to windward (`fetchAt`), the swell falling off as the water shoals, no wave over half the depth. `strainOf( boat, Hs, U )` is 0 when she's fine, 1 when it's rough for her, and more when it's too much; past 0.6 her slams cost more (`slamK`), and past 1 she ships green water (`awashLs`, into `Hull.step`'s bilge). There are no walls.
- **Her gear for it** (`DAYS_TRACKS`, `BASE`): each boat's VHF, plotter, deep finder, deep-water anchor and drift sock, keyed `<what>_<boat>`, and your own `safety` (life jacket; flares and an EPIRB). A compass is free on every boat. The lobster boat's plotter and VHF, and the sportfisher's, are in `BASE`, not bought.
- **Ice and the catch** (`keepCatch`, `DaysOut.awayHours`): fish age by warm hours, 24 times slower on ice; the box melts its ice by its size (`meltKgH`), an icemaker adds `iceMakesKgH` (at sea off the generator: `GENERATOR_LH`), and a fish's `value` goes down as it ages (`v0` is what it fetched fresh). The same reckoning runs in play and while you're away (`App.pickUp`).
- **Stores, sleep and trouble.** Stores are $12 a day (`STORES_PRICE`) in lockers sized to the boat; `Vessel.bunks` are where E sleeps till dawn or dusk (`wakeOf` says what wakes you); `trouble()` is real trouble, and only then does the radio panel offer the flares and EPIRB (`distress()`, `fire()`); the drift sock goes out by itself past the anchor's reach (`BoatController` has it as a cone's drag at the stem head); the deep finder has a screen of its own over the map.
- **A rest, or time going by** (`DaysOut._clock`): a clock that's moved by more than it could have run since the last look, at night's pace, is a rest (her drift reckoned, you on deck: `passed`); less is time going by (`pass`). The clock's the wall's, frames or none, so a hidden page or slow frames aren't rests. (M17) It allows two seconds of the wall more than that (`CLOCK_SLACK_S`): in company the clock's read once in a frame and the wall's time later in it, a slow frame's half second apart, and at deep night the guard had had 1% to spare. A rest, a bunk or a tow moves the clock at once, by half an hour and more, and is still caught (`test/days-out-clock.mjs`).
- (M17) **A flare as it's seen** (`DaysOut._launch`, `_updateFlare`, `_flareBright`; [m17/looks.md](m17/looks.md)): a red meteor up to 120 m over her for 8 s, a sprite drawn as it is (sRGB, never tone-mapped: the brightest thing in view), a saturated red core with a glare about it by night and none by day, at least 12 CSS px across by night and 8 by day. How bright by Allard's law (16,000 cd, the eye's 2e-7 lux at night) with the air's transmission worked out so that it's at the eye's least where `FLARE_M` has it seen, by day against the sky's reach; drawn by the log of its light between its brightest (2 km by night, 250 m by day) and that reach. Its red light on the water and what's about it after dark is a local light (`LocalLights`: 4,000, to 500 m), so the sea's shader takes it as it takes the lamps: a red glitter path on mid and high. Someone else's, seen from here (`theirFlare`), is the same.
- **While you're away** `Away.js` reckons each boat by her own figures (`ableEnv( boat )`, `wearRate( w, boat )`), and every boat you own at her own berth (`othersAway`, `tellOthers`).
- **A sea breaking on her** (`game/BreakingSeas.js`): in a gale's sea, a roller you can see (`Whitecaps.foamAt`) at her bow, middle or stern up on a long wave's crest puts litres in her bilge (`Hull.HULLS`' `sea`), a hard slam, and a knock about her keel (`knock`). It counts only the short seas' rollers. (M17) It's told as it comes: where it came and what it left in her bilge (`seaAboardSaid`).
- **Tanks** (`game/Tanks.js`): a scuba tank is one dive's air; a fresh one comes from a tank rack aboard (the console's), or at the dock or Marta's.
- Tests: `test/days-out.mjs`, `test/days-out-clock.mjs`. Tools: `tools/boat-figures.mjs` (each boat's cruise, drift and draught, measured on her model), `tools/gale-figures.mjs`, `tools/earnings.mjs` (a steady hand's hour at each stage, for the prices).

## Ways to fish

## The anchor

Marta sells one (`anchor` in `Gear.js`: a Danforth on 200 ft of rode, `anchorM` 20, so it holds in up to 66 ft of water).

- **No button:** it goes down when you leave the helm out on the water and comes up when you take it again, as the dock lines do at the berth (`Game.updateAnchor`). It waits for the boat to slow below 2.5 m/s (`ANCHOR_SPEED`) and isn't used within 14 m of the berth, where the dock lines take her (`BERTH_M`). Deeper than the rode holds, a toast says so and she drifts.
- **The rode** (`BoatController.anchor`, the spot on the bottom): it's made fast at the stem head, slack until the bow swings 4 m from over the anchor (`RODE_SLACK`), then a damped spring that only pulls, so the boat is brought up short and her bow comes round to it.
- **What you see:** the rode, the fishing line's ribbon at twice its width in rope's colour, from the stem head down and along the bottom (`Game.rode`). At night the masthead shows the anchor light.
- Others see it too: the pose says your boat is at anchor (`A`), and their copy of it shows the anchor light.
- The wind pushes her (The weather, below), and the rode holds her against it: she swings round to lie head to wind, and in a gale she sails about on it before she settles.
- (M17) **At her bow**, the lobster boat's (her `bowAnchor`, by her roller, reached up her steps and along her side deck): E lets it go where she lies (`dropAnchor`, slower than `ANCHOR_SPEED`) or gets it up, and her drift sock in (`Game.updateBow`; `Player.anchorWork` makes the rail's "Jump overboard" make way). Her stowed plow anchor's off her roller while it's down (`BoatModel.setAnchorStowed`: its triangles are last in her fittings, left out by the draw range; her copies for others have a view of their own, `dressCopy`).
- (M16) Each boat has her own: the Danforth above is the lobster boat's `anchor` track.   - The canvas is held to a phone's pixels.
  - The loading frame is 8 × 8 px and draws only the sky, the ground and the sea.
  - The rest (`_late`: the rocks, the village, the stalls and their traders, the lobster boat) waits out of the scene once its geometry is on the GPU (`uploadLate`). Its shaders are made after the start (`lateReady`), then it's revealed a group per frame.
  - The village's textures are baked after the start too, in `lateReady`, a set a frame (`Village`'s `bakeLater`). Their nine programs were seconds of the load there.
- **Shaders compiled late:** every shader compiled before the start is load time, and All the Spaces' checks allow 45 s to `portal.ready()` on a machine much slower than most. Shaders not needed at once are compiled in `prepareLate`, after the start: the rod and its line, the speargun, the lobster gear, the cast net, the rain and the lightning, and the fish in the swimming fish's skin. ((M17) The catch card's fish, `FishPortrait.compile`, from stand-in meshes in the card's scene: hidden, since a card up while they compiled drew them.)
  - three's `compile()` also compiles the materials of hidden objects. So what waits is taken out of the scene, not just hidden (`heldTools` ((M14) with the lost float and its line), `_heldFish`, `_heldNet`, `_heldWeather` (the rain, its splashes, (M11) the drips, the lightning, (M13) the flying debris, (M14) the litter), `_lateHeld`).
  - On two CPU cores, a phone upright is ready in about 7 s (v1.5.0: 10.6 s, when the tools and the late parts were only hidden and so compiled with the rest).
- **Shaders that build quickly:** there a program is built at its first draw, and the time goes with the code the compiler emits. A function called in several places is copied into each, so code repeated for several things is called from one place round a loop: the shore's swells (`shoreTrainAt`: the south's, a westerly's, the north shore's) and the whitecaps' crests and waves (`wcCarrier`, `wcEvent`). M14's third swell and its whitecaps took the sea's first draw from 1.3 to 2.0 s on this machine; the loops brought it back to 1.3 s. Each first draw before ready counts: v1.10.0 failed All the Spaces' checks with a phone upright not ready in 45 s, the first screen and the slowest (every shader made from nothing), and v1.10.1 is ready in 5.5 s against v1.10.0's 7 s in their play here.
- **Shaders first used off the frame:** a program's first use (three looking up its uniforms) waits for the GPU to finish everything queued before it, the program's own linking too. That's seconds there, with the page frozen; a frame that first uses one freezes it too.
  - After the start, `App.useNewPrograms` first waits for a fence (`gpuCaughtUp`, looked at between frames as `pacedFrame` does), so the page keeps answering. Then it uses every new program once, in a tick of `pacedFrame` that draws nothing, so the asking doesn't wait behind a frame.
  - What it readies stays out of the scene, or held back, until then: `readyToDraw` (`readyOffstage` for what's shown already: the wildlife, the debris), the parts in `prepareLate` (all at once, one wait), the plants, Joe and Marta, the records board, the other players' figures.
- **Heavy builds in workers:** the debris, the fish models and the reef's noise are built in workers, so their garbage stays out of script memory.
  - All the Spaces builds inline workers as module workers, which don't start in its sealed frame. `ClassicWorker` starts them as classic workers instead.
- (M17) **What only the build reads, let go as soon as it can be:** there the animals and the debris come minutes after the start, all through the checks' play, and the masks, the plants' records and the village's shapes waited for them (`letGoOfTheBuild`, `releaseMasks`). So once the plants are placed, `App.plantsPlaced` lets go of what only they and the load read: the plants' records but for what the drips, the crabs and the debris read (`Vegetation.slimRecords`), the gully, rubble and scarp masks, and on the CPU the village's part cache. A boat's parts' arrays are detached once they're merged (`GeoKit.merged`), and her lines' section cache cleared once she's built (`loadBoat`). [m17/room.md](m17/room.md) has what each was worth. (M17 step 2) And with the plants, the flats' kept windows (`letGoOfTheFlats`) and the ground's detail texture's data (`releaseDetailData`, uploaded first: 1 MB).
