# M17: the flats, proposed

The flats on the tide: the fish that are already there (bonefish, permit, redfish, snook, tarpon and trout) seen before they're cast to, stalked from the kayak or on foot; a tide that brings them up onto the flats and takes them off; and the moon behind the tide. With it, everything M16 left for guests aboard, coming back where you were, and most of the rest of what the boats ([M16.md](M16.md)) left. Proposed on 2026-11-04 on v2.1.3 (`f88490a`). None of it is built yet. This page has the decisions, what M17 takes from main as it stands, each part, how it would be built, what each part must pass before release, and what's not in it.

## The decisions (the user's, 2026-11-04)

- The headline is the flats: sight-fishing from the kayak and on foot.
- A tide, with the moon behind it: twice a day, springs at new and full moon, the moon's phase in the sky and on the night. Chosen as the most realistic of three (the others were a tide the same every day, and no tide).
- The flats are fished with the rods you have. No fly rod.
- From what M16 left: all of the guests-aboard list; script memory's headroom; coming back; the small things; the looks seen in play; the lobster boat's foredeck and roof; the wakes' far arms; the figures from sources; Pearl's outboard by ear.
- Nothing is to be done on the user's own devices. What only a real device could settle is stood in for here, and what can't be is said ([Devices](#devices)).
- The two faults seen once and never reproduced are tried again early.
- Not M17: the iPhone's status band inside All the Spaces. It's All the Spaces' own `theme-color`, an All the Spaces change.
- Low's script memory stays at 150 MB, since no 2 GB device can be measured.

**What the flats have already.**

- Bonefish drawn tailing on the shallow flats, and fish that notice you by how fast you come (`world/Fish.js`, M7's `WARY`). The speargun's fish are each their own fish; the rod's are not yet.
- Every flats fish in the fish table, with its model and skin: bonefish, permit and tarpon (all let go), redfish, snook and trout (`FishTable.js`).
- The polarized glasses (`game/rigs/Glasses.js`): how much of the sea's reflection is left through them by the angle you look down, and fish shown as one instanced mesh with a little program of its own, made the first time it's needed, so nobody without them pays for it. They show fish only under the far water's weed, debris and bait.
- The kayak's water, "the flats and the sheltered shallows" (`Seaworthy.ABLE.kayak`), the west's mangroves and their creek (M15), and the clouds' shadows on the sea.
- A sea level that moves. M13's surge made it a uniform (`G.seaLevel`, read by 48 files), and the surge's shore field is solved at its own level and blended in as the sea rises (`ShoreWaves`, `surgeMix`).
- The island at 24 degrees north (`Sky.sunDirectionFromTime`), the latitude of the Florida Keys and the Bahamas' flats.

**What it hasn't.** No tide, and no moon phase (the sky has a moon light, not a moon that waxes). No seen fish that bites: a redfish is caught by the bites' habitat roll, as everything else is.

## The tide and the moon

**The tide.** The sea's level rises and falls twice a day.
- **Its size, to be sourced first.** The nearest published tidal datums in the Bahamas' water are Settlement Point's on Grand Bahama (NOAA 9710441: mean higher high water 0.93 m over mean lower low water). The island's own is to be set from the sources in step 0. The flats are a foot or two deep, so a tide that size uncovers some and floods others.
- **Its shape.** Semi-diurnal: highs about 12 h 25 min apart, coming about 50 minutes later each day, with springs at new and full moon and neaps at the quarters (the M2 and S2 tides). A mixed day's inequality goes in too, if the source has one.
- **At the clock's pace** (`DAY_RATE`, 100 s of play an island hour by day): a flood is about ten minutes of play.
- **The same for everyone**, worked out from the island's clock as the weather is. Resting, sleeping aboard and the tow's jump move it with the clock.
- **With a surge.** A tide and a surge add up, so a hurricane at spring high water is the worst of it.

**What follows it.**
- `G.seaLevel` is the tide plus the surge.
- The shore field is solved at low water and at high water and blended by the tide, as the surge's field is now. On high it shares the surge's texture slot, since the sea's at 16 of 16. The second level is solved after the start, never before ready.
- The CPU's water query (`WaterQuery`) follows the same level, so the boat you're in, the boats at their berths, swimmers, the bobber and the line all ride it.

**What it changes, seen.**
- The flats drying at a spring low: sand and grass bared, water lying in the hollows.
- The mangroves' roots out of the water.
- A wet band on the pier's piles and the rocks.
- The beach's wet sand, and how far the swash runs up.
- The sea caves' inlets.
- The boats riding up and down at their berths, and the water's edge coming to the kayak on the sand and going from her.
- The creek running out on the ebb and in on the flood, a current you paddle against.

**What it changes, fished.**
- The bites go by the tide's stage: the flats on the flood and at high water, the edges and channels on the ebb, the creek's mouth on the ebb, the pier at the turn.
- Joe's slate and the chart give the day's tides, and the Guide teaches them.

**The moon.**
- Its phase follows the island's day: 29.53 days a cycle, about 13 hours of play.
- The disc is lit to its phase, and the night's light goes with it: a full moon on the sea and the sand, a dark night at new moon.
- What M16 left that needs a moon:
  - the turtles minding it (a full moon lessens what a light does to hatchlings, [north.md](m16/north.md));
  - swordfish going deeper under a full moon ([fish.md](m16/fish.md)).
- The springs follow it.

**Not proposed: seasons.** At the clock's pace a year is about 150 hours of play, so a season would hardly turn in a player's whole climb. The traps' closed season stays out for the same reason.

## Sight-fishing on the flats

**Where.**
- The flats and sheltered shallows by the village, the kayak's water.
- The west's mangroves, along their edge and in their creek.
- The north beach's trough, for snook.

Step 0 maps where the island's flats lie under a foot or two of water at each stage of the tide, from `TerrainData`, in Node.

**The fish, each as it's seen.** Modelled after photographs and video of each, as the boats were. Their numbers and habits come from the sources, not from memory:

- **Bonefish:** in schools, tailing head-down as they feed, cruising as nervous water or a shadow on the sand, mudding over the soft bottom. Telemetry at Cape Eleuthera found them coming onto the flats with the high tide and going back to deeper water at low tide (step 0 cites it).
- **Permit:** singly or a few, a black sickle of tail and fin. The hardest to fool.
- **Redfish:** tailing and pushing water along the mangroves' edge. The easiest.
- **Snook:** lying along the mangroves' edge and in the beach's trough.
- **Tarpon:** rolling in the channels, and laid up at dawn.
- **Trout:** over the grass.

**What you see them by.**
- The sun high and behind you. A cloud's shadow over the water hides them; glassy water shows the tails, and rain shows nothing.
- The glasses take out the glare. Their reflection-by-angle is extended to the flats' shallow, bright bottom, and the fish's own shadow on the sand shows.
- Your eye's height:
  - sitting in the kayak, 0.81 m;
  - standing in her on calm water, if her kind can be stood in (step 0's sources, against her stiffness since v2.1.0);
  - wading;
  - on the lobster boat's roof ([The boats](#the-boats));
  - on the console's bow.

**Stalking.**
- Fish notice you as the speargun's do, by how fast you come.
- On the flats they also notice your shadow over them, a paddle knocking the hull, the push of water ahead of the kayak, a lure landing on their heads and the line over their backs.
- A spooked school bolts, its wake seen, and the flat goes quiet a while.
- Wading slowly keeps them unspooked. The game wades already (M14).

**The cast.**
- You cast to a fish you've seen, with the rods you have: shrimp, jigs and plugs, put ahead of the fish on its way.
- That fish is the one that takes. It turns to the lure, follows, eats, refuses or spooks, by what it is and what it's shown.
- This is a seen fish's take beside the bites' roll, as the speargun's fish are their own. It's the heart of the part.

**The record.**
- A grand slam is a bonefish, a permit and a tarpon in one day. All three are let go, so it's kept as a trophy (`Trophies.js`, as the billfish are) and asked for in an order.
- The Guide's tips for the flats, and Joe's hints by the tide.

**Together.** A guest sees the same fish: each school is placed from the clock, the tide and its patch of flat, as the far water's finds are (`Finds.js`). Only what changes needs sending: a school spooked, a fish hooked.

## Aboard together

All of what a second player sees or can do on someone else's boat ([together.md](m16/together.md)):

6. **Spare berths.** One for a visitor's kayak on the sand, more than four along the stem, and a boat leaving the stem not passing through the pier head (`together/Berths.js`).

8. **A speck's height is her fit's**, so a sportfisher with no tower doesn't show past the fog as if she had one (`Specks`).
9. **The horn** is heard from the boat it's sounded on, a rider's too.
10. **The console's ladder on a copy** folds down as on her owner's game. That's one more bit in the fit, and a guest's walking colliders are keyed on the fit, so it's done with care (`ConsoleModel.dressCopy`).
11. **Boarding from the water** by her ladder, at its own reach, told as "Climb the ladder" (`Together`'s 4.2 m).
12. **Standing beside the chair** isn't sitting in it (`Crew.js`: by the seat, not by nearness).
13. **A slow game in company.** Under 10 fps a boat is worked out in steps short enough to keep up with the water. It's not stopped at 0.1 s while the waves go by the wall clock.

## Coming back

Reloading and leaving ([ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md), Picking up where you left off):

- **Where you stood.** The save keeps where on her you were (the deck, the bridge, the tower, the cabin, the chair), and you're put back there, not in the cockpit (`App.pickUp`, `boatLeft`).
- **In the water far out.** You're back in the water beside her if she's still there, or ashore if she isn't, instead of always ashore.
- **The kayak at anchor with you in her** keeps her anchor down. Being put back in her seat isn't taking the helm (`Game.updateAnchor`).
- **A turned-back turtle's night** is saved, so her tracks the next morning are hers and not the clock's ([north.md](m16/north.md)).

## The boats

- **The lobster boat's foredeck and roof.** A way up, a hatch or steps, whichever her kind has in photographs of real ones. And reasons to go: her anchor handled from the bow, and her roof as somewhere to look from, over the flats' edges and the reef.
- **The wakes' far arms as real waves.** A boat's wake is foam only now ([console.md](m16/console.md)). The far arms become smooth crests on the sea's surface:
  - worked out from her track (her recent positions and speeds as uniforms, not a texture: the sea has none left);
  - in the CPU's query too, so a kayak rocks in a passing console's wake and the bobber rides it;
  - the foam stays as it is.
  - It's the costliest look here, because the sea's shader is built in the loading frame: kept out of the CPU path's lean sea if the ready A/B wants it, as the weedlines are.
- **The small things.**
  - A twin console burns as twins do (`Seaworthy.ABLE` by her fit).
  - A key for the plotter's range, and a place for it in the actions wheel.
  - A toast when a sea comes aboard.
  - Trap bait bought from Marta and used up in a soak ([traps.md](m16/traps.md)).

## Script memory's headroom

Low's peak is 146.1 to 148.8 of 150 MB while live memory is 75 to 90, so the peak is mostly garbage waiting:
- the plants placed after the start (`Scatter`);
- a boat built (15 to 20 MB of working arrays);
- the shore fields solved as the weather turns.

Less of that, or less held (the island's masks once the plants are placed, the shore field's CPU copy), is what gives room. The tide holds a second shore field and the flats' fish add a little, so room comes first: the peak at least 10 MB under 150 on two complete checks before the tide goes in.

The download is near its limit too: what M17 adds is to stay under 1 MB in all. Anything bigger needs room found first.

## How it would be built

As M16 was: one foundation, then streams in parallel, each in its own git worktree and branch, merged one at a time into `m17-flats` (cut from main when the work starts), then a second wave, then a final pass alone and with two players.

**0. Before any code** (all of it at once, nothing merged):
- the sources (the tide's range and its constituents for the island's water, the flats fish's habits, whether her kind of kayak can be stood in, the figures list);
- photographs and video of each fish on the flats;
- the looks seen in play run again, and the two faults tried again;
- the flats mapped at each stage of the tide.

**1. Room** (serial, on `m17-flats`): script memory's peak down, as above.

**2. The foundation** (serial, after 1):
- The tide and the moon as numbers (`IslandClock`: the tide's height and the moon's phase at an island time, the same on every game).
- `G.seaLevel` from them with the surge, the shore field at its two levels in the surge's slot, and the CPU's query.
- The boats at their berths and the kayak on the sand riding it.
- The flats' fish as data: a `Flats` module saying where each school is by the clock and the tide, and the events that change them (spooked, hooked).

It must pass the release checks below before any stream starts, because everything after it stands on it.

**3. The streams** (in parallel, after 2):

| Stream | What | Mostly in |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sight-fishing: the fish seen, stalked and cast to; the grand slam | `world/Fish.js`, `game/rigs/Glasses.js`, a seen fish's take in `Bites` and `Game`, the flats fish in `FishTable.js`, `Trophies.js`, `Guide.js` |
| 2 | The tide seen: the flats drying, the roots, the wet bands, the swash, the caves' inlets, the creek's current | the ground's and rocks' materials, `world/shore/`, the creek in `Bites` |
| 3 | The moon: its phase, its light, the turtles minding it and their night saved, swordfish under it | `sky/`, `Turtles.js`, `NestingTurtles.js`, the swordfish in `FishTable.js` |
| 4 | Aboard together: the whole guest list | `together/`, `ConsoleModel.dressCopy`, `Crew.js`, `Berths.js`, the yard's cradle, the shell tray |
| 5 | Coming back, the boats and the small things: where you stood, swimming far out, the kayak's anchor; the lobster boat's foredeck and roof; the twin console's burn, the plotter's range key, the sea-aboard toast, trap bait | `App.pickUp` and the save, `Game.updateAnchor`, `BoatModel`, `Seaworthy.js`, `ui/Actions.js`, the plotter, `BreakingSeas.js`, `TrapWork.js` |

Rules between them:
- **Only stream 6 changes the sea's shader.** It's at 16 of 16 textures and most of the ready time, so anything else the sea needs goes through it or waits.
- **One stream per file** for the turtles (3) and the save (5).
- **`FishTable.js`** has 1's flats fish and 3's swordfish, different entries.
- **`App.js`** is touched by most streams, so they're merged one at a time.

M16's ten at once used up the five-hour limit in about four hours and the week's soon after, so three or four run at once at most, with the usage watched. Sessions start only on the user's word.

**4. The second wave** (needs the first):

- The flats' sounds. Needs 1.
