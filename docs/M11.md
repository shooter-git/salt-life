# M11: weather, for consideration

M8 brought the weather's core, M9 its rain and thunderstorms, and M10 what it costs ([ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md#what-the-weather-costs)): the boat's hull, bilge and engine, Marta's repair and pump, the cast, the float, the cast net and the fight in a blow, murky water after one, the breakers and their bait following the surf, the rain's splashes, the lightning in the sea's reflections, the shutters and the debris in a blow, and the weather in the checks. Everything else talked about for the weather is here, to be weighed and chosen from. None of it is promised.

## Rain

- Curtains of rain in the distance (high).
- Drips (off the roofs, the wheelhouse, the palms).
- The rain on the pier, the boardwalk and the roofs: nothing gives the drops or the splashes their height, so the drops fall through them and nothing splashes on them (`Rain.js`).
- The rain's sound on each surface: on the water, on the pier's wood, on tin roofs (the rain's beds now are a shower, a downpour and the boat's deck: `tools/audio/build-weather.mjs`). It should sound different under a roof, as footsteps do on each surface.

## Thunder and lightning

- Lightning striking the sea near a boat (and the boat's crew feeling it).

## Storm skies and the wind

- Cumulonimbus: towers of their own in a thunderstorm (rain's deck is nimbostratus now: lower, smoother, darker).
- The clouds aloft turning with the weather too (they keep to the trades' direction: their evolution runs along it, and the cirrus's angle is compiled in, `Clouds.js`).
- The sea's reflection of the sky: three sets the water's `envMapIntensity` to `scene.environmentIntensity` (about 0.05 to 0.09), not the 12 `WaterSurface` asks for, because the water has no `envMap` of its own. The sea's look was tuned with it as it is. Either the code says what it does, or the reflection comes up to what was meant and the sea's tuned again.

## The sea and the surf

- The swell from another direction: two or three shore fields, baked when the island's built rather than at load (each is a fast-marching solve on the CPU, and the load on a device without a graphics card is tight).
- The open sea's crests breaking, and spray torn off them.
- Spray from whitecaps and breakers (Tidewater's emitters on the GPU weren't ported).
- The sea's culling box following the sea state (`water.lod.maxY`), if a bigger sea ever needs it.
- The surf zone's milky water in a big surf (`ShoreWaves`' `turbidity` is declared but never reaches the shader).

## Consequences (mild: a cost, never a game over)

- Big surf: rip currents, being tumbled.
- Gear lost: a snapped line's float and hook (tackle isn't an item, and charging for every snap would change a fair day).
- The hull as others hear it: the owner's bilge isn't sent, so whoever rides along hears no slosh, pump or creaks.
- Fish noticing a diver later in murky water (now it only closes in what you see).
- The village: shutters that swing and bang where you see them (they're merged into the houses: only their sound bangs now), and loose things blown about (the beach's debris is merged and still).

## The hurricane

- A storm surge (`G.seaLevel` raised: the shore waves already follow the depth, and the shaders read it live; the shore field's depths are baked at its usual level), and the sea over the beach.
- Surf past M8's cap, with damage to the pier and the dock.
- Rain driven sideways, and less than 100 m seen.
- Things flying.
- The palms bent as far as they go.
- Buildings damaged.
- An evacuation, as an event: a rare one, the same for everyone (the weather comes from the clock), and never on a new player's first days.

## The checks

- Their frame time isn't judged: on their machine `pacedFrame` holds the frames to the display's, and the SDK's `perf` messages don't reach the report (All the Spaces' side).
