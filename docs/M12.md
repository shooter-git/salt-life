# M12: the weather and the sea, for consideration

M8 brought the weather's core, M9 its rain and thunderstorms, M10 what it costs, and M11 the rain where you stand ([ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md#what-the-weather-costs)): what covers the island and the rain stopping, splashing and drying under it, the drips, the rain heard on what it falls on, the showers' curtains in the distance, the sea mirroring the sky, the milkier surf, the fish in the murk, the hull heard riding along, and the boat's instruments. Everything else talked about is here, to be weighed and chosen from. None of it is promised.

## Storms from other directions

- Each blow its own track: most still down the trades (from the north-northwest), some from the south or the west, and now and then a front that swings the wind round as it passes (south, west, north). The wind, the rain's slant, the front's bank on the horizon (`SkyProClouds.front`, read up the trades now), the showers' curtains (`Weather.showers`, laid along the trades now) and where the lightning strikes (`Weather.strikeIn`, toward the trades) all follow it. It's mostly `Weather.js`, so it's tested under Node.
  - As it is, the showers come over the island: from the pier and the village the mountain hides one on its way, and you see it as it goes out to sea. A shower from the south would be seen coming.
- The clouds aloft turning with the weather too (they keep to the trades' direction: their evolution runs along it, and the cirrus's angle is compiled in, `Clouds.js`).

## Rain

- The rain on a tin roof heard from beneath it: under the boathouse, Joe's roof and the stilt huts the rain's muffled now, with no drumming over you (none of the recordings auditioned for M11 was kept).
- The rocks and the moored lobster boat aren't in the cover map (`Cover.js`): the rain doesn't splash on them, and it isn't dry under them.

## Thunder and lightning

- Lightning striking the sea near a boat (and the boat's crew feeling it). The strikes can't aim at a boat, and near ones are rare: it would need the host to place them.

## Storm skies

- Cumulonimbus: towers of their own in a thunderstorm (rain's deck is nimbostratus now: lower, smoother, darker).

## The sea and the surf

- The swell from another direction: two or three shore fields, baked when the island's built rather than at load (each is a fast-marching solve on the CPU, and the load on a device without a graphics card is tight). It goes with storms from other directions.
- The open sea's crests breaking, and spray torn off them.
- Spray from whitecaps and breakers (Tidewater's emitters on the GPU weren't ported).
- The sea's culling box following the sea state (`water.lod.maxY`), if a bigger sea ever needs it.

## Consequences (mild: a cost, never a game over)

- Big surf: rip currents, being tumbled.
- Gear lost: a snapped line's float and hook (tackle isn't an item, and charging for every snap would change a fair day).
- The village: shutters that swing and bang where you see them (they're merged into the houses: only their sound bangs now), and loose things blown about (the beach's debris is merged and still).

## The boat's instruments

- Riding along, the skipper's instruments: their boat's dash is on standby now (the readings drawn are yours). Their revs are sent already; the depth and the chart are where you are.
- The radar: her open array turns, but no screen shows it (the centre screen is the sounder). The island's coast and the other players' boats on a third screen, or a split one.

## The hurricane

- A storm surge (`G.seaLevel` raised: the shore waves already follow the depth, and the shaders read it live; the shore field's depths are baked at its usual level), and the sea over the beach.
- Surf past M8's cap, with damage to the pier and the dock.
- Rain driven sideways, and less than 100 m seen.
- Things flying.
- The palms bent as far as they go.
- Buildings damaged.
- An evacuation, as an event: a rare one, the same for everyone (the weather comes from the clock), and never on a new player's first days.

## The checks

- Their frame time isn't judged: on their machine `pacedFrame` holds the frames to the display's, and the SDK's `perf` messages don't reach the report (All the Spaces' side).
