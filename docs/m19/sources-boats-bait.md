## 1. The kayak's pedal drive: the Perception Pescador Pilot 12

**The game now.**
- `docs/m16/kayak.md:7`: she's a Pescador 12.0: 12 ft 0, 32.5 in, 375 lb capacity, about 64 lb.
- `docs/m16/kayak.md:20-24`: paddled at 2.3, 3.1 and 4.0 kn (hull speed 4.4).
- `docs/m16/kayak.md:35`: the seated eye is 0.81 m off the water.
- `docs/m16/kayak.md:41` and `docs/m19/before.md:15`: with the rod out the paddle's laid by and she drifts.
- `docs/m17/before.md:64`: "The kayak can't be stood in."
- `src/game/Gear.js:185-207`: her tracks (crate, paddle, anchor trolley, finder, hatch). No drive.

**Sources.**
- **The maker.** Confluence Outdoor, Perception's US page for the Pescador Pilot 12.0 ([confluenceoutdoor.com](https://www.confluenceoutdoor.com/en-us/products/pescador-pilot-120-fishing-kayak/9351587042)). **It wouldn't open**: HTTP 429 on every try over about two hours, and the old perceptionkayaks.com links redirect to it. (Snippet only:) 149 in long, 33.5 in wide, 93 lb, 475 lb capacity. I haven't seen what it says about standing.

  - 12 ft 5 in, 33.75 in wide, 16 in deep, 85 lb, 475 lb capacity.
  - Rotating pedals drive her forward and back. The drive lifts up to the hull for shallow water, and he warns the prop can strike bottom or catch weed.
  - Retractable rudder, steered by hand.

- **Paddlesesh**, 2026, no author named ([paddlesesh.com](https://paddlesesh.com/perception-pescador-pilot-review/)).

  - "I can now stand and cast."
  - The drive is raised by pulling pins; he calls it a pain to retract in murky water. He finds it quiet.
  - A commenter (May 2020) folds the drive up against the hull and gets about 4 in (10 cm) of draft.
- **Perception's pedal-drive quick start guide**, on a third-party mirror ([manualedistruzioni.it](https://manualedistruzioni.it/perception/dc-drive/pedal-drive/user-manual/1g3zp)). **It wouldn't open** (403). (Snippet only:) the deployed drive needs approx. 18 in of water. It's stowed by setting a crank arm vertical, pulling the quick key, and raising the drive through the scupper.

- **Speeds.**
  - An owner's review on paddling.com is behind a bot paywall, so I didn't open it. (Snippet only:) about 3.5 mph cruising "with a moderate effort", and "a little over 4 mph" pushing.
  - Reelyaks ([reelyaks.com](https://reelyaks.com/blogs/news/pedal-vs-paddle-kayak-speed-tested), snippet only): pedals 20 to 30% faster than paddling in the same hull. It gives no method or boat.
  - I found no controlled pedal-against-paddle test of any kayak.
- **Kick-up.** None of these sources mentions the Pilot's drive kicking up when it strikes. Every one describes raising it by hand.

**Derived.**
- Beam: 33.5 to 33.75 in is 0.851 to 0.857 m, 2.5 to 3.2 cm (1 to 1¼ in) wider than the 12.0's 0.826 m.
- Mass: 85 to 93 lb is 38.6 to 42.2 kg, against the 12.0's 29 kg.
- Speeds: 3.5 mph is 3.0 kn (1.56 m/s); 4.2 mph is 3.6 kn (1.88 m/s).
- Hull speed is 1.34 √(waterline in ft) kn. She's 12 ft 5 in (3.78 m) overall; taking a waterline of about 11.5 ft (3.5 m, judgement), that's 4.5 kn. So the owner's 3.6 kn flat out is 0.8 of hull speed, where the game's paddler is at 0.7 at 3.1 kn.
- Reelyaks' 20 to 30% on the game's 3.1 kn would give 3.7 to 4.0 kn. That's faster than the owner reports, so I'd go by the owner.

**For the game.**
- **Her figures:** beam 0.85 m (33.5 in, the maker's, snippet only); 40 kg (88 lb, between 85 and 93; judgement); capacity 215 kg (475 lb).
- **Pedalling:** 3.0 kn (1.56 m/s) cruising, 3.6 kn (1.88 m/s) flat out, and reverse. Hands free: W and S drive her with the rod out, as `docs/m19/before.md:15` plans.
- **Paddling her:** the game's 2.3, 3.1 and 4.0 kn, less about 5% for a heavier, wider hull (judgement).
- **The drive:** down, it needs 0.46 m (18 in) of water; raised, she draws about 0.10 m (4 in). There's no kick-up, so with the drive down on a shoal she stops; whether it's damaged is judgement.
- **Standing:** owners and reviewers stand in her; the maker's rating is not seen. Allow it, less stable than sitting, and calm water only (judgement). Standing, the eye is about 1.7 m (5 ft 7 in) off the water: about 0.15 m of deck plus an adult's 1.55 m eye height (judgement).
- **Real price:** $1,500 to $1,800, against roughly $600 to $700 for the 12.0 (the 12.0's price is judgement; I didn't look it up).

## 2. A push and stake-out pole

**The game now.** `src/game/Gear.js:194-197`: an anchor trolley and a 3 lb grapnel (`anchorM: 10`). There is no pole.

**Sources.**
- **YakAttack ParkNPole 7'8"** ([yakattack.us](https://www.yakattack.us/parknpole-78)).
  - 7 ft 8 in (2.34 m) of 0.845 in (21.5 mm) fibreglass tube.
  - A nylon foot for pushing, a tip for staking, and a floating base.
  - $102, out of stock. "Used as a stakeout/push pole for kayaking".
  - (Snippet only:) it replaced the 8 ft model to suit UPS's shipping limits.
- **YakAttack ParkNPole Link**, at TackleDirect ([tackledirect.com](https://www.tackledirect.com/yakattack-pnpl-8-parknpole-link-stakeout-pole.html)): two pieces, 8 ft (2.44 m), 1.8 lb (0.82 kg), $129.95. A 46 in (1.17 m) extension, sold separately, makes it 12 ft (3.66 m).
- **Salt Strong**, Tony Acevedo, 26 June 2020 ([saltstrong.com](https://www.saltstrong.com/?p=32747)).
  - He poles standing, with the Link at 12 ft for the longer push.
  - Asked about poling seated: "You would be better off just using the paddle if sitting."
  - Keep the pole off the hull: the knock spooks fish.
- **Salt Water Sportsman**, Capt. Dave Lear, updated 23 Oct 2019 ([saltwatersportsman.com](https://saltwatersportsman.com/?p=53561)).
  - The rule of thumb is the boat's length plus 3 ft.
  - "18 to 20 feet is the minimum recommended length" from a skiff's platform in under 5 ft of water.
  - He gives nothing for kayaks.
- **No source gives a stake-out depth** for the ParkNPole.

**Derived** (assumptions are judgement).
- **Staking.** The pole goes about 0.3 m into sand, and about 0.45 m stands from the waterline to its holder and the hand. So the depth it stakes in is the pole less 0.75 m: 1.6 m (5 ft 3 in) for the 7'8" or 8 ft, and 2.9 m (9 ft 6 in) for the 12 ft.
- **Lear's rule** asks 15 ft 5 in (4.7 m) for a 12 ft 5 in kayak. The 12 ft Link is the longest kayak pole found.
- **Poling reach.**
  - Seated: hands about 0.5 m off the water, an 8 ft pole held 0.3 m from its top and leaning 45°. It reaches the bottom at (2.44 − 0.3) × 0.71 − 0.5 ≈ 1.0 m.
  - Standing: hands about 1.3 m up, the 12 ft pole leaning 30°. It reaches (3.66 − 0.3) × 0.87 − 1.3 ≈ 1.6 m.

**For the game.**
- The pole: 2.44 m (8 ft) in two pieces, 3.66 m (12 ft) with the extension.
- It stakes her out in up to 1.6 m (8 ft pole) or 2.9 m (12 ft).
- Poling is standing only, so it goes with the Pilot, in up to about 1.6 m. Seated, it pushes off the bottom in under about 1 m, and the paddle's better.
- Staking is quieter than the grapnel's splash and drag (judgement).
- Real price: $102 to $130; the extension's price wasn't found.

## 3. A bow-mount trolling motor for the console

**The game now.**
- `docs/m16/console.md:13`: the Regulator 23 at 7.14 m, 2.5 t displaced.
- `docs/m16/console.md:17`: the outboards' thrust is "capped by the bollard pull and gone at the prop's pitch speed".
- `src/game/Gear.js:208-227`: her tracks. There's no trolling motor.
- `docs/m17/sources-tide-fish.md:232`: guides list a trolling motor among the things that spook fish on the flats.

**Sources.**
- **Minn Kota's selection chart**, Rev. 8.21.2020 ([PDF](https://minnkota.johnsonoutdoors.com/sites/default/files/2023-02/min_productmanual_motor-select-guide.pdf)).
  - "You need at least 2 pounds of thrust for every 100 pounds of fully loaded boat weight".
  - Its table: 3,000 to 3,500 lb (23 ft) takes 70 lb at 24 V; 4,000 lb (25 ft) takes 80 lb at 24 V; 4,500 lb and up (25 ft and over) takes 101 to 112 lb at 36 V.
  - Bow mounts by height of bow over the water: 28 to 44 in takes a 54 to 72 in shaft; 45 in and up, 87 in.
  - The prop tip should be at least 12 in (0.3 m) under.
- **Minn Kota's blog**, 8 Jan 2021 ([minnkota.johnsonoutdoors.com](https://minnkota.johnsonoutdoors.com/us/blog/how-to-select-trolling-motor-thrust)): 112 to 115 lb of thrust runs on 36 V. "thrust does not mean speed".
- **Overton's**, Minn Kota Riptide Terrova 112 ([overtons.com](https://www.overtons.com/minn-kota-riptide-terrova-112-saltwater-trolling-motor-36v-112-lb.-87-shaft-370959.html)): 112 lb, 36 V, 87 in shaft, 52 A at most, Spot-Lock (GPS station-holding), $3,949.99.
- **Top speed.**
  - (Snippet only:) a Minn Kota FAQ, copied on a retailer's page ([kayaksandpaddles.co.uk](https://www.kayaksandpaddles.co.uk/canoe/kayak/uk/shop/productpages/minn-kota/minn-kota-frequently-asked-questions.htm)), says top speed "is about 5 mph"; small boats reach it with most motors, large ones only with the biggest. The page as I fetched it showed only a product listing, so this is unverified.
  - Minn Kota's spec pages give speed only as "Variable".
- **Noise.**
  - Lenny Rudow, PropTalk, 24 Apr 2015 (from BoatU.S. Magazine; [proptalk.com](https://proptalk.com/does-your-boat-freak-fish)). Under water the loudest sound of most engines, "electric trolling motors included — is prop noise, directly related to prop speed". Two-strokes in neutral are the worst.
  - Acoustics Today, Summer 2020, p. 92 ([acousticstoday.org](https://acousticstoday.org/issues/2020AT/Summer2020/files/basic-html/page92.html)). Rudow's hydrophone, for *Boating*, found a push pole crunching sand and shell "notably louder than the propeller noise created by an electric motor" at the same speed.
  - Neither gives decibels.

**Derived.**
- **Thrust.** 2.5 t is 5,512 lb; at 2 lb per 100 lb that's 110 lb, so the 112 lb, 36 V class (three 12 V batteries). 112 lb is 498 N of bollard pull.
- **Speed.** 5 mph is 4.3 kn (2.2 m/s). As a check:
  - At 4 kn (2.06 m/s) her friction drag is about 100 N. That assumes 13 m² wetted (judgement), ITTC Cf 0.0030 at Re 1.1 × 10⁷, and a form factor of 1.2.
  - At Froude 0.26 the wave-making is of the same order, so about 200 to 250 N in all.
  - The motor draws at most 36 V × 52 A = 1,870 W. If a quarter to a third of that reaches the water (judgement), 500 W at 2 m/s is 250 N of thrust.
  - So about 4 kn, which agrees with the 5 mph ceiling.
- **Battery.** On three 100 Ah batteries (judgement) flat out at 52 A: 100 Ah ÷ 52 A ≈ 1.9 h. At a third of full draw, about 6 h. Both ignore Peukert's loss.

**For the game.**
- 112 lb (498 N) bollard pull at 36 V, a 72 in shaft (judgement: the Regulator's bow is in the chart's 28 to 44 in band), and a top speed of 4 kn.
- Its thrust can be modelled as the outboards' is: 498 N bollard, gone by about 2.3 m/s (judgement).
- It runs 1.9 h flat out and about 6 h at a third (derived, on judgement batteries).
- Spot-Lock holds her on a GPS point (the Riptide Terrova has it).
- Noise: at low speed fish notice it about as far as they notice the push pole, less far than the outboards, and further as the prop speeds up (judgement; the sources rank it but give no numbers).
- Real price: $3,950 for the 87 in shaft; the 72 in's price wasn't seen.

## 8. Cut conch as bait, and the queen conch's rules

**The game now.**
- `src/game/Shells.js:11`: the queen conch is a beachcombing keepsake. Its note says trade in its shell needs a CITES permit.
- Conch isn't a bait anywhere.

**Sources.**
- **CITES.** NOAA's archived queen conch page ([UNT web archive](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090508115507/http://www.nmfs.noaa.gov/pr/species/invertebrates/queenconch.htm), snippet only): the US proposed it for Appendix II in 1992 and it was adopted, "the first large-scale fisheries product" regulated by CITES.
- **The flared lip.**
  - The Fisheries Resources (Jurisdiction and Conservation) Regulations, s. 27, as a petition quotes it ([change.org](https://www.change.org/p/minister-of-agriculture-and-marine-resources-protect-baby-conch-in-the-bahamas), snippet only). No one may take, possess or sell a conch whose shell lacks "a well formed flaring lip", or sell such a shell.
  - The FAO's country profile (May 2009, [PDF](https://www.fao.org/fishery/docs/DOCUMENT/fcp/en/FI_CP_BS.pdf)) and Friends of the Environment's Abaco brochure (below) say the same.
- **No closed season; export.** The Tribune, 12 May 2023 ([tribune242.com](https://www.tribune242.com/news/2023/may/12/no-closure-queen-conch-fishery-bahamas)).
  - The Department of Marine Resources (DMR): "there is no closure on the Queen Conch Fishery in The Bahamas".
  - Commercial export has been banned since January 2022.
  - Under consideration: a 14 mm minimum lip, landing conch in the shell, a closed season, and taking conch out of the bag limits.
  - I found no later change, to November 2026.
  - Researchers and the Perry Institute urge 15 mm ([perryinstitute.org](https://perryinstitute.org/?p=9272), snippet only).
- **Taking it out of the country.** The Ministry of Foreign Affairs, 1 May 2017 ([mofa.gov.bs](https://mofa.gov.bs/conch-exportation-bahamas/)): up to 10 lb (4.5 kg) of conch meat in personal baggage with no permit.
- **Visitors' limit.** DMR's excerpt of the regulations, 3 January 2008 (reg. 48(1)(f), [PDF](https://rwbimini.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/SportsfishingRegulations.pdf)): no vessel sportfishing may have aboard "conchs, exceeding six". Friends of the Environment and bahamas.com agree (sections 10 and 11).
- **Not by scuba or compressor.** The FAO profile (2009): "SCUBA diving for any fisheries resource is prohibited". Compressors need a permit.
- **As bait.**
  - Robin Smillie, Florida Sportsman, 16 May 2011, at Chub Cay ([floridasportsman.com](https://floridasportsman.com/editorial/regions_tropics_chub_cay_bahamas_family_islands/401521)). Men cleaning conch put the innards (the slop) over the side, and bonefish follow it into the harbour. The guide: "If they come within 20 feet of conch slop or crab, they won't pass it up."
  - Poveromo, 2022 (section 7): "fresh conch is a killer mutton bait", in strips or chunks bought from local divers, and its scraps in the chum.
  - Fishmasters, 2022: cut conch is the best bonefish bait in the Bahamas, but harder to get because its harvest is controlled.
  - Conner, 2023: conch slop from a conch salad shack as bonefish chum.
- I found **no rule against using conch as bait**.

**Derived.** None worth making. I found no figure for the meat a queen conch gives, or its price at the dock.

**For the game.**
- Cut conch is legal bait, from a legal conch: one with a flared lip.
- A visitor has at most 6 aboard at any time. It can't be taken by scuba. There's no closed season.
- It takes bonefish (as bait, and its slop as chum) and mutton snapper, and does as chum for both.
- A conch cuts into 6 to 8 chunks (judgement).
- Shells: inside the Bahamas a flared-lip shell can be sold (s. 27(2) bans selling only those without one). CITES governs its export, which is what `Shells.js:11`'s note means. The note stands as a keepsake rule.

## 9. Hermit crabs as bait

## 10. The Bahamas' crawfish (spiny lobster) rules

**The game now.**
- `src/game/FishTable.js:138`: the lobster, `minCm: 22.4`, `bag: 6`. `FishTable.js:216-218`: the gauge's 3 in (7.62 cm) of carapace at `CARAPACE = 0.34` of total length.
- `docs/m16/traps.md:11`: "the gauge's 3 in of carapace, berried females back, the day's bag of 6": Florida's rules.
- `docs/m16/traps.md:40`: "there's no closed season: the island's clock has no months". `src/game/Turtles.js:9`: "The island has no seasons".
- `src/game/Gear.js:169-173`: traps sold 2, 4 and 8, aboard the lobster boat.
- `docs/m16/traps.md:5` and its table: the trap is a Barbados chicken-wire trap on a frame of poles, 37 × 22 × 15 in.
- `src/game/FishTable.js:138`: `price: 24`. Lobster is sold to Marta.

**Sources.**
- **DMR's excerpt of the Fisheries Resources (Jurisdiction and Conservation) Regulations**, 3 January 2008, with the amendments of 19 October 2007 ([PDF, via rwbimini.com](https://rwbimini.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/SportsfishingRegulations.pdf)):
  - Reg. 48(1)(f): no vessel sportfishing shall have on board "crawfish, exceeding ten". The limit is per vessel, aboard at any time; there's no per-person or per-day figure.
  - Reg. 48(1)(b): a sportfisher shall not use "a spear, a fish trap, or a net other than a landing net" unless the permit allows it.
  - Reg. 48(2): the limits also apply to a Bahamian vessel fishing non-commercially with non-Bahamians fishing.
  - Reg. 48(4): permanent residents are treated as Bahamians. Reg. 46(4) defines sportsfishing as foreign fishing for sport.
  - Reg. 70(2): no one takes more than 10 lb of conch or crawfish out of the country.
- **The government's notice**, 24 July 2024 ([bahamastradeinfo.gov.bs](https://www.bahamastradeinfo.gov.bs/?p=358959)):
  - closed 1 April to 31 July, the peak breeding time; open 1 August to 31 March;
  - a 3¼ in carapace whole, a 5½ in tail separated;
  - "The catching, selling or purchasing of undersize or berried crawfish is illegal.";
  - a permit is needed for crawfish traps ($50) or an air compressor ($30), and a gauge must be carried;
  - commercial vessels of 15 ft and over are licensed ($100 to $250); only vessels owned by resident Bahamian citizens may fish commercially.
- **The Marine Products (Fisheries) (Amendment) Rules 1976**, rule 10A ([FAOLEX scan](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/bha23703.pdf)):
  - no taking or killing an egg-bearing crawfish;
  - one found in a trap is returned to the water at once "alive and unharmed";
  - no clipping off the eggs, and no possessing one clipped.
- **The FAO's country profile**, May 2009 ([PDF](https://www.fao.org/fishery/docs/DOCUMENT/fcp/en/FI_CP_BS.pdf)):
  - Size: 3¼ in (82.6 mm) carapace and 5½ in (139.7 mm) tail. The tail is measured only when the carapace is gone.
  - Tails with the swimmerets removed are banned.
  - Compressors are for commercial fishing in the open season only, with a permit.
  - Traps "must be made of wooden lathes", 91.4 × 61 × 61 cm, slats at least 2.54 cm apart, unless authorised.
  - Casitas (condos: a zinc sheet on blocks, the lobsters hooked out from under) are the main gear. About 60,000 traps were in use, and trapping has declined since the late 1980s.
- **MSC** ([fisheries.msc.org](https://fisheries.msc.org/en/fisheries/bahamian-spiny-lobster-fishery)): the fishery is certified in two units, hooks and spears, and traps. Certified from 3 Aug 2018 to 30 Jul 2029; 2,387.7 t landed in 2024.
- **Friends of the Environment**, Abaco, two guides:
  - The visitors' one ([PDF](https://www.friendsoftheenvironment.org/_files/ugd/04dc81_e73d426b2ca04e2d83393c91b1659a14.pdf)): "10 Spiny Lobsters (crawfish) per vessel"; nets and traps other than landing nets are prohibited; "Only citizens of The Bahamas may apply for permits for certain practices (air compressors, nets, traps, etc.)".
  - The general one ([PDF](https://www.friendsoftheenvironment.org/_files/ugd/04dc81_5844312c05564d13a5d46d8cafb866b3.pdf)) says the per-vessel limits apply to "citizens, residents, and visitors". That contradicts reg. 48(2) and (4).
- **bahamas.com**, the Ministry of Tourism ([bahamas.com](https://www.bahamas.com/fishing-permit)): 10 lobster aboard when in season. Permanent residents aren't under visitor limits but may not exceed 250 lb.
- **The Association of Bahamas Marinas**, 30 July 2021, updated 1 March 2023 ([bahamasmarinas.com](https://www.bahamasmarinas.com/post/bahamas-lobster-season-august-1-march-31)).
  - It gives 3⅜ in or 6 in in one place and 5½ in in another.
  - It gives 10 a boat, 10 a person and 6 a vessel on the same page.
  - "Spearing legal lobster is legal", with a sling or pole spear only.
  - I don't use its figures where they disagree with the government's.

**Derived.**
- **Size.** 3¼ in is 82.6 mm, 8% over the game's 76.2 mm. At `CARAPACE = 0.34` the game's `minCm` would be 8.255 / 0.34 = 24.3 cm. The 5½ in tail is 14.0 cm.
- **The season.** Closed 122 days a year (1 April to 31 July), a third of the year.
- **The bag.**
  - Florida's 6 a person a day lets a boat of four keep 24.
  - The Bahamas' visitor limit is 10 aboard, however many are aboard. It's a possession limit, not a daily one: run 10 ashore and, by the regulation's wording, she may go back for more.
  - By the 2008 regulations, a Bahamian on her own boat has no recreational limit. By bahamas.com, a permanent resident's is 250 lb (113 kg).
- **The trap.** The legal trap is 36 × 24 × 24 in (91 × 61 × 61 cm): 2 in shorter, 2 in wider and 9 in deeper than the game's.

**For the game** (the figures as the law has them; whether to use them is the user's call):
- Minimum size: a 3¼ in (82.6 mm) carapace, so `minCm` 24.3. A tail alone must be 5½ in (14.0 cm).
- A non-Bahamian may have 10 aboard at any time, a vessel. A Bahamian or resident on her own boat has no recreational limit (250 lb, by bahamas.com).
- Berried females go back alive. Already in the game.
- Closed 1 April to 31 July.
- Traps: only Bahamian citizens, with a $50 crawfish trap permit. Commercial sale needs a licensed vessel ($100 to $250) owned by a resident Bahamian.
- The legal trap is wooden slat, 91 × 61 × 61 cm (36 × 24 × 24 in), slats at least 2.5 cm (1 in) apart.

## 11. Other Bahamas rules that touch the game (reported only)

**Bonefish.**
- **No sale, no netting.**
  - DMR's notice, 1 July 2008, via Bahamas Press ([bahamaspress.com](https://www.bahamaspress.com/?p=1552)): "it is illegal to buy or sell bonefish in The Bahamas"; netting (hauling) them is also illegal.
  - The FAO profile (2009) and Friends of the Environment say the same.
- **The Flats Fishing Regulations, 2017** (S.I. No. 2 of 2017). I didn't open the text: the FAOLEX and LEAP copies returned 404 and 403. As reported:
  - Reg. 6: anyone 12 or older needs a personal angler's licence for the flats.
  - Flats anglers practise catch and release only for bonefish, permit, snook, cobia and tarpon, with an exception for fish taken for food. A guides' association summary says one bonefish may be kept for food. Neither is verified.
  - (Snippet only: [Saltwater Sportsman](https://www.saltwatersportsman.com/?p=53878), [Coastal Angler](https://coastalanglermag.com/?p=75096).)
- **Enforcement suspended.** Scott Sheywood's blog, 24 April 2018, quoting the Tribune of 23 April ([blogspot](https://scottsheywood.blogspot.com/2018/04/bahamas-government-suspends-flats.html)): the government "suspended enforcement of the regulations until their impact can be reviewed". I found no notice of it resuming.
- **What's said now.**
  - bahamas.com: a flats licence for everyone over 12, Bahamian-certified guides for two or more anglers by boat, Bahamian-registered vessels only on the flats. On bonefish, "No catch limit" with catch and release "strongly encouraged". That doesn't square with the 2017 text as reported.
  - The Association of Bahamas Marinas ([bahamasmarinas.com](https://www.bahamasmarinas.com/boating-faqs)): from 1 July 2023 a non-Bahamian's Personal Angler License is $15 a day, $50 a week, $100 a month, $600 a year; free for Bahamians and permanent residents. It applies on the flats (1 to 6 ft deep), from boat, shore or dock.
  - The 2016 draft's fees were $15, $20, $30 and $60 ([Gink and Gasoline](https://www.ginkandgasoline.com/saltwater-fly-fishing/bahamas-bonefishing-regulations-update/), 4 Aug 2016). The 2017 regulations were reported as "$15 for a single day up to $60 for an annual licence".

**Spearfishing,** in one line (`docs/m19/sources-big-game.md` has it in full): a Hawaiian sling or pole spear only, no spearguns or trigger devices. Not within 1 mile of New Providence or Grand Bahama, nor within 200 yd of a Family Island. No scuba. A visitor needs it written on his permit (bahamas.com; FAO 2009; Friends of the Environment).

**Others that touch the game,** from DMR's 2008 excerpt, bahamas.com, Friends of the Environment and FAO 2009:
- Billfish go back unharmed unless landed in an approved tournament.
- Sharks have been protected since 2011, and turtles are too.
- Nassau grouper is closed 1 December to 28 February. No grouper under 3 lb (1.36 kg).
- A foreign vessel: 18 pelagics aboard at most (dolphin, wahoo, kingfish, tuna, in any combination); 20 demersal fish or 60 lb (27 kg), whichever is less; at most 6 rods in use.
- A visitor may not sell or trade any fisheries product.
- A cast net must be authorised on the visitor's permit.
- No scuba or compressor for harvest; a compressor only for Bahamians, with a permit.
- A foreign pleasure vessel's fishing permit is $100 under 50 ft and $300 over, good for 30 days ([bahamasmarinas.com](https://www.bahamasmarinas.com/post/bahamas-boating-and-fishing-fee-structures-and-regulations-as-of-july-1-2025), 2 July 2025).
- Stone crab is closed 1 June to 15 October; claws only, at least 4 in.

## For the user to decide

Each rule below conflicts with what the game has. They're reported, not recommended. Several hang on one question: **is she a visitor, or a Bahamian or permanent resident?**
- The visitor rules (10 crawfish a vessel, no traps, no selling) apply to non-Bahamians.
- Reg. 48(4) treats a permanent resident as Bahamian.
- Friends of the Environment says the per-vessel limits apply to everyone.

1. **The crawfish minimum.** The game's is 3 in (76.2 mm, Florida's). The Bahamas' is 3¼ in (82.6 mm) carapace, or a 5½ in tail.
2. **The crawfish bag.** The game's is 6 a person a day (Florida's). The Bahamas' is 10 a vessel aboard at any time, for non-Bahamians. There's no recreational limit for a Bahamian on her own boat (250 lb for residents, by bahamas.com).
3. **The closed season, 1 April to 31 July.** The game has none, because "the island has no seasons" (`Turtles.js:9`; `traps.md:40`). Adding one needs months on the clock.
4. **Traps.** A non-Bahamian may not trap at all: reg. 48(1)(b), and only citizens may apply for trap permits. A Bahamian needs a $50 crawfish trap permit. The game sells traps to the player with no permit (`Gear.js:169-173`). **If she's a visitor, her traps are illegal.**
5. **The trap itself.** The law's lobster trap is wooden slats, 91 × 61 × 61 cm (36 × 24 × 24 in), unless authorised otherwise. The game's is Barbados chicken wire, 37 × 22 × 15 in (`traps.md:5`; the user's choice in M16).
6. **Selling the catch.** A visitor may not sell or trade any fisheries product. Commercial fishing needs a licensed vessel owned by a resident Bahamian. The game sells lobster and fish to Marta.
7. **Scuba for lobster or fish.** No one may harvest any fisheries resource on scuba; a compressor needs a permit and is for Bahamians only. The game sells scuba tanks (`Gear.js:88`) and lets you take lobster and spear fish on a dive.
8. **The speargun.** Spearguns and trigger devices are banned: a sling or pole spear only. The game's guns are band spearguns (`Gear.js:97-101`). `sources-big-game.md` covers this in full.
9. **Hermit crabs.** Whether collecting them for bait is legal is unclear. A visitors' guide says live shells with an animal in them may not be taken from the sea, and the parks are no-take. I found no regulation naming them.
10. **Bonefish, permit, snook and tarpon on the flats.** The 2017 regulations as reported make them catch and release for flats anglers, enforcement suspended in 2018. The game already lets bonefish, permit and tarpon go (`protected`). The snook has a bag of 1 (`FishTable.js:131`).
11. **The flats licence.** Over 12, a non-Bahamian pays $15 a day to $600 a year (from July 2023, by the marinas' association), and two or more anglers by boat need a guide. The game has no licence.
12. **The pelagic and demersal limits.** For a foreign vessel: 18 pelagics aboard in any combination, and 20 demersal fish or 60 lb. The game has Florida-style per-kind bags: mahi 10, wahoo 2, yellowfin 3 (`FishTable.js:78, 82, 85`).
13. **The Nassau grouper.** Closed 1 December to 28 February, and no grouper under 3 lb. The game protects it all year (`FishTable.js:69`): stricter, not illegal.
