# M15: the world, for consideration

Two things: the island worth going all the way round, and the sea past it. Both come before M16's boats ([M16.md](M16.md)), because there's nowhere new for a bigger boat to take you until there's somewhere new to go. M15 is built first and M16 on top of it; what each owes the other is listed at the end (With M16). None of it is promised.

## Why nobody goes north

Nearly everyone does the same thing: starts on the boardwalk looking down the pier (`WORLD.start`, facing south), walks to the boat, and heads out of the bay to the south, the way the bay opens. Most have never seen the north beach. Nothing gives them a reason to:

- The fish are the same everywhere at the same depth. A bite's water is worked out from depth alone, with the reef and the pier the only places named (`Bites.habitatAt`: shallows, reef, pier, bay, deep). Deep water off the north shore holds what deep water off the bay does, and the bay is closer.
- Everything you need is in the bay: Joe, Marta, the shelter, the dock. The minimap marks only those four and your boat.
- Nothing is there to be found: no landmark, nothing washed up, nothing anyone asks for.
- The north shore's sea (M14) breaks on its windward side, in the mountain's lee from the village, so its surf is seen and heard only by those who go there, and hardly anyone does.

## The island worth going round

Nudges, not rules: no fence on the south, only reasons to go elsewhere.

- Places that fish differently. Give the water around the island places of its own, beside depth (new keys in `habitatAt`, each a place on the map):
  - the north beach's surf: pompano, permit and snook in the wash, caught from the sand (the north shore's sea from M14 makes it its own water, good on a surf day);
  - a creek or a mangrove lagoon on the west: tarpon, snook and juvenile fish, sheltered when the rest is blown out;
  - a windward reef or a wreck off the north point: grouper and snapper bigger than the bay's, and a dive (the speargun);
  - the bay staying what it is now, the easy water by the dock.
- Fish that move with the weather: in a blow from the north the bay in the lee fishes; when the trades' sea is down the north shore's open. Which side of the island is good on a given day changes, and the forecast says so.
- Something to see: a lighthouse on the north point (lit at night, a mark to steer home by once the sea is bigger), a wreck on the windward reef, sea caves under the cliffs, a turtle beach on the north side where they nest on summer nights.
- Things washed up. The north beach is the windward one, so what the sea carries in lands there: wrack, floats, sea beans, a message in a bottle, driftwood, and after a storm more of it (the hurricane's wrack from M13 belongs there most of all). Beachcombing on foot, Joe or Marta buying what's worth something (built here; M16's inventory lists it with the rest of what they buy).
- A chart you fill in. The minimap's coast beyond the bay starts out blank and is drawn in where you've been: the bays and points named as you come to them, the places that fish differently marked once you've caught there, your own marks added. Marta sells a chart of the island for those who'd rather not.
- What people ask for: Joe's orders and Marta's wanted list asking for fish that come only from a certain place (pompano from the north beach's surf, a tarpon from the creek), paid well.
- A reason to stop over there: a fish camp or a shack on the north shore with its own fuel and someone to sell to, so a trip round the island doesn't have to end where it started.
- The island's records for each place, and a first time at each one noted in the log.
- The Guide's tips pointing the way once you've the hang of the bay, and the start's view including a sign: north beach, that way.
- On foot: it can be walked now (checked 2026-10-27, in the headless browser): from the start east round the village (x 110), then straight over the ridge (205 m at x 60, z -450 to -500) and down to the north beach (z about -830), some 6 minutes at a walk. But only because the walk has no steepest slope (`Player.updateWalk` sets you on the ground wherever it is): the 45° climb at z -300 to -360 goes at full speed. A real way over (a trail with switchbacks, or round by the shore) and slopes too steep to walk up would make it a route and not a straight line. (The walk also went into swimming at the summit, at 203 m, likely the test's own doing, its water height read without frames drawn; to be checked in play.)

## The sea past the island

The island's world is a 2 km square (`WORLD.terrainSize`, 2048 m, the island and its seabed at 1 m). Past its edge the sea goes on forever, but its bottom is a flat 90 m (`terrainHeightAt` outside the domain) with nothing on it or under it. The lobster boat makes about 12 to 16 m/s flat out (`BoatController.pitchSpeed`, 16 m/s), so half an hour's run is 20 km or more: ten times past the island's edge, in water nobody's made.

In the order it would have to be done:

1. **The world moving with you (a floating origin).** Far from the world's middle the GPU's 32-bit numbers lose their fineness: by 10 to 20 km out the boat, the water, your hands and the rod shake and jitter. Everything has to be drawn about where you are, not about the island: the water and its waves, the seabed, the sky and the clouds, the weather's fields, the rain, the fish and the wildlife, the shadows, the colliders, the others' poses. This is the base for all the rest, and the riskiest part: nothing far out can be judged until it's done.
2. **The seabed out there.** The island's shelf, then the drop-off, then the deep: a ledge or a canyon to fish and deep-drop on, a sandbar or a cay to find. A coarse depth map around the island (say 32 m a texel over 64 km: 2048², as many texels again as the island's), or worked out where you are, with a few places shaped by hand; blended into the 1 m map at the island's edge. Not by making the 1 m map bigger: it's the island already.
3. **The water by its depth.** Green over the shelf, the deep's blue past the drop-off, a line between them you can see. The sea rougher offshore, where the wind has the fetch (the swell and the whitecaps keyed to the open sea, not the bay); the shore fields' breaking only where there's a shore.
4. **Something to find.** (What M16's tuna tower and polarized glasses are for.) Weedlines, floating debris, birds working bait, a colour change, the current's edge: placed around you as you go, and drifting. Seeded from where they are and the day, the way the weather is, so everyone out there sees the same weedline in the same place.
5. **The fish of the far water.** New kinds of water for the bites: the drop-off, the ledge, under the weed and the debris, the deep blue, where the far-water fish are. M15 makes the water and moves the mahi and tuna (in the fish table already) out to it; M16 adds the far water's own fish (wahoo, the billfish, swordfish) and the tackle and bait to catch them, with its sportfisher.
6. **The sky and the weather out there.** The clouds and their weather fields are laid out about the island (`SkyProClouds.weatherBounds`), and so are the rain, the storms and the swell: they all have to go where you are. A thunderstorm offshore you can run from, or not; a hurricane's track crossing the far grounds before it reaches the island.
7. **The island seen from far off.** At 20 km it's a far shape on the horizon, drawn as one (the island's own detail not drawn past a few km). The earth's curve hides the low land first: past 10 to 15 km only the mountain shows, and then not even that. At night the lighthouse is what you steer home by.
8. **Finding your way.** A compass at the helm, a chartplotter with your marks and where the fish were (M15 builds them and fits them to the lobster boat; M16 makes them each boat's gear, sold for the others), the minimap zooming out to the far grounds, how far home and whether the fuel will get you there.
9. **Getting home.** Out of fuel far out: Marta's tow, its fee by the distance. A weather radio at the helm for the forecast at sea (the same: built here for the lobster boat, each boat's in M16). The island's records and the log naming the far places once you've found them.
10. **Picking up where you left off** (below). Needed once a trip out is a long one, whatever the boat.
11. **Playing together.** The others' boats far off (what's sent for them, and drawn only once they're near enough to see), everyone's sea agreeing on what's out there (item 4), the poses' numbers fine enough far out.
12. **The sound out there.** The open ocean's own: the shore's surf fading behind you, the hull and the wind and the sea's hiss.
13. **Phones.** Far sea drawn in less, the far features fewer, the island's far shape cheap.
14. **The checks.** The headless renders and the QA script taking spots offshore (far out, at the drop-off, at a weedline), and the tests for the floating origin's sums.
15. **Where it ends.** (How far each of M16's boats can go is up to it: the kayak the bay, the sportfisher the far grounds.) A soft edge, not a wall: past the far grounds the sea grows emptier and rougher, the fuel and the weather turning you home. A cay or a sandbar some 10 km out is a first place to make for.

## Picking up where you left off

The save keeps your money, cooler, log, upgrades, fuel and hull (`GameState.toJSON`), but not where you were: every visit starts you on the island. That's fine in the bay. It isn't for a half-hour run offshore, made again every time you come back.

- Keep where the boat is, its heading, and whether you're aboard. Come back and you're there, at anchor where you left it. (Offshore it's too deep for the anchor now, 200 ft of rode: until M16's deep-water anchor and drift sock, a boat left out past the anchor's reach drifts while you're away, its drift worked out.)
- What happened while you were gone (the weather's the same for everyone, and goes on without you):
  - a storm came through: the boat rode it out at anchor, with some damage to its hull, or it was found adrift and towed in, for a fee (Marta's tow);
  - gone long enough: the fish in the box have spoiled, and perhaps the boat's been towed in. (In M15 the catch keeps so long; M16's ice, bought by the bag, is what makes it keep longer.)
- A hurricane on its way when you come back: you're told so, and how long you have to run for port.
- Playing together: your boat shows at anchor on everyone else's sea while you're gone (chosen). Nobody can take it: a boat's pose is only ever its owner's (`TheirBoat` in `Together.js` is their boat drawn where they say it is; those riding along walk its deck, but only the owner steers). With the owner gone, though, there's no one sending its pose: where it lies has to be kept in the group's shared save (as the records are), and every visitor's sea floats it from there.
  - Others may step aboard an anchored boat with nobody home, and fish off it, and nothing more: not its hold, its stores, its helm or its anchor. (Riding along is only while the owner's aboard now.) A "keep off" for each boat can come later, if it's ever abused.
  - A hurricane: once its warning's out, Marta tows in every anchored boat whose owner's away, for a fee (as she hauls out ahead of one now), and a note says where it went and what it cost when you're back. Left out in it, a boat would be wrecked, which is too hard on someone who wasn't there.
  - Ordinary weather, thunderstorms too, it rides out at anchor, a squall taking a little off its hull.
  - Gone about a week, it's towed in, so the sea doesn't fill with the boats of those who've stopped coming. The fish in its box have spoiled by then anyway, the ice gone.
- Worth having for every boat, not only the sportfisher: M15 builds it for the lobster boat, and M16's boats each use it (off a reef in the center console, with the lobster boat's traps set).

## With M16

M15 is built first. What M16 ([M16.md](M16.md)) takes from it, and what M15 leaves for M16:

- The floating origin, the offshore seabed, the far water's look and its features: the ground every M16 boat goes out on. The sportfisher can't be made until they are.
- The offshore features (weedlines, debris, birds): M16's tuna tower and polarized glasses are for seeing them.
- The far water's kinds of water for the bites, with the mahi and tuna moved out to them: M16 adds the far fish themselves, and the tackle, leaders, lures and bait (ballyhoo, squid, chum) to catch them.
- Navigation (compass, chartplotter, weather radio) built and fitted to the lobster boat: M16 makes them each boat's gear.
- Picking up where you left off, and the anchored-boat rules, built for the lobster boat: every M16 boat uses them. M16's deep-water anchor and drift sock let a boat lie at anchor offshore; until then it drifts.
- The catch keeping so long: M16's ice makes it keep longer, its stores make days out possible.
- Marta's tow priced by the distance: M16's flares and EPIRB get you found far out.
- The soft edge: how far out each of M16's boats can go is its own.
- Beachcombing's finds, bought here: in M16's inventory with the rest.
- Things M15 doesn't need from M16: the north side (its places, landmarks, beachcombing, the chart, the slope limit and a trail) stands on its own.
