# M15: the world, for consideration

Two things: the island worth going all the way round, and the sea past it. Both come before M16's boats ([M16.md](M16.md)), because there's nowhere new for a bigger boat to take you until there's somewhere new to go. M15 is built first and M16 on top of it; what each owes the other is listed at the end (With M16). None of it is promised.

## Why nobody goes north

Nearly everyone does the same thing: starts on the boardwalk looking down the pier (`WORLD.start`, facing south), walks to the boat, and heads out of the bay to the south, the way the bay opens. Most have never seen the north beach. Nothing gives them a reason to:

- The fish are the same everywhere at the same depth. A bite's water is worked out from depth alone, with the reef and the pier the only places named (`Bites.habitatAt`: shallows, reef, pier, bay, deep). Deep water off the north shore holds what deep water off the bay does, and the bay is closer.
- Everything you need is in the bay: Joe, Marta, the shelter, the dock. The minimap marks only those four and your boat.
- Nothing is there to be found: no landmark, nothing washed up, nothing anyone asks for.
- The north shore's sea (M14) breaks on its windward side, in the mountain's lee from the village, so its surf is seen and heard only by those who go there, and hardly anyone does.

## The island worth going round

Nudges, not rules: no fence on the south, only reasons to go elsewhere.

- Places that fish differently. Give the water around the island places of its own, beside depth (new keys in `habitatAt`, each a place on the map):
  - the north beach's surf: pompano, permit and snook in the wash, caught from the sand (the north shore's sea from M14 makes it its own water, good on a surf day);
  - a creek or a mangrove lagoon on the west: tarpon, snook and juvenile fish, sheltered when the rest is blown out;
  - a windward reef or a wreck off the north point: grouper and snapper bigger than the bay's, and a dive (the speargun);
  - the bay staying what it is now, the easy water by the dock.
- Fish that move with the weather: in a blow from the north the bay in the lee fishes; when the trades' sea is down the north shore's open. Which side of the island is good on a given day changes, and the forecast says so.
- Something to see: a lighthouse on the north point (lit at night, a mark to steer home by once the sea is bigger), a wreck on the windward reef, sea caves under the cliffs, a turtle beach on the north side where they nest on summer nights.
- Things washed up. The north beach is the windward one, so what the sea carries in lands there: wrack, floats, sea beans, a message in a bottle, driftwood, and after a storm more of it (the hurricane's wrack from M13 belongs there most of all). Beachcombing on foot, Joe or Marta buying what's worth something (built here; M16's inventory lists it with the rest of what they buy).
- A chart you fill in. The minimap's coast beyond the bay starts out blank and is drawn in where you've been: the bays and points named as you come to them, the places that fish differently marked once you've caught there, your own marks added. Marta sells a chart of the island for those who'd rather not.
- What people ask for: Joe's orders and Marta's wanted list asking for fish that come only from a certain place (pompano from the north beach's surf, a tarpon from the creek), paid well.
- A reason to stop over there: a fish camp or a shack on the north shore with its own fuel and someone to sell to, so a trip round the island doesn't have to end where it started.
- The island's records for each place, and a first time at each one noted in the log.
- The Guide's tips pointing the way once you've the hang of the bay, and the start's view including a sign: north beach, that way.
- On foot: it can be walked now (checked 2026-10-27, in the headless browser): from the start east round the village (x 110), then straight over the ridge (205 m at x 60, z -450 to -500) and down to the north beach (z about -830), some 6 minutes at a walk. But only because the walk has no steepest slope (`Player.updateWalk` sets you on the ground wherever it is): the 45° climb at z -300 to -360 goes at full speed. A real way over (a trail with switchbacks, or round by the shore) and slopes too steep to walk up would make it a route and not a straight line. (The walk also went into swimming at the summit, at 203 m, likely the test's own doing, its water height read without frames drawn; to be checked in play.)

## The sea past the island

The island's world is a 2 km square (`WORLD.terrainSize`, 2048 m, the island and its seabed at 1 m). Past its edge the sea goes on forever, but its bottom is a flat 90 m (`terrainHeightAt` outside the domain) with nothing on it or under it. The lobster boat makes about 12 to 16 m/s flat out (`BoatController.pitchSpeed`, 16 m/s), so half an hour's run is 20 km or more: ten times past the island's edge, in water nobody's made.

In the order it would have to be done:

1. **Drawn about where you are (a render origin).** Far from the world's middle the GPU's 32-bit numbers lose their fineness. Measured in step 1 (The far sea, measured, below): the boat, your hands and the rod stay steady at any distance, but the sea, the shadows, the line and the foam don't, and the foam and the rain's rings break outright by 40 km. The game keeps its own positions; only the drawing moves with you. This is the base for all the rest.
2. **The seabed out there.** The island's shelf, then the drop-off, then the deep: a ledge or a canyon to fish and deep-drop on, a sandbar or a cay to find. A coarse depth map around the island (say 32 m a texel over 64 km: 2048², as many texels again as the island's), or worked out where you are, with a few places shaped by hand; blended into the 1 m map at the island's edge. Not by making the 1 m map bigger: it's the island already.
3. **The water by its depth.** Green over the shelf, the deep's blue past the drop-off, a line between them you can see. The sea rougher offshore, where the wind has the fetch (the swell and the whitecaps keyed to the open sea, not the bay); the shore fields' breaking only where there's a shore.
4. **Something to find.** (What M16's tuna tower and polarized glasses are for.) Weedlines, floating debris, birds working bait, a colour change, the current's edge: placed around you as you go, and drifting. Seeded from where they are and the day, the way the weather is, so everyone out there sees the same weedline in the same place.
5. **The fish of the far water.** New kinds of water for the bites: the drop-off, the ledge, under the weed and the debris, the deep blue, where the far-water fish are. M15 makes the water and moves the mahi and tuna (in the fish table already) out to it; M16 adds the far water's own fish (wahoo, the billfish, swordfish) and the tackle and bait to catch them, with its sportfisher.
6. **The sky and the weather out there.** The clouds and their weather fields are laid out about the island (`SkyProClouds.weatherBounds`), and so are the rain, the storms and the swell: they all have to go where you are. A thunderstorm offshore you can run from, or not; a hurricane's track crossing the far grounds before it reaches the island.
7. **The island seen from far off.** At 20 km it's a far shape on the horizon, drawn as one (the island's own detail not drawn past a few km). The earth's curve hides the low land first: past 10 to 15 km only the mountain shows, and then not even that. At night the lighthouse is what you steer home by.
8. **Finding your way.** A compass at the helm, a chartplotter with your marks and where the fish were (M15 builds them and fits them to the lobster boat; M16 makes them each boat's gear, sold for the others), the minimap zooming out to the far grounds, how far home and whether the fuel will get you there.
9. **Getting home.** Out of fuel far out: Marta's tow, its fee by the distance. A weather radio at the helm for the forecast at sea (the same: built here for the lobster boat, each boat's in M16). The island's records and the log naming the far places once you've found them.
10. **Picking up where you left off** (below). Needed once a trip out is a long one, whatever the boat.
11. **Playing together.** The others' boats far off (what's sent for them, and drawn only once they're near enough to see), everyone's sea agreeing on what's out there (item 4), the poses' numbers fine enough far out.
12. **The sound out there.** The open ocean's own: the shore's surf fading behind you, the hull and the wind and the sea's hiss.
13. **Phones.** Far sea drawn in less, the far features fewer, the island's far shape cheap.
14. **The checks.** The headless renders and the QA script taking spots offshore (far out, at the drop-off, at a weedline), and the tests for the floating origin's sums.
15. **Where it ends.** (How far each of M16's boats can go is up to it: the kayak the bay, the sportfisher the far grounds.) A soft edge, not a wall: past the far grounds the sea grows emptier and rougher, the fuel and the weather turning you home. A cay or a sandbar some 10 km out is a first place to make for.

## The far sea, measured

M15's step 1 (2026-10-28): the boat put out 0.6 to 40 km south of the dock in the headless renders, on each tier (high in fair weather, mid blustery, low in rain), and the code read for what it takes for granted. `tools/far-sea.mjs` does it again: screenshots ahead, over the side, back at the island and up, and how much the picture jumps as the camera's moved 0.05 mm a frame with the world held still (anything drawn from world positions on the GPU snaps as it crosses a 32-bit float's step, the rest moves smoothly). Drawn on the CPU (SwiftShader): a phone's GPU may do worse, its `sin` and division less exact.

A 32-bit float's step is 0.5 mm at 5 km, 1 mm at 10 km, 2 mm at 20 km and 4 mm at 40 km.

Steady:
- **The boat, your hands, the rod and everything else three.js places.** It works out where each thing is from the camera in double precision on the CPU (`modelViewMatrix`), so a thing a metre from you is as steady 40 km out as at the dock. The old fear, the boat and the rod shaking 10 to 20 km out, was wrong.
- **The poses sent to the others** (JSON numbers, to 1 cm and 1 mm: fine at any distance), the depth buffer (its fineness doesn't depend on where you are), the clouds (marched from the camera, their maps repeating or following it), the rain and motes about the camera.

Jittering, drawn from world positions on the GPU:
- **The sea**: its vertices are world positions (`WaterSurface`: `transformed = wPos`), and its ripples, foam and rain rings are keyed on them. Over the side on high, the picture's mean change a frame grows with the distance: 0.06 at 0.6 km, 0.11 at 1.5, 0.14 at 5, 0.23 at 10, 0.39 at 20 and 0.63 at 40 (six and ten times), a sparkle over the whole sea. On mid and low it had doubled or trebled by 20 km.
- **Shadows on deck**: three's `shadowMatrix * worldPosition`; their edges crawl.
- **The fishing line and the anchor rode**: built from world-space uniforms (`GameMaterials`).
- **On mid, the sea's grid**: its lattice snap (`CDLOD`: `floor( p / h + 1e-3 )`, the 1e-3 less than a float's step out there) puts one vertex in 20 on the wrong point at 20 km and one in 10 at 40 km where a GPU divides exactly: holes and folds in the sea. SwiftShader didn't show it (a float32 simulation does); low and high snap exactly.
- **Kept in 32-bit floats on the CPU**: the cast net's cloth (its speeds come in steps of 0.23 m/s at 20 km), the spray and the wake's puffs (the slow ones stop), and the water's height queries (a little noise in the boat's buoyancy).

Broken outright, by 40 km (fine at 20 on SwiftShader):
- **The whitecaps' foam** goes from broken streaks to smooth white slabs: its noise is a `fract( sin( dot( cell, … ) ) * 43758.5 )` hash of world cells, and out there the cells are too big a number for it (`waterNoise`; the windrows' lace and a storm's spume streaks the same).
- **The rain's rings** fall into a lattice, every ring the same age, by the same hash (`waterRainRing`).

Missing out there, each for its own step:
- **The island's gone by 5 km** on high, 3 km on mid, 1.6 km on low: the fog's full there (`Sky`'s `FOG_DISTANCE`), and its ground isn't drawn more than 4 km from its square (the terrain's `CDLOD`); its buildings and trees still are, out to 12 km, under the fog. A 200 m peak stays above the horizon to 50 km, and a clear day's air shows it a long way. (Step 12.)
- **The bottom's a flat 90 m** everywhere past the square: the sounder reads 90 m, the anchor's 200 ft of rode won't reach, every bite is the deep's, and the fish finder rings two "rich" places on empty sea (`Minimap`'s `_richest`). (Steps 4 and 8.)
- **No fish, no whale**; birds coming back after a storm appear 280 m from you and fly the 20 km home. (Steps 7 and 8.)
- **The weather's the island's wherever you are** (`weatherAt`): its rain falls on you with no shower in sight. Showers come only within 14 km of the island across the wind, strikes within 12 km of it, the thunderstorms' map reaches 48 km, a hurricane's bands 42 km. (Step 6.)
- **The minimap and the chartplotter are empty blue** past 1.3 km; the radar shows the island to 24 NM. (Step 15.)
- **Nothing stops you going on**, and Marta's tow is $90 at most. (Step 16.)

So, a render origin, not a floating one:
- The game keeps its own world positions as they are. JavaScript's doubles are fine to well under a micrometre 40 km out, and everything that uses them (the weather, the fields, the fish, the colliders, the saves, the others' poses) goes on as it is.
- Only the drawing moves: an origin snapped to whole kilometres, jumping as you go, the scene drawn about it (all of it under one group at minus the origin, the camera too), so three's matrices, its shadows and its own materials are all in small numbers.
- What has to know about it:
  - the shaders that make world positions themselves: the sea's grid, its nodes' corners given about the origin (which also mends mid's snap);
  - those keyed on world positions for a noise or a phase: the waves' phases, offset by the origin's share of each; the foam's and the rings' noise on an integer hash, offset by whole cells; the ripples and the sea's detail, by the origin modulo their repeat; anything sampling the island's maps;
  - the uniforms set from the game's positions (the line, the rode, the wake);
  - the 32-bit pools on the CPU (the cast net, the spray, the wake's puffs, the water's queries), kept about the origin and moved when it jumps.
- Weighed and set aside: moving the game's own coordinates with you (a floating origin proper). Every system that keeps a position would have to convert, and players far apart would each have their own origin, for nothing: the doubles are fine already.

## A phone, measured

M15's step 2 (2026-10-28): the game on a phone for the first time with its figures read, to judge All the Spaces' low budget (150 MB of script memory) by. An iPhone (414 x 896 at 3x: an XS Max or 11 Pro Max, 4 GB; Safari), the low tier, a build served over the network, with the readout (`?readout`, [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md), Testing). Played from the start into a hurricane (8 minutes), then through a whole one with the ballast (10 minutes):

- **Graphics memory** 176 to 182 MB of 256; **textures** 121.6 to 127.5 MB of 128: the low budget's textures are full, most of them come in as it's played (74 MB at the start), and anything more on low needs something out.
- **Frames**: 20 to 23 a second (41 to 50 ms, 55 to 69 ms at the 95th percentile), drawn at about one pixel a point already (412 x 673 on the 3x screen). The checks never judge it; this is the first a phone's has been seen. It's the phone's limit, not its memory (step 20).
- **Draw calls** at most 83 of 150, **triangles** 252k of 300k, **sounds** 14 of 16.
- **Script memory**: Safari doesn't give it. (Chrome on a build, on this machine: 108 MB settled, 137 at its peak while loading.)
- **Room left**: with the readout's ballast (`&ballast`: 50 MB more held every 15 s, filled with noise, until the browser kills the page), the page held 1,350 MB on top of the game right through the hurricane and was never killed.

So on this phone memory is far from the limit: the game's script memory could grow several times over and still fit. The phone's frame rate is what's short. A phone the platform itself calls low (2 GB or 2 cores and under, `main.js`'s `standaloneTier`, as the app's) isn't measured: this iPhone would play mid on its own.

## Drawn about where you are, built

M15's step 3 (2026-10-28). Not one group at minus the origin, as step 1 had it: three already places everything it owns from the camera in doubles, so moving the whole scene would have fixed only what three does right, and it would have moved every shader's world positions under it. Instead each thing that draws from world positions on the GPU is drawn about a point near it, worked out in doubles ([ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md), Drawn about where you are):

- **The sea**: its grid follows the camera 512 m a step, each level's offset and parity worked out so every vertex lands on the world's own lattice point and morphs its way (this mends mid's snap too). Its ripples, the rain's rings, the foam's lace and the whitecaps' crests and marbling take the origin's share in doubles, on an integer hash where they were `fract( sin )`. The whitecaps' marbling now turns about you as the wind goes round; about the world's middle, 40 km out it would have swept across the sea at hundreds of metres a second.
- **The sun's shadows**: looked up from view space, their matrices times the camera's, for each render.
- **The fishing line and the anchor rode**: drawn about a point by the rod's tip.
- **The cast net, the spray, the wake and the drips**: their positions in doubles, drawn about a point by them or by the camera; the boat's lamps by their own matrices; the water's height queries in doubles.

`test/sea-origin.mjs` works out the grid, the foam's noises, the crests and the line as the GPU does, in 32-bit floats, against the world's in doubles, out to 60 km.

How much the picture jumps, the camera moved 0.05 mm a frame (mean change a pixel, over the side), at 40 km and (for comparison) near the island, where a float's step is a fraction of a millimetre:

| | before | after | near the island |
|---|---|---|---|
| high, fair | 0.155 | 0.058 to 0.065 | 0.065 (1.5 km) |
| mid, blustery | 0.087 | 0.046 | 0.040 (0.6 km) |
| low, in rain | 0.200 | 0.041 | 0.041 (0.6 km) |
| high, rain at night | | 0.057 | 0.054 (0.6 km) |

What's left is what moves anywhere: edges crossing pixels, the shadows' soft edges, the sea's glints. On high the figure follows the light more than the distance: 0.032 to 0.040 at 0.6 km, where the boat was under a cloud's shadow, 0.065 at 1.5 km and 0.051 at 5 km in the sun, as at 40 km (the share of pixels jumping hard is the same everywhere, 0.005). The whitecaps' foam keeps its lace at 40 km and the rain's rings scatter again. The deck's shadows stopped crawling at the dock as well (high's 0.6 km figure was 0.060 before).

Left for the steps that take them out there: the birds (drawn from a 32-bit texture of world positions; step 7), the whale's marks (step 7) and a hurricane's spume streaks (step 6), all still keyed on world positions; and how the wind turning moves the spume's streaks, about the world's middle. The rain and the air motes wrap their box about the camera from its world position, a few mm out there; measured, it doesn't show.

## The seabed out there, built

M15's step 4 (2026-10-29). Past the island's square the bottom was a flat 90 m; now it's a seabed of its own, laid out as a small volcanic island's in the trades is (the Lesser Antilles': Saba, Montserrat, St. Croix), placed by hand and roughened with noise. `tools/seabed-chart.mjs` draws a chart of it.

- **The shelf**: the island's own seabed going on out to the shelf break at 45 to 105 m, narrowest on the windward north (the break 1.3 km off the north shore) and widest to the south-west, 3 km out toward the bank. Its edge wanders and is scalloped where the gullies' heads cut back into it.
- **The drop-off**: steepest at the top, about 40 degrees, the windward side the steeper; easing as it goes down to the deep's floor at about 1,800 m, 10 to 15 km out. Gullies run down it.
- **The ledge**, on the north: a terrace at about 210 m along the slope, 250 to 650 m wide, 2.5 km off the north shore, where a deep-drop rig would go. It's the north's own, a reason to go round.
- **The canyon** off the bay: its head cuts into the shelf 1.4 km south of the beach, 72 m deep where the shelf beside it is 43. A branch heads further east. It winds south, 200 to 360 m deep into the slope, down to the deep's floor, and comes out level onto it 11 km off.
- **The hump**, 6 km east-north-east: a seamount rising from 1,800 m in a ridge of three peaks, the highest to about 100 m.
- **The bank**, 10 km south-west: a reef-rimmed platform about 5.6 by 3.2 km, its windward rim shoaling to 2 to 6 m and its middle about 20 m. Its walls drop to the deep all round. On its lee side there's a crescent sandbar 1.4 km long, 0.4 m under at its crest, with a channel through it a third of the way along. You can stand on it, and the boat runs aground on it.

How it's made and read:
- It's baked like the island, by `pnpm bake` (`terrain/SeabedModel.js`; 3 to 6 s of work, too long for a phone to do at start-up), into `public/world/offshore.bin`: 32 m over 32 km, 0.76 MB. Low-powered devices get `offshore-512.bin`, 64 m, 0.25 MB.
- `TerrainData` reads it wherever its own 1 m map doesn't reach. The bake's last 120 m of the island's square, which faded to the flat 90 m, are blended into the seabed instead when it loads. The island's seabed there and the one it meets are 0.6 m apart on average, 5.6 m at most.
- So everything that asks how deep it is has the seabed: the anchor (it holds on the shelf, 44 m deep 760 m past the square to the north), the waves' feel of the bottom, the boat running aground, wading.
- The ground's grid now follows you as the sea's does and is drawn about its origin. Seabed deeper than 40 m isn't drawn, as it's never seen. Past the island's maps its surface is the seabed's: its normal from its heights, sand, with seagrass and rubble in patches.
- **The sounder** loses the bottom past 1,200 ft (a workboat's 600 W, 50 kHz one, about half what its makers claim). Off the drop-off it shows "-- ft" and no bottom, just the fish it finds on the way down.
- **The shore's breaking waves** stay the island's: its field's waves fade out over the last 100 m of its square, so the bank's shallows get none of them. Their own breakers belong to step 5, with the water's colour by depth. Over the bank the water already reads turquoise, as the sea's colour takes the depth into account.

`test/seabed.mjs` checks that:
- the island's square is as baked, but for its edge;
- there's no step across the edge;
- the break is 30 to 115 m deep all round (the canyon heads the shallowest) and the drop-off up to 43 degrees;
- the ledge is over 250 m across;
- the canyon cuts below the slope beside it, its floor going only down;
- the hump's top, the bank's middle and rim, and the sandbar's crest are as deep as above, on both bakes;
- the deep and the sounder's reach behave as above;
- the grid's bounds hold the seabed.

Left for the steps that use it:
- the water's colour by depth, and breakers on the bank's rim and sandbar (step 5);
- the far water's bites by it (the ledge, the hump, the canyon, the bank; step 8);
- the island seen from far off, and the far shape of the bank's breakers (step 12);
- the charts and the minimap showing it (step 15);
- low's share (step 20): its map is a 1 MB float texture on the GPU, and the phone's textures were full already (122 to 128 MB of 128). Half floats would halve it.

## The water by its depth, built

M15's step 5 (2026-10-29). `test/sea-out-there.mjs` tests it.

- **Its colour by its depth.** The water's colour is now the light the sand under it sends back up through it, each colour fading as the water takes it: red first, then green, blue last. The rate is the water's absorption, the one the seabed's own tint already used. Under that is the water's own colour. The island's coastal water over its shelf is the green water: teal, and a little less clear, its plankton taking the blue. Past the drop-off, and out over the bank, is the open ocean's blue water, the clearest there is, sending back the most blue and the least green.
  - Over sand that's the bay's turquoise in the shallows, as before to 5 m, and a brighter blue at 10 to 30 m, where it was a dark teal: the bank, 20 m down, is a lighter blue over its sand than the deep round it. Then the shelf's teal, and the open ocean's vivid blue past the drop-off (it was a duller navy).
  - The line between them, the edge where the green water meets the blue, is over the shelf's edge: at about 110 m of depth, wandering ±30 m as the currents set it, sharp over ±12 m. The shelf's teal beside the open ocean's vivid blue is a change of hue, not just of shade, so it shows through the sky's reflection.
  - Along it, in stretches, a slick: the two waters converge there, a line of glassy water some 20 m wide, its ripples damped and the sky in it as in a mirror, with a ragged line of foam gathered down its middle, in clumps and gaps. From a height (a few hundred metres up, as a spotter plane or a tuna tower's view farther off) it shows as a broken white line along the drop-off with the deep's darker blue past it. From the deck, 2 m up, it's hard to pick out unless you're on it, as a real front can be at that angle. The weed and debris that gather on such lines, which make it plain from a boat, are step 7's.
- **A rougher sea offshore, a calm one in the lee.** The wind's sea is only as grown as the open water upwind lets it. That's JONSWAP's fetch-limited growth: in the trades (7 m/s) the 4 m chop needs 2.8 km of open water to be grown, the 23 m wave 36 km, less in a stronger wind.
  - The island blocks part of each wave's spread of directions. So in its lee, the bay, there's hardly any wind sea in the trades (the swell still comes in). Downwind it fills in from either side as it goes: half grown 1.6 km past the island, 89% at 6 km. Upwind and abeam it's the open ocean's.
  - The swell's shadowed behind the island, to 15% of it off the north shore for the south's swell, filling in further off.
  - The whitecaps go with the wind sea: none in the bay, as many as ever in the open. The boat rides the same sea as is drawn.
- **The bank's breakers.** The island's own breaking-wave model, run on the bank: its field is solved over a square of its own once you're within 9 km, for whichever sea's bigger out there, the south's swell or the trades' sea from the north.
  - A fair day's swell breaks on the sandbar's crest, a white line of surf seen from far off. A bigger one builds as it comes onto the bar and breaks across it from 2 m of water, and the rim's heads break in a big sea.
  - The bank's middle never breaks. The boat feels the breakers, and the surf zone is milky.
  - The island's own surf stays at the island.

Left for later:
- **Seagrass seen through the water.** Seagrass beds and rubble seen through deeper water (dark patches on the sand, as from the air over the Bahamas' banks) would need the seabed's map in the sea's shader. That shader's at 16 textures on high now, a phone GPU's limit (steps 4 and 5 took the last two), so it waits until something's taken out.
- **The bank's surf heard** (step 19).

## Picking up where you left off

The save keeps your money, cooler, log, upgrades, fuel and hull (`GameState.toJSON`), but not where you were: every visit starts you on the island. That's fine in the bay. It isn't for a half-hour run offshore, made again every time you come back.

- Keep where the boat is, its heading, and whether you're aboard. Come back and you're there, at anchor where you left it. (Offshore it's too deep for the anchor now, 200 ft of rode: until M16's deep-water anchor and drift sock, a boat left out past the anchor's reach drifts while you're away, its drift worked out.)
- What happened while you were gone (the weather's the same for everyone, and goes on without you):
  - a storm came through: the boat rode it out at anchor, with some damage to its hull, or it was found adrift and towed in, for a fee (Marta's tow);
  - gone long enough: the fish in the box have spoiled, and perhaps the boat's been towed in. (In M15 the catch keeps so long; M16's ice, bought by the bag, is what makes it keep longer.)
- A hurricane on its way when you come back: you're told so, and how long you have to run for port.
- Playing together: your boat shows at anchor on everyone else's sea while you're gone (chosen). Nobody can take it: a boat's pose is only ever its owner's (`TheirBoat` in `Together.js` is their boat drawn where they say it is; those riding along walk its deck, but only the owner steers). With the owner gone, though, there's no one sending its pose: where it lies has to be kept in the group's shared save (as the records are), and every visitor's sea floats it from there.
  - Others may step aboard an anchored boat with nobody home, and fish off it, and nothing more: not its hold, its stores, its helm or its anchor. (Riding along is only while the owner's aboard now.) A "keep off" for each boat can come later, if it's ever abused.
  - A hurricane: once its warning's out, Marta tows in every anchored boat whose owner's away, for a fee (as she hauls out ahead of one now), and a note says where it went and what it cost when you're back. Left out in it, a boat would be wrecked, which is too hard on someone who wasn't there.
  - Ordinary weather, thunderstorms too, it rides out at anchor, a squall taking a little off its hull.
  - Gone about a week, it's towed in, so the sea doesn't fill with the boats of those who've stopped coming. The fish in its box have spoiled by then anyway, the ice gone.
- Worth having for every boat, not only the sportfisher: M15 builds it for the lobster boat, and M16's boats each use it (off a reef in the center console, with the lobster boat's traps set).

## With M16

M15 is built first. What M16 ([M16.md](M16.md)) takes from it, and what M15 leaves for M16:

- The floating origin, the offshore seabed, the far water's look and its features: the ground every M16 boat goes out on. The sportfisher can't be made until they are.
- The offshore features (weedlines, debris, birds): M16's tuna tower and polarized glasses are for seeing them.
- The far water's kinds of water for the bites, with the mahi and tuna moved out to them: M16 adds the far fish themselves, and the tackle, leaders, lures and bait (ballyhoo, squid, chum) to catch them.
- Navigation (compass, chartplotter, weather radio) built and fitted to the lobster boat: M16 makes them each boat's gear.
- Picking up where you left off, and the anchored-boat rules, built for the lobster boat: every M16 boat uses them. M16's deep-water anchor and drift sock let a boat lie at anchor offshore; until then it drifts.
- The catch keeping so long: M16's ice makes it keep longer, its stores make days out possible.
- Marta's tow priced by the distance: M16's flares and EPIRB get you found far out.
- The soft edge: how far out each of M16's boats can go is its own.
- Beachcombing's finds, bought here: in M16's inventory with the rest.
- Things M15 doesn't need from M16: the north side (its places, landmarks, beachcombing, the chart, the slope limit and a trail) stands on its own.
