# M13: the weather and the sea, for consideration

M8 brought the weather's core, M9 its rain and thunderstorms, M10 what it costs, M11 the rain where you stand, and M12 storms from other directions ([ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md#the-weather)): each blow its own track (most down the trades, some from the south or the west, now and then a front swinging the wind round behind a line of showers), the showers, the front's bank and the lightning coming from up it, a westerly's swell crossing the island's own in the bay, rip currents and being tumbled in the surf, splashes on the rocks and the boat, and the skipper's dash for riders. Everything else talked about is here, to be weighed and chosen from. None of it is promised.

## The hurricane

- A storm surge (`G.seaLevel` raised: the shore waves already follow the depth, and the shaders read it live). The shore field's depths are baked at the usual level, but since M12 another field is solved in a worker from the island's own field's depths (`App.solveWestField`, `ShoreField.fieldGround`), and the solve takes a sea level, so a surge's fields could be solved the same way once one's forecast.
- The sea over the beach.
- Surf past M8's cap, with damage to the pier and the dock.
- Rain driven sideways, and less than 100 m seen.
- Things flying.
- The palms bent as far as they go.
- Buildings damaged.
- An evacuation, as an event: a rare one, the same for everyone (the weather comes from the clock), and never on a new player's first days.

## Storms from other directions

- The clouds aloft turning with the weather too: they keep to the trades' direction (their evolution runs along it, and the cirrus's angle is compiled in, `Clouds.js`). Behind a front the upper wind is a westerly's.
- A trades gale's own sea on the north shore: the bay's surf in a blow down the trades is the island's swell from the south grown bigger, as it always was, while the gale's sea would break on the north shore, in the lee of the mountain from the village.
- A third swell field, from the north (behind a front) or the south-west, if two crossing trains ever look wrong where a swell's between them (`SWELL_WEST` and the island's own are the two now).
- Each swell's train has its own period, but the open sea's swell is one set of waves: a westerly's turns its shorter swell waves to the west (`Waves.setSwellWest`), with the same period as the rest.

## Rain

- The rain on a tin roof heard from beneath it: under the boathouse, Joe's roof and the stilt huts the rain's muffled now, with no drumming over you (none of the recordings auditioned for M11 was kept).
- Dry under a rock's overhang: the cover map reaches only the village and the pier, and no rock standing out of the sea is in it (the splashes on the rocks about you are their own, `Rain.setRocks`).

## Thunder and lightning

- Lightning striking the sea near a boat (and the boat's crew feeling it). The strikes can't aim at a boat, and near ones are rare: it would need the host to place them.
- Several flashes at once: the sky draws the brightest one's glow, with all their light; on the horizon in a line of storms (one a second, or so) two may overlap.

## Storm skies

- Cumulonimbus: towers of their own in a thunderstorm (rain's deck is nimbostratus now: lower, smoother, darker). Raymarching in `SkyProClouds` at a cost a GPU would have to show. Since M12 a thunderstorm on the horizon shows a tower only while its lightning lights it (Sky's flash: a lumpy glow up to its top); by day, or between flashes, you see only its rain's curtain, under whatever cloud's there.

## The sea and the surf

- The open sea's crests breaking, and spray torn off them.
- Spray from whitecaps and breakers (Tidewater's emitters on the GPU weren't ported).
- The sea's culling box following the sea state (`water.lod.maxY`), if a bigger sea ever needs it.

## Consequences (mild: a cost, never a game over)

- Being knocked over wading in a big surf (a swimmer's tumbled since M12; wading, the waves don't push you).
- Gear lost: a snapped line's float and hook (tackle isn't an item, and charging for every snap would change a fair day).
- The village: shutters that swing and bang where you see them (they're merged into the houses: only their sound bangs now), and loose things blown about (the beach's debris is merged and still).

## The boat's instruments

- The radar: her open array turns, but no screen shows it (the centre screen is the sounder). The island's coast and the other players' boats on a third screen, or a split one.

## The checks

- Their frame time isn't judged: on their machine `pacedFrame` holds the frames to the display's, and the SDK's `perf` messages don't reach the report (All the Spaces' side).
- A westerly's swell isn't in them: they start on a fair day, so its field is never solved there (a few tenths of a second in a worker, and a 4 MB float texture once it is).
