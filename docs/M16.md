# M16: the boats, for consideration

The boats to work up to, once M15's bigger sea is there for them to go out on. None of it is promised.

## Boats to work up to

You start in a small boat suited to the island's shallows, and buy your way up to boats that reach new water, new fish and new ways to fish. Each one is walked about, every deck of it.

1. **A fishing kayak** (about 12 ft, paddled): the start. No fuel, no cost to run. The flats and the sheltered shallows by the village, sight-fishing for redfish, snook and trout. A small cooler. Low to the water, so a squall's a real danger.
2. **A center console** (about 23 ft, outboards, a T-top): the first engine, and fuel to think about. Fast, and the first boat to reach the reef, the wrecks and the near offshore water. The deck goes all the way round the helm, so you fish from the bow or the stern. A ladder at the stern and room for tanks: diving and spearfishing on the reef start here (the speargun has a boat for it). No cabin: a squall soaks you, and a blow sends you running in.
3. **The lobster boat** (36 ft, diesel, a wheelhouse): the boat we have now, its stern open to stand and fish from. The first real shelter from the weather, a big hold, nearshore and the reef's edge. Lobstering comes with it (its hauler and davit are aboard already; the traps taken off the deck in M14 are in git at 6bfc0d9 or before, `DeckGear.buildTraps`, `BoatMaterials.createTrap`, the trap colliders in `BoatModel`).
4. **An offshore sportfisher** (about 48 ft, twin diesels): past the reef for the bluewater fish (mahi, tuna, sailfish, marlin). Up in its tuna tower you look for birds working bait, weedlines, debris and colour in the water, and fish on top, through polarized glasses into the water. The captain steers from the flybridge below it and watches the baits. When a rod goes off you go down to the cockpit, aft, to fight it from the chair. Big and fast enough to run ahead of a storm. (After a hurricane, the mahi gather under its debris offshore.)

The sportfisher has to be worth saving up for. What it gives you:

- Days out: bunks, a galley, a fish box with ice, and fuel for the far grounds. Real ones make overnight runs to the deep water as a matter of course. Sleep aboard to pass the night; the catch keeps as long as the ice does; fuel and stores to watch; a big hold to fill before the run home.
- Fish of its own, because mahi and tuna are in the fish table already and it needs more than those to be worth it:
  - wahoo, fast, caught trolling;
  - sailfish, white marlin and blue marlin, mostly let go, the island's trophies;
  - swordfish at night, or deep-dropped by day: the reason to stay out overnight;
  - bigger yellowfin and blackfin tuna, and the mahi moved mostly offshore.
- Ways to fish of its own:
  - trolling a spread of baits off the outriggers;
  - kite fishing for sailfish;
  - deep-dropping on an electric reel.
- Every part of it to go about: climbing the tower's ladder (a climb of its own: the pier's walkable stair boxes won't do for something that high and steep, nor for falling off it in a seaway), the cabin and galley inside, the flybridge's helm, and the fighting chair.

A trawler was weighed for the last one and set aside: net fishing would be a whole new way to fish for one boat, where the sportfisher builds on the rod and reel. A poled flats skiff was weighed for the second and set aside: it fished the kayak's water the kayak's way, only faster.

What it would take:

- The three new boats modelled from photographs of their kinds (side, stern, deck and tower views gathered first), and each its own handling: a paddled kayak is a different control altogether.
- The save: the boats you own and the one you're in. Those playing now keep the lobster boat: none of them goes back to a kayak.
- Playing together: the others' boats, each the boat it is, on your sea.
- Somewhere in the village to buy them (Marta's?), and their prices against what the fish fetch.
- What each one can stand: the weather and how far out, which is most of the design.
- Picking up where you left off: M15 (needed once the sea is bigger, whatever the boat).
- The lobster boat's stern: in `BoatModel` its deck already runs to the transom, so whatever keeps you off it is to be found (a leftover trap collider, the bait barrel, or where you're let walk).

## Per-boat upgrades

The upgrades now (`Gear.js`) are of two kinds:

- Your own gear, which goes with you onto any boat: the rod, reel, line, speargun, cast net and tickle stick.
- The boat's gear, which belongs to its hull: the hold, tank, engine, fish finder, floodlights, anchor and bilge pump, and the livewell most likely.

Each boat gets its own boat upgrades, sized to it:

- the kayak: a crate, a better paddle, an anchor trolley, a fish finder puck;
- the center console: bigger outboards, the T-top, a livewell, the dive ladder;
- the lobster boat: what it has now;
- the sportfisher: the outriggers, the tower, an electric reel, a bigger fish box, an icemaker, a generator.

A boat's upgrades stay with it when you buy the next one, and trading it in gets some of their worth back.

## The rest of it

- Money: the upgrades cost 45 to 700 now, the boats need thousands. The new offshore fish's prices and what the big boats cost to run (the sportfisher burns far more fuel, plus its upkeep, and perhaps a slip at the dock) tuned together, so each boat takes a good while to earn but isn't a grind.
- The sportfisher's crew: riding along exists already. A big boat gives a group real jobs at once: one at the helm, one up the tower spotting, one in the chair. Perhaps the best reason to own one.
- Starting in a kayak: those playing now keep the lobster boat and its upgrades. New players start in the kayak: the opening and the Guide's tips rewritten for it, and Marta's haul-out before a hurricane working for every boat.
- Your fleet: where the boats you own are kept, choosing which one you take out, and selling one.
- Phones: the sportfisher, with its cabin inside and its tower, will be the heaviest model in the game, and needs a lighter version for them.
- The billfish: let go, as the rules say. A bonus for releasing one, with its picture and the log as the trophy.
