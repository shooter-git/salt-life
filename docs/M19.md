# M19: the fly, the big fish, and the shops

A fly rod for the flats; the biggest blue marlin landed alone, on 130-class tackle with a hired mate backing her down; bait and ice at Joe's and bait and the day's challenges at Pearl's; sixteen new things to buy, for you and for each boat; the polarized glasses checked against everything they're meant to show; and most of what M17 left ([M18.md](M18.md)). Proposed on 2026-11-13 on v2.4.1 (`58d20c7`), to be built on `m19`. This page has the decisions, what M19 takes from M18's list and what it leaves there, how it would be built, and what each part must pass before release.

M18's number is skipped for now: its list isn't. M19 takes every group of fixes on it, the line-class records, and a decision on each figure nobody could source. What M19 leaves on it is listed at the end ([Left on M18's list](#left-on-m18s-list)).

## The decisions (the user's, 2026-11-13)

- **The kayak anchored on the sand** is fixed: no anchor goes down on dry sand or in under 10 cm of water at her bow.
- **The biggest blue marlin, 400 to 450 kg, can be landed alone**, by both:
  - **130-class tackle:** a 130 lb line (the line's fifth level) and a 130-class two-speed reel with its bent-butt chair rod (the reel's fifth);
  - **a hired mate** on the sportfisher, paid a day rate for each day out, who takes her helm and backs her down on the fish while you're in the chair.

  The fight sweep sets the figures: the 80-class alone stays as M17 has it (landed to about 350 kg), and 130-class tackle with the mate lands a 450 kg fish at least four times in five.
- **A fly rod for the flats, with full fly casting:** false casts lengthen the line, and each can spook a fish; letting go shoots it; the fly is stripped back and the fish strip-set. 8, 10 and 12-weight rods for bonefish, permit and tarpon, with flies sold. Each fly is cast on the rod it's tied for (shrimp on the 8, crab on the 10, tarpon on the 12), or the heaviest you have. This reverses M17's "No fly rod".
- **Joe sells bait and ice.** From Marta's: ice, the frozen bait (ballyhoo, squid, chum), trap bait, and the day's order for live bait fish, which Joe takes only while he's open. After hours his night box has an ice chest on trust, as Marta's diesel has its honesty box. Stores (food and water) stay with Marta. The signs follow: Joe's gets a "BAIT · ICE" line under "JOE'S FRESH FISH", and Marta's "BAIT · TACKLE · FUEL" becomes "TACKLE · FUEL · BOATS".
- **Pearl's camp** still buys fish at 80% and sells diesel, and adds bait, and the day's challenges: one each for the rod, the speargun and the cast net, the same for everyone on the island's clock (seeded by the day, as the orders are), paid in cash at her camp.
- **New things to buy**, all sixteen the user's picks:

  | For | What |

- **The polarized glasses** are checked against everything they're meant to show, on every tier, and what's found is fixed ([The glasses](#the-glasses)).
- **From M18's list, every group of fixes:** playing together, the fight and tackle, the checks never run and the looks, and room and housekeeping ([From M18's list](#from-m18s-list)).
- **Line-class records:** the heaviest fish of each kind on each IGFA line class, in your log and the Log tab, announced on the catch card, and a line on the island's records board.
- **The figures nobody could source**, each decided:
  - **The radio:** the island's ridge blocks it. From behind the island you raise Pearl and her tow, not Marta.
  - **The sportfisher's whips:** two 8 ft VHF whips drawn on her hardtop, where her reach is worked out from.
  - **A flare** is seen from the village and from Pearl's camp, whichever is nearer, not from the island's middle.
  - **The traps:** `most` goes to 3.85 lobsters a haul, and the traps follow **the Bahamas' crawfish rules**, sourced in step 0. Anything in those rules that clashes with the traps as built, or needs seasons, comes back to the user.
  - **Kept:** a day's stores at $16 (the water bottled); parched 12 hours after the stores run out, and half strength 36 hours on.
  - **Kept, and marked as judgement where they're written:** one melt rate for the whole day; Marta's and Pearl's antenna heights; the traps' bycatch curve; the soak's 40% floor and 14-day fade; the trap bait's 0.35 and 5 nights.

## What step 0 found, and the user's calls on it (2026-11-13)

The sources ([m19/before.md](m19/before.md) and its three pages) set most figures without a question; those are in each page's "Numbers for the game". The Bahamas' own rules clashed with the game in places, and the user decided each:

- **She's a Bahamian, or a permanent resident** (the regulations treat the two alike, reg. 48(4)), and the rules are hers:
  - **Crawfish:** the Bahamas' 3¼ in (82.6 mm) carapace, not Florida's 3 in; no bag of 6, but no more than 250 lb (113 kg) aboard (the Ministry of Tourism's figure for a resident); the traps need **a crawfish trap permit, $50 a year, at Marta's**. The traps stay wire (the legal Bahamian trap is wooden slats, 91 × 61 × 61 cm: kept as built). The closed season, 1 April to 31 July, waits for seasons ([Left on M18's list](#left-on-m18s-list)).
  - **The flats need a licence**, free to her (a visitor pays $15 a day to $600 a year), picked up once at Marta's. Nothing on the flats takes your bait or fly until you have it.
  - **No pelagic bag:** the 18 pelagics a vessel is a visitor's limit, so the Florida-style bags on mahi (10), wahoo (2), yellowfin (3) and swordfish (1) go.
  - **Snook are let go**, as the 2017 flats rules have them, with bonefish, permit and tarpon (already so).
  - She may sell her catch, as she does.
- **Spearguns stay.** Bahamian law bans them (a Hawaiian sling or a pole spear only, nothing taken on scuba, none within 200 yd of a Family Island's shore: [m19/sources-big-game.md](m19/sources-big-game.md)). The game keeps its guns, the scuba tanks and the blue-water gun, and says so where the spearfishing's written up: a choice, not an oversight.
- **She's stood in, once she's the Pilot.** Owners and reviewers stand in the Pescador Pilot 12 with care (the maker's page wouldn't open). With the pedal drive she's stood in to pole and to cast, slower and less steady than sitting; sat in her, the pole only stakes her out.

Figures that moved from the decisions above, by the sources:
- **The hauler:** about 1.9 m/s, not 2.2 (a Hydro-Slave 12 in head on an 11 gpm pump, 380 ft a minute, derived; [m19/sources-boats-bait.md](m19/sources-boats-bait.md)).
- **The mate:** $200 a day out (a private boat's mate on a fishing day); he backs her down at up to 3 m/s on a calm sea, less as it gets up (boat tests back down at 3.3 to 4.6 m/s; the taper by sea is judgement).
- **The reels hold what their makers' charts say** (metres × diameter² is constant to within 1.4% on the big reels): the 80-class's 868 m of 80 lb stands; the 50-class two-speed holds about 500 m of 80 lb, not 600; the conventional about 900 m of 30 lb braid. The 130-class holds 914 m of 130 lb. (Stream BIG counts braid as mono of its strength, judgement: at its own thinness a 50-class reel holds two kilometres of 60 lb and the biggest marlin came in on it. So the conventional holds 302 m of 30 lb, [m19/big.md](m19/big.md).)
- **The line classes:** 15 lb mono over-tests the 6 kg class, so it's in the 8 kg (16 lb); "60 lb" braid is in the 37 kg (80 lb) class with the 80 lb mono.
- **A fly rod's fight is its tippet's:** 12, 16 and 20 lb tippets (5.4, 7.3, 9.1 kg) on the 8, 10 and 12 wt; the drag as anglers set it is at most half the tippet.
- **The gyro** on a 48 ft boat is a Seakeeper 6: 82 to 95% less roll at rest, nothing measured under way; it runs on the generator.
